Candidate principles:
1. Light is wired to what it reports. A lit object (tube, meter, board) is joined by one short drawn line to the row it describes, lit only from now to next, and nothing glows that is not on that path.
2. A frame over a grammar. Every page opens on one head row (title, figure, one primary), every list in time hangs from one rail with one lit NOW, and lists without time sit unboxed on the ground under a rule head.
3. Colour is replaced, never removed. Grounded companions keep their share (green at or under about 60% of a page's saturated pixels), and a change that drops a hue names the one that takes its place.

Candidate principles:
1. One pilot light at rest. Every page keeps one steady, dim phosphor lamp (the status foot's Ready) while nothing is happening. It breathes only while Vera answers, and it never goes dark or steel when all is well.
2. Lines end where content ends. A rail, post, leader or buffer is as long as what it carries and no longer: a lead runs under about 90px to the row it reports, and a rule that would stretch a drawing (every rail to a buffer, a plate to a height) gives way to content.
3. Counts are ink, live is light. A figure that counts is printed in its section's ink; the one figure that is live, on glass or in a tube, is lit phosphor. Amber is the ink of late alone, never a glow, a category or a ring.
