# Dossier: Flight Console

The idea: a 1969 flight console: a readout strip across the top (TODAY, PLANS, TO DO, LATE, IDEAS), the coming weeks drawn as a flight plan of hops between stops, an annunciator of lit lamps on Status, a crew board of lamps on To do, a T-minus countdown for setup, a flight-log chat.

Every judge's words on every design of this lineage, in every round it was judged, oldest first (main tournament rounds, then the tour's). Scores compare best within their round; 'today's page' is the yardstick.

## What lessons.md recorded about this lineage

- FAILED REACHES. (1) Flight Console (6.67, 7 promise) had a period instrument language: a zero-padded readout strip, flight-plan arcs, an annunciator, a crew board, T-minus setup, a flight-log chat and a warm-up with green card afterglow. It failed on tracked mono caps for the nav and 'ALL →' links, 23 small targets, amber with two jobs, an annunciator that repeats the Status lists, FIG captions and a one-sided Chat. To try again, make the readout strip a global row of 44px links (LATE to To do filtered, PLANS to Plans), with sentence-case labels, Plex Mono figures and today in steel. (2) Videotex Sommaire (5.98, 5 promise) typed the question on the glass under a blinking cursor, had a warm-up from a line, lit an LED on the current key and opened on a numbered Contents with leaders to counts. The panel called these the round's most faithful return of the CRT soul and its best 'what is on this page' device. It failed on a brown plastic case, VT323 on band heads, five pastel filled tiles, nav moved to a foot key row, setup placed last, 35 small targets and a 3574px Home. To try again, use a graphite case (#2c3135) and keep VT323 on the glass and page tiles only. Heads go in condensed Instrument Sans 600 at about 1.5rem, the tiles become one neutral tile with a 3px section cap, and the nav stays in the top bar. (3) Night Gallery (5.67) hung a picture light over each monitor, the most elegant scarcity idea, and used bone ink with brass wall labels. It lost on a one-size type system, sideways titles, brass tracked caps and an olive tray. Add a 34-40px question and title step. (4) Signal Legend, Drawn (6.68, 2 promise) drew engraved line pictures. Make them a system: one per page head and for empty states, about 48px with 1.75px lines, alongside the icons, not in place of them.

- CANDIDATE PRINCIPLES. (a) Every list names its facts, by a head column that states how the band stands or by column heads, and late is amber words on Home and To do, never colour alone. (b) Phosphor light is information: a lit tube draws or holds live data sized to its content, and no glass is lit that is empty. (c) The bar names every place in words, and controls and see-more links are sentence case; tracked caps are kept for eyebrows, legends and speakers.

Carried into round 12 (chosen by advance.py for score and variety): r11-idea-1 Evening Listing, Relit 8.04 (the best score of the round); r11-idea-2 Long Persistence 7.88 (new family, not a near-duplicate); r11-mut-4 Track Diagram Relit 7.52 (new family, not a near-duplicate); r10-mut-5 Signal Legend Relit 6.91 (new family, not a near-duplicate). Carried in the wild lane: r11-wild-3 Flight Console 6.67; r11-wild-4 Videotex Sommaire 5.98.

- TRAITS. seeds.json was empty again, so for the second round running there is no evidence for the random slot. The informed entrants took 1st and 2nd. The graphics mutant, Long Persistence, Metered (7.49), tied its parent and won 2 promise votes. Its phosphor moving-coil needle on the Status spend tube, with a hot core, a halo and hatched overrun, was called 'the most period-true lit graphic of the round'. What held it back: the needle rests at zero in stills, and the printed number-wheel odometer ('001.26') and tiny scale numerals ('0.50', '25 50 75') are ornament, not reading. Outside Status the change was only a thick-then-thin engraved rule, so four judges called it a near-duplicate. Keep the lit dial for live data beside printed registers for past data, but flatten the wheels to tabular figures, show a ghost peak or the limit so $0.00 still reads, and put the figure in words beside it. The type mutant, Signal Legend, Widths (6.62), was barely above its parent (6.51). Archivo's width axis (about 70% condensed titles, normal text, 115% wide caps labels) was judged elegant and worth grafting. But Archivo 400 body is paler than Plex, the wide caps date rivals Vera's name, and the slashed zero ('zero' feature) in blooming VT323 blurs to an '8' at Next up's size. Slashed zeros go on Plex Mono only, never on the glass. The crossover Slate Flight Console (6.96) beat both parents' lines for Flight Console (6.02). Evening Listing's slate, sentence-case nav, 0 small targets and amber with one job fixed its usability, and the lit annunciator tiles on Status were soul's 'best lamp panel'. Its costume (CONSOLE 03, T-minus, HOLDS, FIG captions) and a 2100px Home kept it out of every top four. The wild entrants all changed the whole page, and all but Switchboard failed the 44px rule badly (Single Quote 53, Ceefax 46, Zenith 40). That, not their ideas, held them down.

- FACES AND PICTURES. Rewarded: condensed Instrument Sans or Plex Sans Condensed heads over Plex body; a figure over a small-caps label in head columns; dates set on the digit in fixed sub-columns; dotted leaders only where a fact follows; ringed band numbers; zero-padded mono readouts in a strip that doubles as links (Slate Flight Console: 'LATE 02' in amber was usability's fastest glance). Also rewarded: ASCII bars drawn on the glass. Not rewarded: VT323 at double height or 25px off the glass (Ceefax, 5.88, the loudest page; its tall-tick apostrophe shows at headline size, as it does in Desk Terminal's glass question); ransom-letter titles and one type size for everything (Glow Ink Zine, 4.52, last, with five faces); the atlas serif with italic decks, where every underlined title reads as a link; denim ink on month names that are not links (Desk Terminal); and page titles at about 2rem next to their icon. Pictures: the mosaic drawings and pixel pictograms were liked as a system but read flat and costume-like; the Zine's green tube glowing up through a torn hole was its one idea worth keeping.
