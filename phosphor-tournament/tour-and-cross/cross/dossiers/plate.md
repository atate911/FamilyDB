### r13-idea-2 Backlit Plate: main round 13, #3 of 17, mean 7.8 (today's page 4.72; in 5 judges' top fours, 5 promise votes)
What it set out to be: One live phosphor screen lies behind the whole page and a matte slate plate is laid over it: light shows only where the plate is cut, and the plate is cut only where the machine is live. The page is a backlit faceplate: one continuous, square, matte slate plate is laid over a live phosphor screen and centred on the black room. Every light the original tube gave off is still there at full strength, but each has a physical cause. "mind" is stencilled through the plate, Send is a backlit key whose light pools out of its gap, a field's cut edge lights while you type, every lamp is a pinhole, and the screens are windows whose light falls on their bevels and spills onto the plate. The shape of a cut says what it shows: a slot is time, a round window is place, a square window is Vera, and a 4:3 window is the screen proper. Where nothing is live the plate stays whole, so the page feels calm and material, and the green appears only where attention belongs.
Its designer's own weaknesses: Ideas' words differ from today by 20.2%. That comes from the 'All ideas' rule head, the radar readout's own words, and dropping each card's where, on, duration, cost, rating and tags from the list; the idea's page still has them. | The 4:3 windows (Next up, spend, sign-in, 404) keep some spare glass around centred content. The brief's '4:3 is the screen' and 'sized to its content' pull against each other, and I chose 4:3. | The month page's slot can only show the weeks that page draws, so there NOW falls where today is rather than at the left edge. The '2 late' link goes to an anchor on the list, because the To do view has no late filter. Both would want a small view change to ship. Like Desk Terminal, the templates read the app's clock through config.FAMILYDB_APP. | Not built: the round window with its own blip on an idea's page, because idea.html gets no radar data. Settings, Family, Memory and Wishes are restyled by the stylesheet only; for example, the settings index keeps its rounded icon tiles. | The sweep is a 3px trace along the axis, not the 1px beam the brief described. | The page is calm to the point of plainness in places. Long stretches of uniform slate with thin rules can read as an admin page, and the steel and denim companions are quiet: green share is 82% on Home. Row-title links are underlined in every row, which may read as busy. | The warm-up frames show the picture partly opened at 60ms rather than a clean line, because of capture timing. Home is 1701px, at the target rather than under it. base.html still preloads DM Sans, which the page no longer uses.
- [feedback 7] A poetic concept: light only through cuts in a matte plate. The lead-rule section heads and the amber-marked late slot are good ideas. On screen, though, it is calm to the point of plain: underlined titles in every row, an 82% green share on Home and a bare Status, with the backlit idea hard to read outside Send and the monitor.
- [soul 8.4] The best explanation yet of why the glow is scarce: light shows only where the plate is cut. Next up's window blooms and spills onto its bevel, the bar has an underglow, lamps are pinholes, and Vera's newest line glows at the edge. The slot traces (Coming up, Plans, To do with amber late ticks) are light drawing data. It loses points because 'mind', stencilled as it is, barely blooms at 100%, and the greeting lost its lit pool from below.
- [style 6.4] A beautiful idea in words: a matte plate cut only where the machine is live. In the shots it reads as a plain dark page with narrow heads and leading rules. The traces on Coming up, Plans and To do are the only new character, and the steel and denim companions are timid.
- [system 8.4] The most elegant new system. It uses one square slate plate, a small vocabulary of cuts (a slot for time, a round window for place, a 4:3 window for the screen), rule-led condensed heads with the count on the same line, and one-line rows with tabular facts. Home has a real 40px question with the glowing 'mind', and the lit slots on Home, Plans and To do are data. Weak points: Settings and Family are only restyled, and still carry rounded tiles. Every row title is underlined. The 4:3 windows have spare glass. The open select list draws as an empty black band.
- [interaction 8.5] The clearest single-column sight lines on the panel: title, then the instrument, then one bank of latched keys, then the list. The same controls appear on every page: the kind keys, a one-line 'For anyone · Any but dropped' summary, a '2 late' link on the To do slot, WHO/DUE heads and a NOW divider. The squint holds and there are 0 small targets. Two costs: To do's Add sits at the foot, and every row title is underlined.
- [type 7.7] Careful typesetting throughout. Home's question is a condensed 40px headline with a true curly apostrophe. Rule heads carry a figure and a link at the right ('5 plans · All plans'). Coming up sets each day as a stacked 'THU / 1' with times in tabular mono and 'in 2 days' right-aligned. Ideas has DRIVE/WAY/FOR/# columns, and Chat's intro breaks deliberately at the colon. Against it: every row title is underlined, Status's leaders run 600px out to the far edge, and the Settings intro is indented off the field edge.
- [usability 8.7] The easiest page to read tired: 16px body, WHO/DUE heads, amber 'was due' words, a '2 late' link, a NOW divider and 'Edit or snooze' in words. Ideas has one search, one row of one-tap kind keys (Restaurant is a single tap) and a For/Status fold that shows its state. Chat's thread is on the first screen on the phone too, and no target is under 44px. Costs: every row title is underlined, which is noisy to scan; 'Add something to do' sits under the To do list, where a long list would bury it; and Home says '5 plans' over 4 rows.
- [skeptic 7] A genuinely new, coherent reason for light: cuts in a plate, with a slot for time and a round window for place. The round radar window among the Ideas filters works. But the plate turns the page into one long slate sheet, Status is a plain single column, and every row title is underlined, which is busy.
