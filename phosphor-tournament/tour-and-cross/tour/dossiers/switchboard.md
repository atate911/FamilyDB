# Dossier: Videotex Switchboard

The idea: Home as the map of the whole service: every page a cell with its number, a lamp and a count (a videotex sommaire), the question typed on the glass under a cursor, each page opening on the same rack with its own green summary screen.

Every judge's words on every design of this lineage, in every round it was judged, oldest first (main tournament rounds, then the tour's). Scores compare best within their round; 'today's page' is the yardstick.

### r11-wild-4 Videotex Sommaire: main round 11, #14 of 17, mean 5.98 (today's page 4.71; in 1 judges' top fours, 5 promise votes)
What it set out to be: The family's own videotex service: one thing at a time under a mosaic band, numbered contents, a row of keys along the foot of every screen, and the phosphor lit on its terminal. FamilyDB is set out as a 1980s videotex service like the Minitel. Every screen opens with a band holding the page's mosaic picture, its name in double-height VT323 and its page-number tile, then shows one large thing, with a row of brown-plastic keys along the foot. Home is the terminal itself: a green tube fills the first screen above the keys, with Next up and a large radar at the top and the question and a blinking cursor at the foot of the glass, and a numbered Contents list sits beside it. The feel is a warm, calm public-service terminal where only the machine gives off green light.
Its designer's own weaknesses: Home is still long (3574px against today's 2111). That is partly the idea (the terminal takes the whole first screen, and each part is spaced as its own page), but the Coming up and To do rows remain generous. | When the demo has no last line from her, Home's stretched tube leaves about 100px of empty glass between the radar and the question on a 900px screen. It reads as a screen, but it is space the design pays for. | The sticky key row is drawn where the first viewport ends in the full-page shots of long pages. I placed Home so it lands under the terminal, but on the Family, Plans list and Personality full-page captures it crosses the content (real scrolling is fine). | On the phone, the chat's docked console (tube, select with mic and Send, then Send where I am) takes about 30% of the screen above the keys. | VT323 has no distinct curly apostrophe, so 'The kids’ wishes' still looks almost straight in the tube face. | One idea's own page still uses its split panel head rather than the band, so it is the one page without the shared head anatomy. | Words: Home differs by 8.8% from today because of the Contents list, which is intrinsic to the sommaire idea.
- [feedback 5.6] The Home terminal, a big glowing tube holding Next up, the radar and the question with a cursor, has more CRT soul than anything this round. But the brown plastic cases and key row, the khaki title bands and the pastel pink and lilac tiles are the warm browns and pinks the owner has rejected.
- [soul 7.4] The most CRT-hearted first screen: you type the question on the glass under a blinking cursor, beside a large radar. The tube warms up from a line, and the key row shows an LED on the current key. Mosaic bands are true videotex graphics. It is let down by double-height VT323 on every heading off the glass, noisy dither blocks behind titles, a brown case and a 3574px Home, so it risks costume.
- [style 6.9] The boldest return of the original tube. Home is the terminal itself, with the question typed on the green glass and a radar blooming above it, framed by warm brown plastic and a Minitel contents page. It is memorable and nostalgic. The pastel filled number tiles read as confetti, the VT323 double-height bands are costume on every page, and Home is 3574px long.
- [system 5.5] It has the strongest identity and the best phosphor moment of the round: the question is typed on the lit tube itself, with a Contents list with counts and leaders beside it. The system underneath is heavy. VT323 double-height section titles are a fuzzy fourth voice, the brown plastic reads khaki, five pastel number tiles make confetti, rows are oversized (Home 3574px) and the sticky key row crosses the content.
- [interaction 5.3] The numbered Contents on Home, with counts and anchor links, tells you what the page holds in one look. - [type 5.8] Some lovely typographic devices: a numbered Contents list with dotted leaders to its figures, page-number tiles, and double-height VT323 titles that bring the tube's voice to each band. But VT323 off the tube at 28-40px reads coarse, item names in 700 weight with underlines shout, the nav is mono caps, and Home runs to 3574px.
- [usability 5.2] The Contents list with counts and the large row type help. But on desktop the main nav moves to a key row under the terminal, so Home, Vera, Ideas, Plans, To do and Wishes are not in the top bar and come late in the Tab order. Finish setting up drops to the last section of a 3574px Home, and Home has 35 small targets. ### r12-wmut-1 Videotex Switchboard: main round 12, #8 of 17, mean 7.01 (today's page 4.84; in 2 judges' top fours, 7 promise votes)
