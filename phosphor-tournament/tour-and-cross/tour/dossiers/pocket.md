# Dossier: Pocket Timeline

The idea: Home ordered as time on one spine: NOW (the question), then NEXT (the tube), then Coming up, then LATER, with the lists beside it; every page strung along a timeline.

Every judge's words on every design of this lineage, in every round it was judged, oldest first (main tournament rounds, then the tour's). Scores compare best within their round; 'today's page' is the yardstick.

### r11-wild-1 Pocket Timeline: main round 11, #17 of 17, mean 5.11 (today's page 4.71; in 0 judges' top fours, 0 promise votes)
What it set out to be: A family handheld: four greens, pixel sprites and chunky boxes, every page strung along one timeline with NOW lit on it. The page is a family Game Boy. The slate plastic hardware (bar, foot, bezels, buttons) carries berry and indigo lens trims, and it frames a screen drawn in four greens. Everything hangs from one spine down the left. The title tile is the first stop and each section is a square stop. Anything with a time sits on the spine at that time: date tiles, ticks, speakers, settings pages as metro stops. NOW is a steady berry power light, NEXT is a green lamp that breathes, LATER is a hollow dashed stop, and past LATER the line runs on dotted. It should feel toy-like and friendly, and calmer now. The phosphor light (bloom, blur, scanlines, dot matrix, afterglow) is kept for the lit screens, the mark and the few lit controls.
Its designer's own weaknesses: The To do page cannot yet colour overdue rows berry. views.task_row does not carry `late`; views.task_brief works it out for Home only. The template reads row.late and the CSS is ready, but nothing on the render shows it until views.py adds the one line, and I could not edit the repository. | Silkscreen at 16px uppercase is wide and loud. Long day names ('TUESDAY 29 SEPTEMBER') and 'IN 10 DAYS' take a lot of the row, and on a phone the NOW date nearly fills the width. | Pixelify at 20px wraps cartridge titles in Home's narrow shelf ('Silver Falls / hike', 'Cannon Beach / weekend'), and wrapped pixel type reads less easily than it would on one line. | The dotted tail past LATER is drawn by a positioned LATER section with a masking background. That is a delicate trick: it depends on the Home grid stretching and on nothing else being positioned in that column. | Home's word difference is 4.8%, just over the 4% guideline. Most of it is her last line aging out of today's demo clock; the rest is the three new stop words, which I kept on purpose. | Wishes and the 404 eyebrow were the last places where berry did not mean 'you are here'. The 404 eyebrow is now pea, but the Wishes section still uses berry as its section colour by design, so berry does two jobs there. | The A-button and slate buttons now use Nunito 800 rather than pixels. It is more legible and coherent, but a little less 'console' than the first draft's Pixelify buttons.
- [feedback 5.2] The spine with NOW, NEXT and LATER stops through every page is line art the owner would enjoy, and the screens glow. But the pixel-font heads are toy-like, the chunky khaki-olive frames are the heavy plain boxes the owner dislikes, and berry pink marks 'now'.
- [soul 5] A handheld's LCD soul rather than a tube's. Four greens tint the ground and the olive card fills, so the page drifts back to green monochrome. Pixel fonts and chunky boxes are lively rather than calm, even though the phosphor display itself is kept.
- [style 6.3] The most personality in the room. It is a family Game Boy, with a NOW/NEXT/LATER spine and a berry power light that kids would love. It is heavy-handed: pixel type on every item name, beveled cartridge boxes everywhere and an olive-green ground that makes the whole page greener, which works against the owner's calm.
- [system 5] The NOW, NEXT and LATER spine is a strong structural rule that could run through every page. But the surfaces are moss and olive (green-family, so more monochrome, not less), pixel faces are set on card titles and page titles, and chunky double boxes wrap everything. It reads as a toy rather than the family's daily tool.
- [interaction 5.6] The NOW / NEXT / LATER spine is a strong way to tell time at a glance. But the pixel faces on heads and titles slow reading and wrap, there are 7 small targets on Home and 25 on To do, and the berry 'was due' reads as an alarm.
- [type 4.2] The pixel faces carry personality but cost legibility. Idea names in Pixelify at 20px wrap ('Oaks Park / roller rink'), Silkscreen's wide caps ('IN 10 DAYS') are loud, and rounded Nunito body pulls toward a toy rather than the phosphor.
- [usability 5.3] The NOW/NEXT/LATER spine orients well, and Plans' week agenda with 'Nothing on.' answers 'what is on this weekend' directly. But the pixel faces on titles and item names hurt reading ('Oaks Park' reads like 'Ooks Pork', and 29 in the date tile reads like 89), and the pixel icons are hard to recognise. The month grid shrinks to a small tube, To do has 25 small targets and no late marking.
- [skeptic 4.3] The ground itself is tinted green (17,26,17), and the pixel-font heads plus khaki-olive cartridge cards make a Game Boy costume that reads more monochrome, not less. The berry 'was due' reads as an alarm.
Fixes the judges asked for:
- feedback: Keep the spine with its NOW, NEXT and LATER stops through every page. Keep pixel type for figures at most and set heads in a sans. Replace the chunky khaki frames with thin rules, and let NOW be the lit green lamp rather than berry pink.
- soul: Keep the NOW / NEXT / LATER spine with a lit NEXT lamp, and the phosphor display in its slate bezel. Return the ground to green-black and the card fills to slate, so green stays light rather than paint. Set headings in the sans; pixel type belongs on the screen only.
- style: Keep the NOW/NEXT/LATER spine and the berry 'you are here' light, both good ideas for any design. Set item names and card titles in the sans, keeping pixel type only for page titles and stop labels. Return the ground to near-black rather than olive, and thin the bevels to one step, so the handheld frames the screen instead of covering the page.
- system: Keep the NOW, NEXT and LATER spine as a rule for anything with a time. Move every surface off moss and olive onto the slate plastic already in the palette, keep pixel faces to the page title at most (never card or row titles), and thin the chunky double frames to one.
- interaction: Keep the NOW / NEXT / LATER spine on Home. Set titles and heads in a readable sans (pixel face only for the NOW stamp and the tab icons), use amber rather than berry for 'was due', and raise the To do ticks and edit links to 44px.
- type: Keep Pixelify for page titles and the NOW / NEXT / LATER stop labels only. Set item names in the body face, reduce Silkscreen's tracking, and replace Nunito with a straighter sans so the pixel voice is an accent, not the reading face.
- usability: Keep the NOW/NEXT/LATER spine and the week agenda that says 'Nothing on.' Set item names, dates in tiles and card titles in Nunito, and keep the pixel face for page titles and the tube only. Replace the pixel icons (the family and gear) with clear line icons. Make the tube month's days links to their week. Colour overdue tasks on the To do page.
- skeptic: Keep the NOW/NEXT/LATER spine. Put it on a neutral green-black ground, not (17,26,17). Make the cartridges slate with a kind-coloured cap, not khaki. Limit the pixel face to the mark and the stop labels.
