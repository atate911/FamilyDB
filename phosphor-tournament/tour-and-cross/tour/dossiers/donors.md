Designs whose best features the grafts may take. Every judge's words on each, in every round it was judged.

## r7-idea-2: Signal Box: the mimic-line track the owner called high style (the chat hung from Vera's mark, the ring round the page icon, To do's timetable margin, the dot under the current page)

### r7-idea-2 Signal Box: main round 7, #4 of 12, mean 6.99 (today's page 3.9; in 6 judges' top fours, 0 promise votes)
What it set out to be: The control room's mimic line: every sequence in the family's week hangs from one blue-pewter track, each stop a slate plate with its lamp and its time in a timetable margin, and the phosphor is the lamp that means now. A signal box or power-station desk, where green VDUs sat beside a mimic panel. Everything FamilyDB shows in time (the next plans, what is due, the conversation, setup, activity) hangs from one 2px blue-pewter track. Each stop is a flat slate plate with a lamp on the track, and its time hangs in a right-aligned timetable margin. The page's own Status lamps become one legend for the whole app: hollow ring for to come, lit sage for done, amber for needs a look, red for off, accent for you are here, and #6dff9c only for now and live. It should feel calm, legible and mechanical, like a desk that tells you at a glance what is running and what comes next, with the phosphor as the one lamp that is on.
Its designer's own weaknesses: The colour change is modest. Only 12.5% of Status pixels, 20.3% of Home below the fold and 14.8% of Chat differ from today by more than 5 dE, well under the lead's 35% aim. The slate plates are about 4.5 dE from today's charcoal panels, and the black between them is today's black. | Slate at this lightness is within 1.5 dE of GitHub Dark's #161b22, like every slate entrant, so a judge may still read the plates as a borrowed theme. | The CSS is at 14,923 of 15,000 characters, so the brief's Wishes (rank inside the node), Memory log, the 'What has changed' log and month-view event plates with kind rings were not built. Wishes and Memory look as today, and month events keep today's cyan-edged chips. | Lines everywhere can read as a 'timeline component' or a git graph to a skeptic, especially Status's three vertical rails. | Where a line ends without a way-on station (Status's 'Who answers' and 'Connected to', the Plans list), the track runs about 15 to 20px past the last lamp instead of ending on it. | On Home's amber setup plate, the 1px siding before each step link reads a little like a dash in front of the words. | The Ideas monitor still wraps '#7 Pumpkin patch at Bi-Mart / farm'. It is the brightest thing on the page, and Ideas stays 88% green. | Home is 1940px at 1280, 90px over the brief's 1850 target, though shorter than today's 2111. | The To do page still has 22 of 97 targets under 44px, inside the Edit folds' forms. A done task's box inside its plate is today's square while its lamp lights; that state is not in the demo. | The 'today' lamp at the start of the fortnight line has no label, because no new words may be added, so its meaning rests on the lamp legend. | The phone keeps a narrower margin rather than folding the times into the plates as the brief asked, which costs width on a 390px screen (Chat's bubbles are about 260px).
- [feedback 6.9] The most inventive structure in the round: a mimic-panel track with signal lamps turns Coming up into a timetable line, To do into a due-time margin and Chat into a timestamped log, and it reads as a real CRT-era control room. But the grounded colour is thin (slate plates on mostly black; Home below the fold and Status change little in tone). The Ideas masthead track wraps oddly, with 'Add an idea' hanging off it, and tracks on every page risk reading as a git graph.
- [soul 7.3] The most original CRT-era idea here: a mimic-panel track with lamps, where #6dff9c lights only 'now' and live and the Status lamp legend becomes the whole app's grammar. That is exactly what glow scarce and meaningful should mean. But the colour change is modest, the rails everywhere can read as a git graph, the Chat log loses its two sides, and cards lose the hover afterglow.
- [style 7.7] The freshest idea of the tournament that actually works. The mimic line turns the page's own content into the drawing: Coming up becomes a fortnight track with stations and dates, To do hangs its due times in a timetable margin, Chat reads as a timetable of speakers, and Status, setup and Sign in hang from the same rail, with one lamp legend for all. The family would see a new Home, Chat and Status at a glance, and it is a system, not a trick. It loses a little on colour: the slate plates are close to stock GitHub-dark, much of the page is bare black, and the Ideas head is clumsy ('Add an idea' stranded on the track's elbow, the tabs inside the head).
- [system 7.2] The freshest structural idea. The horizontal Coming up track with its date margin is excellent, To do's timetable margin reads straight down, the lamp legend would hold on any page, and Sign in sits beside its case. But the track reaches only some sections (Lately added and the wishes are ordinary cards), the chat puts everyone on the left and so loses 'mine versus hers', rails run past their last lamp, the plates are barely off today's charcoal (grounded colour is weak below the fold), and the Next up cursor drops to its own line.
- [interaction 7.2] The best at-a-glance 'what comes next': Coming up as a horizontal fortnight line and To do hung from a track with due times in a margin both answer 'when' faster than any list. But Chat drops the left/right split, so every speaker is on one side. The Ideas head tangles the primary 'Add an idea' into the track below the save fold, with the tabs moved above it. The phone chat's time margin squeezes bubbles, cards show no visible hover, and To do has 22/97 small targets.
- [type 6.4] Coming up is a lovely setting: large day numerals with OCT and THU stacked as small mono caps beside them on the track. Chat's single left edge reads cleanly. The timetable margin, though, is too narrow for what it holds. Every stamp breaks ('WAS DUE / YESTERDAY', 'DUE IN 3 DAYS, / 19:56', '27 SEP, / 19:47'), and on the phone it cuts the bubble measure to about 22 characters. The indented To do head also breaks Home's left edge.
- [skeptic 6.2] The largest structural change on Chat (a track with times hung in the margin), and it changes Sign in too. But the tracks-and-rings idea reads as a git graph or CI pipeline on Status and To do. The colour change is modest: mostly today's black with thin slate plates. 'You are here' is a 5px dot. On Ideas, 'Add an idea' is left hanging inside the capture group under a tube crammed into the masthead.
Fixes the judges asked for:
- feedback: Carry more grounded colour: put Coming up's plates and To do's stops on a slate tray (#12181e) so Home below the fold and Status read slate rather than black. Straighten the Ideas masthead: end the page-head track on the rule, and set 'Add an idea' on the title's baseline at the right as the other entrants do. End every track on its last lamp. In Chat, give the family's bubbles a 3px #94b0e8 edge so the two speakers separate at a glance now that both sit on the left.
- soul: Keep the lamp legend, which is the best glow grammar in the field. In Chat, keep the track but tell the speakers apart by material: family plates #1f2630 with a 3px #94b0e8 left bar, and Vera's plate on green-black #111712 with a 30% brand edge. End each rail on its last lamp (`.stops>li:last-child::before{bottom:auto;height:1.1rem}`). Drop the vertical drop under page-title h1s (keep the terminus ring) so mastheads are less fussy. Put a tray fill (#12181e) behind each Status column so the slate shows across the page. Restore the card hover afterglow.
- style: 1. Give the plates grounded colour so the track sits on material, not bare black: set surface #151a1f to #141b1d and surface-2 to #19211f (toward green-black, away from GitHub's #161b22). Tint each idea plate with its own kind: .idea-plate{background:color-mix(in srgb,var(--kind) 8%,var(--surface))}. Put the thread on a faint well: .in-chat .line{background:#10161a;border-radius:12px;padding:1rem 1rem 1rem 0}.
2. Ideas head: take 'Add an idea' off the track's elbow. Use .in-ideas .page-head{grid-template-columns:1fr auto} and .in-ideas .page-head .btn{grid-column:2;align-self:start}, and move the All ideas and Restaurants tabs below the head rule.
3. End every rail on its last lamp, so no track runs 15-20px past it (the Status columns, the Plans list): .line>li:last-child::before{bottom:auto;height:1.1rem}.
4. Scope nowrap on the radar so '[18 MIN NW]' and 'Bi-Mart farm' stay together: .crt .radar a,.crt .radar .dist{white-space:nowrap}.
5. Move the accent #94b0e8 to #9ea9f2 (h275), away from Tokyo Night's #7aa2f7.
- system: Bring Lately added and the kids' wishes onto the track as stops (kind ring as the lamp), so the grammar holds everywhere. End each rail on its last lamp (`.track::before{bottom:calc(var(--last-stop-h)/2)}`, or clip the rail with `:last-child` padding). Give the plates a fill that reads grounded (`#161c21` against `#0b0e0c`, with edge `#2e343b`). In Chat, keep the family on the right, or give their plates a 3px accent edge so self and other stay clear at a glance. Fix the Next up cursor wrap (`.next-more{white-space:nowrap}`). On a phone, fold the times into the plate head below 30rem to recover about 120px of bubble width.
- interaction: (1) Restore the masthead on Ideas, with 'Add an idea' top-right on the title baseline (`.in-ideas .page-head{display:grid;grid-template-columns:1fr auto;align-items:end}`), and put the tabs back after 'Save a thought' in source and visual order. Take the track out of the Ideas head, because the primary action must not sit on a rule. (2) Chat: keep the timetable margin but put family bubbles right of the track and flush right (`.in-chat .stop.me .bubble{margin-left:auto}`) so who spoke reads at a glance. Below 40rem, fold the time into the bubble meta (`@media(max-width:40rem){.stop>time{display:none}.bubble .meta time{display:inline}}`) to win back about 60px of bubble width. (3) Give plates a hover and afterglow (`.plate:hover{background:#1a2026;border-color:#465260}` with the same transition as today's cards). (4) To do edit folds: `.task summary,.task form :is(button,select,input){min-height:2.75rem}` to take 22/97 toward 0. (5) Label the 'today' lamp with the existing date text or `aria-label`, since a lamp's meaning should not rest on a legend. (6) End each track on its last lamp: `.track::after{bottom:var(--last-lamp)}` or `clip-path`.
- type: The timetable margin needs about 7.5rem and one-line stamps: `.stop .time{white-space:nowrap;width:7.5rem}` with a shorter chat stamp (time only, the date once per day). Align To do's head with the other section heads (`.todo .section-head{margin-left:0}`). Below 40rem, fold the times into the plates to recover the bubble measure.
- skeptic: Replace the 5px 'you are here' dot with a latched key or a 2px accent bar under the tab. End every track on its last lamp (no 15-20px overrun). Put 'Add an idea' back on the masthead baseline and keep the tube beside the list, not in the head. Give the slate one fill with presence (the thread on a tray #12181e, Status columns filled) so the grounded colour is seen and not only the lines. Curve the page-head drop less, or square it, so it stops reading as a subway map.

## r9-idea-4: Signal Lines: Signal Box's rings and tracks on every page, and a page head that holds its title, tabs and actions in one bracket

### r9-idea-4 Signal Lines: main round 9, #6 of 13, mean 6.86 (today's page 4.0; in 2 judges' top fours, 2 promise votes)
What it set out to be: Signal Galley's slate trays and strips, drawn together by one line language: every page and section begins at a ring, a bracket holds a head's controls, a steel track carries anything with a time or an order, and #6dff9c lights only now and live. Signal Galley's materials are left exactly as they were: green-black slate trays and strips, Plex type, the chinned case and the lit greeting. On top of them Signal Box's line art now runs through every page as one grammar. Each page and each section starts at a ring. A bracket from the head's ring holds that head's controls, a steel track carries anything with a time or an order, and hollow stops sit on the tracks. It should feel like a control-room mimic panel drawn with a fine pen. The lines show how each page is organised, and the phosphor lights only what is happening now.
Its designer's own weaknesses: To do's two past-due tasks are still pewter rings on the To do page (amber on Home). The template now reads row.late, but views.task_row has no 'late' field and the harness forbids editing the repository. It needs `late` (open, due_at before now) added in views.task_row at implementation. | Heads with nothing to hold (Status, Settings, New idea, Memory, Wishes) end in a short drop and a buffer stop. At about 15-20px it can read as a pin more than a line. | The To do head is now tall. The note, the Add fold and the filter form sit in the bracket. With the Add fold open (the default when the list is empty) the bracket holds a whole form, and on a phone the head runs to about 500px before the list. | Home above the fold is still Signal Galley's: the greeting and the Next up monitor are unchanged by design, so the line language shows from 'Finish setting up' down. | Home's To do head is set over its track, which leaves the timetable margin to its left empty beside the title. | Sign in carries no line. Its last element changes (a hint appears when people sign in by name), so a buffer stop there would land in the wrong place. | The CSS is at 24,464 of 25,000. To fit, the build now inlines constant tokens (brand, lit, surface-2/3, bg) and folds constant calc()s, and the dotted head leaders were cut.
- [feedback 8.6] It carries Signal Box's line language through every page with restraint: a ring and bracket for each page head, the setup track, the fortnight line, To do's timetable track, the Chat thread hanging from Vera's mark, Status rails and the dot under the current page. The lines delimit areas and lead the eye, which is exactly what the owner asked to see again, and it keeps Signal Galley's grounded slate and 0/56 small targets. Faults: it still uses the thick, rounded pewter case the owner criticised, and it has a little more periwinkle than it needs.
- [soul 7.1] Signal Galley's soul is intact above the fold. The mimic-panel lines are drawn with a fine pen, but rings and tracks on every page, including Chat's message track and rings on To do, drift back toward the git-graph reading that round 7 retired.
- [style 6.5] The mimic-panel line language is new and runs through every page: rings, tracks, hollow stops, and To do's overdue rings in amber on a track. It gives Chat, To do and Status a drawn structure that none of the others have. But ringing every section icon reads as a generic 'icon in a circle', the chunky pewter case stays, and above the fold it is still Signal Galley.
- [system 6.7] One consistent line language, but it adds delimiters without adding information. Rings sit on every head and buffer stops read as pins, and the To do head's bracket holds a whole form (about 500px on a phone before the list). The Chat track is the one place the lines earn their weight.
- [interaction 7] Its bracket is the best head idea on the panel: title, tabs, 'Save a thought' and 'Add an idea' sit in one row, and To do's Add, Show and Search share one line, so every list starts sooner. But the bar marks the current page with a tiny dot, the weakest 'here' among the informed entrants. Tick wells are radio-like rings on a track, the track eats width on phone Chat, and Home is the longest of the informed (1967px).
- [type 6.6] Signal Galley's type is unchanged, and the ring-and-bracket line art adds structure but no typographic gain. The Ideas and To do mastheads now stack tabs, fold and filters inside the bracket, which crowds the title, and Home's To do head leaves its margin empty beside the title.
- [usability 6.3] The heads are efficient: Ideas' tabs, Save and Add share one head, and To do's Add and filter sit above the list. But the to-do checkboxes are rings on a track, the same rings used as decoration on the Chat track, so a newcomer cannot tell which circles are pressable. The To do page shows no overdue flag, and the month is past the fold.
- [skeptic 6.1] Round 7 already found that lines on every page read as a git graph or subway map, and here they are on every page. Home above the fold is Signal Galley's. The To do ticks are rings on a track, so they look like timeline stops, not controls. 'You are here' in the bar is a 5px dot, which lessons.md calls too small, and the ring-and-buffer-stop heads on Status and Settings read as pins.
Fixes the judges asked for:
- feedback: Keep every line. Swap the thick rounded pewter case for Flight Deck's thin, square, screwed faceplate: this is the owner's standing critique, and it is the one thing holding Signal Lines back. Nudge the periwinkle accent toward a lower-chroma cyan, about #8fbfdc, in line with the owner's CGA note and to show less blue. Home's To do sets its title over the track and leaves the timetable margin empty beside it: hang the title from the head ring in the margin column instead. To do page: take the Add fold out of the head bracket so the bracket holds only Show, Search and Filter and the head stays short on a phone. Add a `late` field in views.task_row so overdue lamps are amber on the To do page as well as on Home. Where a head has nothing to hold, drop the buffer stop, or end the line in a small tick, so it does not read as a pin.
- style: Keep the tracks, the hollow stops, and To do's overdue rings on a track. They are the best part and carry structure into Chat and Status. Drop the ring around every section icon, which reads as a generic icon badge, and let the track start at a plain tick. Fill To do's empty timetable margin beside the head. Replace the chunky pewter case with a thin, square, flush case.
- system: Keep the Chat track with stops, where the line carries order. Drop the rings on every head, the buffer stops and the bracket around To do's form. The line language should mark only time or sequence (Coming up, Chat, To do's due column).
- interaction: The bracket that gathers a head's controls beside its title is worth making standard on every page: it shortens the path from title to action to content. Replace the tiny bar dot with a 2px cap and a tinted cell. Use square tick wells, since a ring on a track reads as a radio button or a stop. Hide the chat track below 40rem so phone bubbles get their width back. Give the stub heads (Status, Settings) no drop at all rather than a buffer stop.
- usability: Keep the compact heads (tabs, Save and Add in one Ideas head; Add and filter above the To do list). Make the to-do checkbox a square well with the ring beside it on the track as the stop, so a pressable thing never shares a shape with a decorative one. Add the `late` field so overdue shows amber on the To do page too.
- skeptic: Keep one track where time runs (Home's fortnight, To do's due margin) and drop the rest. Put a square tick well beside each ring rather than making the ring the checkbox, and give the bar a latched cell for here instead of a 5px dot.

## r7-idea-1: Galley Rack: the Coming up strips the owner said look far better than any other (a date designator block, the title and time, a tabular 'in 3 days' facts column)

### r7-idea-1 Galley Rack: main round 7, #1 of 12, mean 8.23 (today's page 3.9; in 7 judges' top fours, 0 promise votes)
What it set out to be: A composing room's galley rack at night: what you read is set in slate trays under Oxford rules, every list in them a rack of flat strips with a designator, a body and a tabular facts column; what you act in is a raised box; the machine speaks on green-black glass in a pewter case, and the exact phosphor is the one light. This is the child that every round-6 lens named. It takes Slate Proof's page frame (masthead, galley trays under Oxford rails, raised act boxes, a chinned pewter case, the type steps, focus and targets) and uses Radar Room's flight strip as the one list component inside the trays. The strip is a single `strip()` macro with fixed slots: designator, body, and a tabular facts column. It feels like a quiet, orderly typesetting room at night. Grey slate trays hold the family's lists. Each row is a flat strip whose designator carries a small breath of its kind's colour. The only glowing things are the #6dff9c mark, Send, the key word "mind", focus rings, live lamps and the monitors.
Its designer's own weaknesses: Home is 1963px tall at 1280, over the brief's target of about 1850. The greeting (570px) and a one-column setup plate (258px) take a lot before the board begins. I did not shrink the setup rows, because they hold the 44px targets. | Feet meet because the trays stretch, but the stretched tray is not always full. In row 1, To do's four strips leave about 85px of empty tray under them beside Coming up (which carries a source note). The kids' wishes tray has about 40px of slack. | I did not follow the brief's 'two by two' for Lately added; it is one column. I think the page is better for it (feet meet, reads straight down), but a judge who wanted denser cards may disagree. | Home's green share of colour is 34.5%, against 56% for Slate Proof and 66% for Radar Room. The kind-tinted icons, kind labels and cyan date leaves bring more non-green colour. The green is still the only glow, but a skeptic may say Home has drifted from the phosphor. | Chat's masthead rule and the thread tray's Oxford rail sit about 36px apart, so the top of Chat shows two rules in a row. This is the system (masthead, then tray) but it stutters on a page with one tray. | Ideas' Search field is squeezed to about 156px in the one-row filter box beside the 22rem scope, so its placeholder is clipped. | The designator tints are faint by design (10%). Amber restaurant designators read close to warm grey, and the cyan and pink breaths are subtle at a glance. | The kid's own 'My wishes' Home branch (numbered strips) and the Family and Memory strips are not shown in the harness's sheets. Family and the Plans list checked out in my own screenshots; the kid's view is untested live. | The CSS is at 14,486 of the brief's 14,500, which leaves the next generation almost no room without refactoring. `justify-content` and `align-content` cannot be used at all, because the harness's content check refuses them.

Slate trays under Oxford rails, with racked strips (designator, body, tabular mono facts), carry grounded colour across Home below the fold, Status, To do, Ideas and now Chat's thread. The bright green is kept to Send, 'mind', the mark, focus, the lamps and a chinned pewter tube. The cobalt corner gives the greeting its second light back, and kind-tinted designators add quiet variety without neon. It is held back by empty tray under To do, a doubled rule at the top of Chat, a clipped Search field on Ideas, and restaurant/activity designators that tint toward brown.
- [soul 8.4] The most complete period room in the field: slate galley trays under Oxford rules, the flight strip as the one list drawing (Coming up, To do, Status lamps, the Ideas list), Vera on green-black glass and every tube in a chinned pewter case. It is also the first entrant to change Sign in and 404 (a tray with the tube beside the words, a portrait tube on 404). The phosphor is plainly the one light, and the cobalt corner and kind-tinted designators add calm colour without glow. The costs are some grey mass, a faint hover afterglow and a double rule at the top of Chat.
- [style 7.5] The most complete and considered system on the table: galley trays under Oxford rails, one strip component with designator, body and tabular facts column on Home, Ideas, To do and Status, faint kind tints in the designators, dotted leaders to each head's links, and the monitor set beside the form on Sign in. It is handsome and grounded on every page. As an identity it is the predictable breed: slate with periwinkle is round 4's palette again, the 10% tints barely read, and at a glance it can feel like a very well-made admin console more than a lovely place.

Read content is in slate trays, act content in raised boxes. One strip component with fixed slots (designator, body, tabular mono facts) runs Coming up, To do, Lately added, the kids' wishes, Status, the Ideas list and the To do page. Chat's thread sits on a tray, and Sign in finally puts the case beside the form. The Next up orphan and the select-ring bugs are fixed, and #6dff9c is kept to the lit things. Against it: Chat stutters with two rules about 36px apart, the Ideas search field is squeezed to a clipped placeholder, the radar text breaks into about 7 lines in the 22rem scope, the To do tray has about 85px of slack, and a 3.5rem designator holding one Status lamp dot is heavy.
- [interaction 8.5] The clearest page to use. Slate Proof's mastheads and targets (1/57 on Home, 0 on Status and General) come with one strip component everywhere: Coming up, To do, Lately added, the kids' wishes, Status lamps, To do rows and a list-first Ideas. So a glance reads what it is, what it says and when, straight down an aligned facts column. The chat thread now sits in a tray, and focus rings and the slate picker are complete. It fixes Slate Proof's orphaned Next-up chevron and brings the setup list back to one well-led column. Faults: the Ideas search placeholder is clipped, the scope text breaks into choppy short lines, the facts column on Lately added holds only '#1', Chat has two rules in a row, and on the phone the chat tray costs bubble measure.
- [skeptic 7.9] The only entrant whose change runs through every page as one drawing: slate trays and strips on Home below the fold, Status, To do and the Ideas list, a tray under the Chat thread, and even Sign in turned into a landscape card. Its green stays on the mark, Send, focus and the tubes, and it fixes Slate Proof's orphaned '›'. Skeptic's charges: it is a lot of grey-blue mass sitting on GitHub Dark. It is elegant and grounded. The one-size rule flattens the hero: 'What's on your mind?' is no larger than body text, the extended tracked caps heads feel mannered, and Home runs long.
- [system 5.3] Calm and large-print, and the picture light over each tube is a lovely way to say 'light where attention belongs'. But the one-size rule removes the greeting's hierarchy: the question is set at body size. Heads are tracked extended caps at about .2em, setup is a brown-filled tray, Chat is a one-sided log and the lit glass greeting is gone.
- [interaction 5] The large print is easy to read and the setup panel is clear. With one type size, hierarchy is gone when squinted, the heads are tracked caps, Chat drops the split between mine and hers, there are 20 small targets on Home, and Home is 2346px long.
- [type 5] The bold single-size rule gives large-print calm and two fine things: a playscript chat (speaker and stamp in a column, words beside) and wall-label pairs on Status. But the page title is gone from the reading line (it survives only in a rotated banner), the question is no larger than the body, section heads are extended caps at wide tracking, and the legal line is set at 22px, so there is no hierarchy to scan.
- [usability 6.4] The most legible type for a phone at night (22px, 1.4 leading), and Home, Chat and the Plans grid are effortless to read. But the page titles (Ideas, Plans, To do, Status, Vera) were taken off the page and set sideways in the margin banners, so you lose where you are. Home has 20 targets under 44px, To do does not mark late, and Ideas shows only one row above the fold.
- [skeptic 4.8] One type size for every word removes the hierarchy: the question is the size of its placeholder, and the heads match the body. The setup tray is filled olive-bronze (31,29,21), and brass heads in extended tracked caps over khaki banners read as costume. The picture light over the monitor is the one idea worth keeping.
Fixes the judges asked for:
- soul: Keep the picture light over each monitor and the monitors as the lit exhibits; it is the most elegant idea for scarcity. Put the greeting's glass pool back behind the question. Break the one-size rule at least for titles and the question (two steps). Set labels in sentence case, not brass tracked caps.
- style: Keep the picture light over the monitor, the swallowtail room banners, brass labels and bone ink: the museum mood is the best new grounded palette of the round. Break the one-size rule once, for the question: a 34-40px display step for 'What's on your mind?' and page titles. Replace the extended tracked caps heads with small caps at .06em. Tighten Home by setting Coming up as one line per plan.
- system: Keep the picture light above each tube and the comfortable large-print body. Give the ask its own title step (the question must be the largest words on Home), replace the .2em extended-caps heads with condensed 600 sentence case, draw setup as an amber edge on slate rather than a brown fill, and bring back the two-sided Chat and the lit glass greeting.
- interaction: Keep the large 18-20px body for the family, but add one heading step so hierarchy survives the squint. Set the section heads in sentence case rather than tracked caps, restore the Chat bubbles with the family on the right, and raise the 20 small Home targets to 44px.
- type: Keep the playscript chat and the wall-label pairs (bold name, regular state). Add back two steps: a title step of about 2x for the page title (horizontal, in the reading line) and for 'What's on your mind?', and a small step for the legal line and captions. Cut the extended caps tracking from about .2em to .06-.08em.
- usability: Keep the one-size large type: it is the best answer to tired eyes on a phone. Put a horizontal page title back (the banner can stay as ornament beside it), so Ideas, Plans, To do and Status say where you are. Mark late on the To do page ('was due' in copper, as on Home). Give the inline name and 'All →' links 44px hit areas. Set buttons in sentence case (Send, Filter, Add an idea). On desktop Chat, tint Vera's lines or indent the family's so speakers show without reading names.
- skeptic: Keep the picture light over the monitor and the idea of room banners. Restore at least a three-step scale (question, head, body), and make the setup tray a neutral fill with an amber edge.
