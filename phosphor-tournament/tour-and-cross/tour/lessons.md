Lessons:
- REFINEMENTS. The judges noticed the fixes that change how a page is used. Every refinement and graft took Home's small targets to 0; the carried designs had up to 33. They also put controls in sentence case, which interaction put at about two points on its own. One-line tables, amber 'was due' words, a NOW rule and words in the bar were credited too. In heat a that was not enough. All three grafts beat their refinements, because a refinement that only cleans "looks like its parent at thumbnail size" (style), and all six track entrants share one Home (skeptic). In heat b two refinements beat their grafts because the fixes were themselves new drawings: Lit Manual's NOW line across the list, and Trued's contents line on every page. What was not fixed, or went unnoticed: the row afterglow as a 12% steel wash, still invisible; the question set small (Trued's 22px under 24px heads cancels its title); and faults the brief itself prescribed. Timetable Relit's "small number badges" on the icons read as unread counts, and Go kept the zero-padding its brief praised. Test a brief's borrowings for how they read, not only its list of old faults.

Titles:
- TRIED, with the key word in brackets:
  - "What's the plan?" [plan], on seven designs, 7.01 to 7.84: the track, timetable and pocket refinements, Live Figures, Flight Console, Go and both switchboards.
  - "What's next for us?" [next], on the three heat-a grafts, 7.56 to 7.84.
  - "What shall we line up?" [line], on Signal Box Relaid, 7.84.
  - "Tell Vera what to plan." [plan], on Lit Manual, 8.02.
  - "Tell Vera what's next." [next], on Lamps and Ladders, 7.53. The titles file lists it as 'Tell *next*.'.
  - Both switchboards left another title in their _ask.html beside Home's "What's the plan?": "What comes next?", Lamp-lit's draft, and "What's on your mind?" in Trued. Feedback and type flagged it: the title needs one source.
  - The carried designs, the mutants and both yardsticks kept "What's on your mind?", and all sixteen title notes failed it as a generic chat opener that says nothing of planning.
- FAVOURED. "What's the plan?" was first or joint first in 13 of the 16 notes. It is the family's own kitchen-table phrase, its lit word is the job, and at 16 characters it holds one line at 40px. Its echo of the placeholder's 'Plans for the weekend' reads as question, then answers, and was accepted. Its risks are that it is becoming the tour's default (style and the skeptic in heat b), and that it can read as asking the family for a plan they may not have. "Tell Vera what to plan." was first for style, interaction and the skeptic, joint first for usability, and second for the rest. It is the clearest label for a first-time user, because it says who hears you and what the box is for, and it is the manual's own voice. It is a little stiff, and 'what to plan' can read as Vera doing the planning. "What's next for us?" has a warm household 'us' and was liked where a NEXT stop or board sits below it. But 'next' leaves out an idea to keep and the wish lists, and it says 'next' three times on one screen. Beside NEXT UP it reads as a question the page has just answered. "Tell Vera what's next." was the weakest new title in all eight heat-b notes: a second glowing 'next' beside NEXT UP competes with it, and it can be read as a request to Vera. "What shall we line up?" was set best (condensed, 40px, the fullest halo), but it lights the motif, not the job. 'Shall' is formal, and lighting 'line' alone splits the verb 'line up'.
- PLACEMENT counts as much as wording. The question must be the largest head on Home. Setting it inside the box's border (Go) was usability's best placement. On glass, when the whole question glows, the one lit word is lost: set the rest at the tube's middle strength, or set the question in Plex as the label. Never light a word that another glow on the same screen already claims.
- TRY NEXT:
  - "What's the plan?" as the default, above all on a terminal's glass.
  - "Tell Vera what to plan." where a lineage speaks in instructions: the manual, and perhaps the console.
  - Feedback's "What shall we plan?" for the track series, which keeps Relaid's 'we' and display size with the job word lit.
  - Untested: "What's our plan?", which keeps the household voice the 'us' titles were liked for while lighting 'plan', for Timetable and Pocket, whose tubes already say NEXT.
  - Drop 'next' from every title set above a NEXT UP screen.

Candidate principles:
1. A figure touches its rows. A drawing of a page's data is joined to the list it draws. Its NOW line crosses the list, its stops are numbered to the rows, and pointing at a row lights its mark: the afterglow's visible place. A lit board replaces the list it would repeat instead of standing beside it.
2. An instrument adds what the words cannot, and never re-codes what they already say. Every lamp group is labelled, and each count is said once, in plain figures with no zero-padding. A device that restates 'in 4 days' or an existing count in a code the family must learn (ladders, mosaics, unnamed lamp shelves, numerals in the navigation) is left out.
3. The greeting's lit word is the family's job, lit once. The title over the box names what the family does with Vera ('plan'), and it is the largest head on Home. No other glow on the same screen claims its word, and on glass only that word runs hot.
