# The owner's own judging notes

Read ONLY by the owner's judge (the "owner's feedback" lens). These are the owner's first-hand
reactions, as one judge's opinion among the panel's: they calibrate the owner's judge to the
owner's taste. They are not guidance for the designers and not rules for the other judges, and
the owner's judge still scores every candidate on its own merits, in the context of what it sees.

## On round 3's four winners (carried into round 4)

The owner's ranking: Desert Night (r3-rand-4) and Navy Room (r3-idea-1) at the top, close
together; on reflection the owner "might put Desert Night slightly ahead of Navy Room, but it's
close". Then a big gap; then Plum Ink (r3-idea-2) third, and Reading Room (r3-rand-1) last.

In the owner's words:
- Navy Room: "the best, but has more blue than I think is necessary and doesn't highlight the
  CRT motif, and doesn't support the signature green/black as well as it could."
- Desert Night: "good, but still uses a little too much monochromatic green and its alternate
  colors don't feel supportive as much as complementary, which isn't my goal; however it is a
  close contender for #1."
- Plum Ink: "I don't like the pink."
- Reading Room: "my least favorite. It's too much brown, and I think the warm colors do not
  match with the CRT phosphor motif. The brown borders don't work in the CRT Phosphor theme.
  The secondary colors should support the CRT green, not detract from it."

What this says about the owner's taste, for calibration: secondary colours should SUPPORT the
green phosphor on black (serve it, recede for it, set it off), not stand beside it as a
complement or compete with it; the CRT motif should be highlighted, not diluted by a room's
colour; warm browns, tans and pinks read as off-motif; a cool companion is closer, but in a
measured amount (not "more blue than necessary").

## On round 7's four carried (carried into round 8)

The sheet showed, in order: #1 Galley Rack (r7-idea-1), #2 Indigo Bindery (r7-rand-5), #3 Radar
Room (r6-idea-2), #4 Signal Box (r7-idea-2). The owner gave no strict ranking this time.

In the owner's words:
- Galley Rack: "I like the variety of colors. I don't love the violet color, but I also don't
  hate it. I do think the color variation and the highlight of the violet color work well. This
  is mostly the CRT look, which is good, but the complementary colors don't fit the 90's/80's
  CRT coloration, which is ok, but kind of distracts from the feel. Cyan might be a better
  choice, or another computer CGA color scheme might work better." And: "The 'coming up' boxes
  in #1 look far better than any other render in shape and rendering, though the color is up
  to interpretation." (Home's Coming up: flat strips with a date designator block on the left,
  the title and time in the body, and a tabular "in 3 days" facts column on the right.)
- Indigo Bindery: "On first glance this is too much purple, but it does complement the green. I
  could be convinced to like this, and the use of two colors that bounce off each other is
  great. I still think it's a little too much purple, but despite my initial aversion to the
  color, the mix does really work. And the purple mixes with other colors. I like the boxes on
  items less than in #1."
- Galley Rack and Indigo Bindery together: "In both, the pewter screen is a little too dominant
  and the border is too thick, plain, and rounded; this is a theme for most of these similar
  renderings." (The monitors' case, the bezel around the green glass.)
- Radar Room: "simpler, less sophisticated, but more on-brand. The pewter box is more subtle,
  which I like, but also less detailed."
- Signal Box: "The line art around 'Ideas' is fantastic. I really love that, and the
  segmentation is beautiful. That rounded line around the Ideas box is one of the best design
  elements I've seen. I'd like to see more of this type of design. It really fits with the feel
  of the system." (On the Ideas page, a thin line rings the page icon, drops down the left side
  and turns a rounded corner under the page head to hold its controls, the segmented All ideas
  and Restaurants switch among them: the mimic-line track as line art.)

What this adds for calibration:
- A second colour is welcome when it plays off the green: two colours that "bounce off each
  other", in a measured amount. Violet and purple work in mixes, but they read as off-period
  for an 80s/90s CRT. Period computer colours are closer to the feel: cyan, or another CGA-like
  set. Too much of any one companion is still a fault.
- Fine line art that draws structure is the owner's favourite element so far: thin rules and
  tracks that ring, connect and segment. Heavy, plain, rounded frames are not. The monitors'
  cases are too dominant across most entrants: they want to be thinner, subtler and more
  detailed, not a thick rounded pewter slab.
- Well-shaped list rows (Galley Rack's Coming up strips) are admired. The boxes around items
  should be as well drawn as those.
- On-brand simplicity (Radar Room) counts in a design's favour, but not at the cost of detail.

Added by the owner afterwards, on Signal Box: "The line art in the Signal Box design is high
style. Aside from the line around the Ideas box, the other lines are creative and unique. I'd
love to see them echoed in other designs." The lines meant (r7-idea-2's shots):
- Home's Coming up: a horizontal track with a lit green "now" dot at its start and a hollow ring
  at each stop. Each day's number and month are set above its ring, the plate hangs below, and
  the track ends in a tick at "All plans".
- Home's To do: a vertical track with a lamp at each item, an amber ring for the overdue one. The
  due times are right-aligned in a timetable margin to its left, a short tick joins each lamp to
  its plate, and the track ends in a foot at "All 4".
- Chat: the conversation hangs from one vertical track under Vera's mark. Each speaker's mark is
  a stop, the times sit in the left margin, and the track ends in a green ring at the message
  box.
- The bar: its bottom rule carries a small dot under the current page.

For calibration: this is the strongest positive signal the owner has given. Fine, thin line art
that draws the page's structure (tracks, stops, ticks, rings, margins) is "high style" and fits
the system's feel. The owner wants it echoed in other designs. Reward entrants that carry this
kind of line language, in their own way and with their own colour, and reward it more when it is
carried through every page rather than one.

## On round 8's four carried (carried into round 9)

The owner, after seeing that Signal Box was not carried into round 9: "disappointed none of the
designs with the lines from Signal Box made it through, really liked that design concept, would
like to see that implemented."

For calibration: Signal Galley (r8-idea-1) kept only one of Signal Box's lines, the Coming up
track on Home. It lost the rest: the dot under the current page in the bar, the line that rings
the page icon and brackets the page head's controls (Ideas), the vertical To do track with its
lamps and timetable margin, and the Chat thread that hangs from Vera's mark. For the owner, a
design that carries this line language well, through every page, is worth more than one that
does not. The owner's disappointment says this weighs about as much as the palette notes above:
treat its absence as a real loss, and its presence, done with restraint (one track per list,
lines that mean time, order or belonging), as a strong reason to score high.

Added by the owner afterwards, on the rails other judges called a "subway map" or "git graph":
"The subway lines in the Signal Box design were a design feature, not a failure. I like how the
lines delimit areas and draw the eye through the frame. There are areas for improvement for
sure, but the lines make the navigation feel fresh and easy to see."

For calibration: this is the owner's own view, and it differs from the skeptic's and style
lenses. Do not mark a design down for reading as a transit map or for having several tracks on
one page (Status's rails included). Judge whether its lines delimit areas, lead the eye through
the page and make the navigation easy to see; that is what the owner values in them. Faults in
how the lines are drawn (weight, clutter, a line that means nothing, a stranded control) still
count.

## On round 9's four carried (carried into round 10)

The sheet showed, in order: #1 Programme Guide (r9-idea-2), #2 Signal Legend (r9-idea-1), #3
Flight Deck (r9-idea-3), #4 Reverse Video (r8-idea-2). The wild lane's sheet showed Attract Mode
(r9-rand-1) and Porcelain Atlas (r9-rand-2).

In the owner's words: "I especially like Flight Deck and Signal Legend. These are beautiful,
modern interfaces. Let's not lose the CRT theme and soul of the original phosphor design."

The two the owner singled out:
- Signal Legend: green-black slate trays with every section head set into its rail, reverse
  video for the current item, square family surfaces against round machine glass, a thin
  monitor case, and #6dff9c lit only for now and live.
- Flight Deck: a 1980s glass cockpit at night. The family's things sit on matte instrument
  plates grouped by fine demarcation lines, with their legends set in the breaks. The machine
  speaks on flush display units in thin screwed faceplates, and each colour holds one meaning,
  as in a cockpit's colour code.

What this adds for calibration:
- The owner likes where the leaders have gone: grounded, well-made, modern surfaces, with fine
  lines that group and delimit (Flight Deck's demarcation lines and Signal Legend's rails are
  both kin to the line art praised in round 7). Beauty and modern craft count in a design's
  favour.
- They must not cost the CRT soul. The page began as a CRT phosphor terminal, and it should
  still feel like one: a machine's green glass that the family talks to, phosphor light where
  it means something, terminal and machine type where the machine speaks, the sense of a
  screen and its instruments. That soul is not the brightness or the all-green monochrome the
  owner asked to calm at the start; it is the character.
- So weigh how much of the CRT phosphor character runs through the whole page, not only inside
  the two monitors. A beautiful design whose CRT lives only in the Next up and radar screens,
  with the rest a generic modern dark interface, is drifting from what the owner wants. A
  design that is both beautiful and modern and unmistakably a phosphor CRT interface on every
  page is what the owner is after. Do not reward a return to the old glare or monochrome: the
  earlier notes on grounded, supporting colour still hold.
