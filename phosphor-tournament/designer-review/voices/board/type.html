Specimen

Type

One sign family and one reading face. Barlow, drawn from California’s highway signs and rail signage, in two widths: Condensed for the board (times, dates, figures, column heads, navigation) and Semi Condensed for headings. Atkinson Hyperlegible for every sentence and every name of a thing. The mono lives only on the glass.

The faces

  • Barlow Semi CondensedHeadings and the wordmark. 700 only. Mixed case, like a British or Californian road sign, because whole word shapes are read faster than capitals. Slightly rounded, low contrast, firm: it reads like a sign, not a book, and it is nobody’s default.
  • Barlow Condensed 0123456789The board: times, dates, drive times, due dates, money, ranks, column heads, navigation and the board’s names. 600 and 700. Narrow figures with tabular spacing, so a column of times lines up and a big “12:30” fits in a phone row. Its zero is open.
  • Atkinson HyperlegibleEverything the family reads as words. 400 and 700, 17 px body. Kept from every earlier round because the kids read it; its slashed zero helps in sentences. Clock times and amounts inside a sentence stay Atkinson, so a line is one voice.
  • JetBrains MonoOnly on the glass: the status pill when something needs noticing, and the lines inside a pane. Never on paper.

Files: barlow-condensed-600.woff2, barlow-condensed-700.woff2, barlow-semicondensed-700.woff2, 60 KB together, Latin, OFL (fonts/barlow-OFL.txt).
