# The Board

**Voice:** the family's day, set like a station departure board: what leaves next, when to go and whose it is, readable from across the kitchen.

## Type

**Barlow** (OFL), from California's highway and rail signs. **Condensed** 600/700 sets the board: times, dates, drive and due figures, money, ranks, column heads, navigation. Its narrow tabular figures line up in columns. **Semi Condensed** 700 sets headings and the wordmark. Everything is mixed case, as on road signs, because word shapes read faster; only small column heads and plate weekdays are capitals. **Atkinson Hyperlegible** stays for every sentence and every title a kid reads. The new fonts total 60 KB; Bricolage is gone.

## Layout, and what each change makes easier

1. **Home's departure board.** Next up runs full width, right under Ask Vera (Round 5). The next plan is the big row, with **Leave by 12:30 pm** as the largest figure on the page. You see when to leave without reading a sentence.
2. **Route stripes.** Each plan row has a bar split into the colours of the people going (Everyone is grey), so you see whose plan it is before reading names.
3. **To-dos on a board.** The due date and "6 days late" sit in their own right-hand column, so the eye runs down one line of dates. Overdue gets its own board with a red block.
4. **Plans: Coming up first**, beside How did it go?, so the next four plans are read before the grid. The month follows as a timetable.
5. **Ideas as a destinations board**: the drive time as a figure, with an arrow turned to its direction. Picking something near is quick.
6. **Wishes as a line diagram**: ranks are platform-number plates joined in the kid's colour.

Everything hangs from ink. The sidebar and phone bar are a navy fascia, every panel has a 4 px top rail, and the page title sits on a heavy rule (in her colour on a kid's pages). "You are here" is always an inverted plate.

## Where it is weaker

- There is a lot of navy.
- The wish-list line only bridges the gaps between cards; it is not one continuous line.
- Narrower weekday columns on the month mean more weekday titles are cut to two lines.
- On the phone, the transcript's margin and the to-dos' own date line cost some width and height.

## Round 5: Vera's box back on top

The family missed the hero box. **Ask Vera is again the first thing under the greeting on Home and the kid's Home.** It is full width, with a 60 px box, the lit Send and the starters (hers on Maya's page). **It is Vera's, not the board's**: charcoal glass rather than navy, rounded like her screen while every board panel is square-cut, a phosphor halo, her screen as its sign, and her one line in the mono after a `>` prompt (Atkinson on a kid's page, since she reads it). **Composition:** the box sits above the board, and the greeting gives up its space for it (see below). I put the box above the board rather than beside the greeting because a full-width box invites writing, and the phone stacks it in the same order. On the phone the greeting, the box and the leave-by figure all fit in the first screen. **Chat's own box** now sits on the same glass with the same Send, so writing to Vera looks the same everywhere.

**The greeting earns its space.** "Good morning, Sam." was a 56 px headline that told nobody anything. It is now one line, the day head: the date in today's green, a small greeting (still the page's `h1`) and, after a rule, the news set as information. "Roller rink tomorrow" is underlined in green and "three to-dos" in red. Ask Vera's head shares a line with her typed sentence, and her starters share a row with "Goes to the family chat as Sam". On a 1280 × 900 desktop, Ask Vera, the whole Next up board with the leave-by time, and the first late to-do are now all on the first screen. On the phone, Ask and the leave-by figure are on the first screen. Every other page's title shrank from 56 to 44 px and shares its line with its sentence.

**Borrowed from House Log, and made the Board's:**
- **One "when" edge.** When is down the left edge on every list and the one figure is down the right. To-dos now open with their due date and how late, like a departure opens with its time. Kids' to-dos do too. On the phone the date takes its own line above the title.
- **Ruled sections, not boxes.** A card that only groups things is now the 4 px ink rail and its head, with no box. Forms, the setup card and the chat room keep their boxes.
- **Chat as a transcript.** Who and when sit in a margin in Barlow Condensed, like the board's when column, and the words sit beside the person's line colour. No bubbles. Vera's receipts and suggestions stay as panels.
- **Wider weekend columns** on the month (1.45×), where the family's plans are.

**Not borrowed:** the numbered sidebar contents (the counts already align, and numbers would be noise), the mono for dates (a third face; Barlow Condensed's tabular figures already do this), the Ideas numbered index (the destinations board already compares drive times), rank numbers in the margin (the Board's plates and line do this), and cool stone paper (the Board's warm enamel is part of its voice).
