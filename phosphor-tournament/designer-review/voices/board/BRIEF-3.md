# Brief 3: make the greeting earn its space

The family again: **"The big 'Good morning' header looks good, but takes a lot of space that
doesn't do any work."**

The greeting block (date, "Good morning, Sam.", and the line under it, "Roller rink tomorrow, and
three to-dos are late") takes the top of Home on desktop and most of the phone's first screen, and
only the last line carries information.

- Make the top of Home do work. Keep the greeting's warmth but make it small: for example one line
  with the date, or folded into the page's head or into Ask Vera's card. The useful part, what's
  coming and what's late with its links, stays prominent, set as information rather than as a
  headline.
- Give the space back to Ask Vera and the board: on desktop, Ask Vera, the next plan with its
  leave-by time and the start of the to-dos should all be on the first screen; on the phone,
  Ask Vera and the next plan's leave-by on the first screen.
- Do the same on the kid's Home. Look at the other pages' big titles with the same question (does
  it do work?) and tighten where it doesn't, without losing each page's one `h1`.
- Words stay as they are; only their setting and place change.

Everything in `BRIEF.md` and `BRIEF-2.md` still holds. Re-render every page light and dark, look
at the shots, fix, render again; add to `DIRECTION.md`'s Round 5 section and update `STANDARD.md`.
Reply with what changed and anything unfinished.

## And: borrow from House Log

The family again: **"House Log is a close second, so perhaps we could borrow some design ideas
from that layout also."**

House Log was another designer's round-4 direction: the household logbook. Its `DIRECTION.md`,
standard, stylesheet and shots are in `borrow-log/`. Its ideas, in its own words: a date and time
margin on every list (when down one edge, what down the other); ruled sections instead of cards, so
more fits on a screen; Chat as a transcript with who and when in the margin; Ideas as a numbered
index with comparable columns (kind, for whom, drive from home, status); the sidebar as a numbered
contents with counts aligned and a running head saying where you are; big rank numbers in the
margin for wishes; dates and times in a narrow mono.

Borrow what makes The Board better and more consistent, and leave the rest. Keep The Board's voice
(the departure board, Barlow, the leave-by figure, the route stripes) in charge: what you borrow
should look like it was always The Board's. Likely good fits: one consistent "when" column on
every list (The Board already has one on Next up and a due column on To do; decide which edge and
use it everywhere), ruled sections where cards are only boxes, and Chat as a transcript. Avoid a
third typeface unless it clearly earns its place. In `DIRECTION.md`, list what you borrowed, what
you didn't and why.
