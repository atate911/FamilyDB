Four voices for FamilyDB

A round on layout, typography and a voice of its own. Four designers each started from a different idea of what FamilyDB is, then rebuilt the layout and type around it. Every page keeps its job and its information. Each shot shows the first screen; press “Whole page” under a shot to see it all.

Departure board

The Board

The family’s day set like a station departure board: what leaves next, when to go and whose it is, readable from across the kitchen.

Type
Barlow Condensed for times, dates and figures, from California’s highway and rail signs; Barlow Semi Condensed for headings; Atkinson for every sentence.
What it makes easier
“Leave by 12:30 pm” is the biggest thing on Home. Each plan has a stripe in the colours of who’s going. Due dates sit in one right-hand column, and ideas show drive time as a big figure.
Where it’s weaker
A lot of navy, so Ask Vera stands out less, and on Home it moves below the board. Chat changed least.

The week on one page

Week at a Glance

The family’s week, printed big: seven days across, today in solid ink, everyone in their own pen.

Type
Archivo at three widths: squeezed narrow for big day numbers and money, heavy for headings, wide for small capitals. Atkinson for every sentence.
What it makes easier
Home leads with the next seven days, so free days show at a glance. A top masthead replaces the sidebar to give the week full width. On the phone the week turns into rows. To-dos lead with their date.
Where it’s weaker
An empty day is a loud black column. Home rolls seven days from today while Plans keeps Monday to Sunday. Long titles wrap in narrow day columns.

The household logbook

House Log

The family’s logbook: Vera keeps the ruling, with dates, times and numbers in a typed margin, and everybody writes the lines in plain words and their own colour.

Type
Archivo condensed for titles and wide capitals for running heads; Martian Mono for the margin of dates and times; Atkinson for every sentence.
What it makes easier
Every list has a date margin, so “when” runs down one edge and “what” down the other. Sections are ruled instead of boxed, so more fits on a screen. Ideas become a table where drive times compare down one column.
Where it’s weaker
Thin rules can fade on dim screens. On a phone the margin squeezes titles. The Ideas table needs a wide screen.

Talk first

Thread

The household’s running conversation: one line down the page with the clock in its margin, Vera typing in a steady mono, and every plan, to-do and wish dropping in as its own object.

Type
Familjen Grotesk for the names of things, with its italic for kids’ wishes; Atkinson Hyperlegible Mono for Vera and the clock; Atkinson for every sentence.
What it makes easier
Home is one timeline from “Now”. Chat is the middle tab on the phone, and there’s a “Write to Vera” box on every desktop page. Plans look like tickets, to-dos like ticked lines, and wishes like quotes.
Where it’s weaker
The closest of the four to what you’ve seen. Your own messages no longer sit on the right. Some pages offer two places to write.

Reading them together

The Board and Week at a Glance are the boldest: each builds Home around one idea you can see from across the room, the time to leave or the week ahead.

House Log is the calmest and densest: text instead of boxes, with when and what in two clean columns.

Thread changes the least to look at, but the most about how you use it: writing to Vera is always to hand, and everything else reads as one timeline.

Pieces travel between them. The Board’s leave-by figure, the Week’s seven-day strip and the Log’s date margin could each live inside another.
