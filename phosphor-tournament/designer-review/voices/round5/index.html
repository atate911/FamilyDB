The Board

The family’s day set like a station departure board: what leaves next, when to go and whose it is, readable from across the kitchen.

Type
Barlow Condensed for times, dates and figures; Barlow Semi Condensed for headings; Vera’s line in a typed mono; Atkinson for every sentence.
What it makes easier
One-line greeting, then Ask Vera full width on her own dark glass, then the board with “Leave by 12:30 pm”, then the late to-dos, all on the first desktop screen. Every list has its date down the left edge. Chat is a transcript. Ruled sections where boxes did nothing.
Where it’s weaker
Navy panel heads beside Vera’s charcoal box: told apart by shape and her green glow. Wider weekend columns cut weekday titles shorter. Maya’s pink is unchanged until the colour round.

House Log

The family’s logbook: Vera keeps the ruling, with dates and times in a typed margin, and everybody writes the lines in plain words and their own colour.

Type
Archivo condensed for titles, wide capitals for running heads; Martian Mono for the margin of dates and times; Atkinson for every sentence.
What it makes easier
One-line greeting, then Ask Vera as the only lit, rounded block on the page, then a departure-board Next up with “12:30 pm” at 72 px and route stripes. The due date and how late sit together in the margin. Darker rules for dim screens; on the phone the date sits above the title so titles get the full width.
Where it’s weaker
Long directions wrap on the phone. The phone calendar still shows dots, not stripes. Maya’s plum is a stand-in until the colour round.

What’s left between them

After this round they do the same things in the same order. The difference is the feel. The Board is bolder and more structured: navy panel heads, narrow sign lettering and square plates. House Log is lighter and quieter: no panels, lines instead of boxes, and dates typed in a margin.

Next is the colour round on whichever you pick, without the hot pink.
