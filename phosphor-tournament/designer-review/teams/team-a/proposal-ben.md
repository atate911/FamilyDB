# Proposal: Ben

**The idea:** A calm, modern app where phosphor is light, not costume: green glow only means "now", "live" or "act here", and each page has one instrument that draws what its list cannot.

## The look

- **Type.** Two voices. A clean grotesque for everything people read: headings large and tight, body at 15px, nothing under 13px. A phosphor mono for what the machine says: times, counts, drive minutes, instrument screens. No serif, no third face.
- **Colour.** A near-black, green-tinted ground with graphite panels and bone-white text. One green at three strengths: trace (hairlines), lit (live state, current page, primary button) and glow (focus and "now" only). Amber for late and needs-a-look. Each kind of idea gets one muted hue on its icon tile, and nothing else is coloured.
- **Structure.** Each page has a title carrying its count ("Ideas 12", "To do 4 · 3 late"), then one instrument joined to its list by a trace line, then the list, in rows.
- **Rules.**
  1. One instrument per page, and only where it shows what the list cannot.
  2. Glow is a signal, never a texture.
  3. Scanlines and bezels only inside instruments.
  4. Numbers people compare are big; labels are small mono capitals.
  5. Phone first: an instrument becomes a strip and never clips its text.

## The pages

- **Home.** "Tell Vera. Let's plan." beside the input. The starters say where each one lands. The instrument is a radar clock: four weeks round a dial, each plan a blip, with Next up read out in the centre. Below come a ledger (Today, Plans, To do, Late, Ideas), three numbered setup steps, then Coming up, To do and Lately added.
- **Chat.** One readable column, with the times on a thin trace between the speakers. The composer is marked "your turn" in the "now" glow. Vera's mark is her small glyph screen. The kids' conversations go in a side rail.
- **Ideas.** The instrument is the line from home: each idea a numbered blip by drive minutes. The list is sorted nearest first, with the drive time as each row's big number. Kind and status are segmented controls.
- **To do.** The instrument is one bar: late, this week, later, no date. Rows carry date tiles, and a "NOW" line separates late from coming. Filters by who ("Alex 2 late"); add inline at the top.
- **Plans.** The month grid. A day with a plan has a ringed numeral, today is a lit disc, weekends are shaded and empty days are dim. Above the grid, a hop-arc timeline across the weeks.
- **Status.** The instrument is a moving-coil spend gauge against the limit. Then one list (who answers, keys, connections) marked lit dot, ring or amber triangle, with the state in words.
- **Settings.** The parts with their state in words, needs-a-look first in amber. No instrument: nothing here needs drawing.

## Taken from, and refused

- **Taken from.** D14: the counts in the titles, the ledger, the hop-arc joined to Next up, the spend gauge and the footer status line. D27: the radar clock and the headline. D18: the line from home, "your turn", and starters that say where they land. D13: the "NOW" divider and the date tiles. D15: Vera's glyph screen. D12: the cursor after the logotype.
- **Refused.** A monitor on every page. Teletext page numbers. Three typefaces. Glowing panels. Text clipped by a bezel. Mono for sentences. Legends explaining the marks: a mark that needs one is the wrong mark.

## Why it wins

Any of the 37 could make a green screen. The best ones (D14, D27, D18) made the screen mean something. This turns that into rules a builder can hold on every page: one meaning for glow, one instrument with one job, readable sizes. It is unmistakably phosphor, and still the easiest app in the field to use.
