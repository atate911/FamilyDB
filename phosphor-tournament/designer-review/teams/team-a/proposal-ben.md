# Proposal: Ben

**The idea:** A calm, modern app whose phosphor is light rather than costume. Green glow only ever means "now", "live" or "yours to act on", and each page has exactly one instrument that draws what its list cannot.

## The look

- **Type.** Two voices. The first is a clean grotesque for everything people read: headings large and tight, body at 15px minimum, never below 13px anywhere. The second is a phosphor mono for what the machine says: times, counts, drive minutes, the instrument's own screen. No serif, and no third face.
- **Colour.** A near-black green-tinted ground, graphite panels and soft bone-white text. One phosphor green, used in three strengths: trace (hairlines and rules), lit (live state, the current page, primary buttons) and glow (focus and "now" only). Amber is kept for late and for needs-a-look. Each kind of idea gets one muted hue for its icon tile, and that is the only other colour.
- **Structure.** The page title carries its count in the same line ("Ideas 12", "To do 4 · 3 late"). Under it comes the page's one instrument, sitting beside or above the list it draws, joined to it by a trace line. Below that is the list, in rows rather than cards.
- **Rules it keeps.**
  1. One instrument per page, and only where it shows something the list does not. No monitor as ornament.
  2. Glow is a signal, not a texture. The only things that glow are the focus ring, the "now" marker, the current page and the one primary action.
  3. Scanlines and the bezel appear only inside instruments, never on panels.
  4. Numbers people compare are big. Labels are small mono capitals.
  5. Everything works on a phone first. Instruments shrink to a single strip there and never clip their text.

## The pages

- **Home.** The headline sits beside the input ("Tell Vera. Let's plan."). Under it, the starters say where each one lands ("lands in Plans"). The instrument is a radar clock: the next four weeks as a dial, each plan a blip on its day, and "Next up" read out in the centre. Below come a ledger row (Today, Plans, To do, Late, Ideas, each with dotted leaders), Finish setting up as three numbered steps, then Coming up, To do and Lately added. On the phone the dial becomes a strip of four weeks.
- **Chat.** One column, wide enough to read. The times sit on a thin trace between the speakers, and the composer is marked "your turn" with the "now" glow. Vera's mark is her small unreadable glyph screen. The kids' conversations go in a side rail on desktop and below the thread on the phone.
- **Ideas.** The instrument is "the line from home": every idea as a numbered blip on one line of drive minutes, nearest to furthest. Sorting is "Nearest first" by default, with the drive time as the row's big number. Kinds and status are segmented controls, and there is a single search.
- **To do.** The instrument is a quiet bar of open, late, this week and no date. Rows carry big date tiles, and the "NOW" line separates late from coming. You tick to finish, filter by who ("Alex 2 late"), and add inline at the top.
- **Plans.** The month grid. Days with a plan get a ringed numeral, today is a lit disc, and weekends are shaded. Empty days stay dim, so the plans carry the page. The instrument is the hop-arc timeline across the weeks above the grid.
- **Status.** The instrument is a moving-coil gauge for spend today against the limit. Below it, one list (who answers, keys, connected) with a lit dot, a ring or an amber triangle, and plain words for each state.
- **Settings.** A list of parts, each with its state in words, and needs-a-look floated to the top in amber. No instrument: nothing here needs drawing.

## Taken from, and refused

- **Taken from.** D14: the headline counts, the ledger, the hop-arc timeline joined to Next up, the spend gauge and the footer status line. D27: the radar clock dial on Home and the headline. D18: the line from home on Ideas, "your turn" on the composer, and the starters that say where they land. D13: the "NOW" divider in To do and the dated tiles. D15: Vera's glyph screen and "lands in". D12: the cursor after the logotype.
- **Refused.** A monitor on every page. Teletext page numbers. Three typefaces. Glowing panels. Text clipped by a bezel. Mono for sentences. Tiny legends that explain the marks: if a mark needs a legend, it is the wrong mark.

## Why it wins

Every design in the field could make a green screen. The ones that scored best (D14, D27, D18) are those where the screen means something. This proposal turns that into rules a builder can keep on every page: one glow meaning, one instrument with one job, readable sizes. The result looks unmistakably phosphor and is still the easiest app in the field to use.
