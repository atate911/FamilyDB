# Proposal: Finn

**The idea:** A calm, plain family app where only what matters now glows, like a word just written on a phosphor tube, and fades as it ages.

## The look

- **Type.** IBM Plex Sans for everything people read: titles in sentence case, semibold, no display tricks. IBM Plex Mono only for times, counts and the text inside the one screen.
- **Colour.** A near-black green ground (#0b0f0c), warm off-white text (#e6e3d6), phosphor green (#6bff9e) and amber (#ffb347). There are no other hues. Kinds of idea are told apart by a line icon and a word, never by colour.
- **Structure.** A single centred column, up to 1100px wide, with one level of border. Lists are rows on hairlines. Nothing is boxed inside a box.
- **Rules it keeps:**
  1. **Glow means now or new.** Persistence is the phosphor idea: whatever is new or next glows, and the glow decays in three steps (today bright, this week dim, older plain). Vera's latest reply, an idea added this morning and the next plan all glow; last month's do not.
  2. **One screen per page at most.** Each page gets a single dark green monitor panel for its one key answer. There is no bezel gloss and no fake hardware: just a recessed panel with faint scanlines.
  3. **Amber means it needs you.** Late tasks and "needs a look" are amber, and nothing else is.
  4. **Glow never carries meaning alone.** Every glow has a word or a position too. Text meets AA contrast, and `prefers-reduced-motion` stops the sweep and the flicker.

## The pages

- **Home.** It opens with "What needs a plan?", then the box, a labelled Send, and three starters that say where each usually lands (Plans, To do, Ideas). The screen sits beside the box. It shows Next up and a NOW to +4W ruler with the other plans marked on it. Below the screen, a lit line drops into three plain lists side by side (Coming up, To do, Lately added), and one "now" rule crosses them. Setup is a single amber line, not a panel.
- **Chat.** One column, 680px wide, with no bubbles. Each line has the name and time in mono, with the words underneath. Vera's newest reply glows and fades to plain as the next one arrives. The box stays pinned at the foot. The kids' conversations are tabs above the thread, not a section below it.
- **Ideas.** The screen is "the line from home", a full-width strip with places plotted by minutes on a log scale so they spread out. Pointing at a row lights its stop. The table below runs at full width, and a filter strip sits above it.
- **To do.** There is no screen, only a big mono count with "3 late" in amber. Rows hang on a day rail. A glowing NOW rule splits overdue from upcoming. Each row ends in its tick and Edit.
- **Plans.** The screen is a Next up banner with the four-week ruler. The month grid stays quiet: today's cell is outlined green, a plan shows as its time and name, and empty weeks fold into one line ("26 Oct – 1 Nov · nothing on").
- **Status.** This page gets the most instrument. The screen holds lamp rows for who answers, keys and connected, with counts, beside a needle meter for today's spend. Plain lists sit below, each opening from a line in a contents list on the page.
- **Settings.** No screen. Nine rows, each with its status stacked under its label, so nothing sits across the page. An amber dot marks a part that needs a look.
- **Phone.** A bottom tab bar, a labelled Send, and the screen shrunk to a strip.

## Taken from and refused

- **Taken from:**
  - D19: the Coming up monitor and its NOW to +4W ruler, and the clean header (icon, title, count).
  - D20: the Status lamp panel with its meter, and folding empty weeks into one line.
  - D16: the lit wire dropping from the next plan into the timeline.
  - D18: starters labelled with where they land.
  - D17: dotted-leader stat lines.
  - D01: the scope's scale caption.
- **Refused:**
  - Glossy bezels and watch dials.
  - A rainbow of kind colours.
  - Cards inside cards.
  - Radars on pages where distance does not matter.
  - Green body text.
  - Legends parked in the footer.
  - An icon-only Send.

## Why it wins

Most of the 37 designs reached the same answer: CRT props everywhere. That gives a lot of character and a lot of clutter. This proposal keeps the character in the screen's behaviour rather than in its furniture. Glow is the screen's real property, and here it does a job: it shows the family what is new and what is next at a glance, then gets out of the way. With one screen per page, one warning colour, one border level and plain readable type, the app is modern and easy, and the phosphor still shows from across the room.
