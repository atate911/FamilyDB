# Kitchen Wall

**The idea:** FamilyDB as the family calendar on the kitchen wall: a bright, ruled grid where dates and times are the main material, today is green, and every other colour is a person.

## What changed from Kitchen Table, and why

**Type first.** The recipe-site feel came mostly from Fraunces: soft serif headings and serif figures. Headings, the wordmark and every date, time and amount are now **Manrope** (OFL, one 21 KB variable file). I chose it for its figures: lining, wide, with an open zero and true tabular digits, so "17", "12:30 pm" and "$0.00" read as a schedule from across the room. Headings are 800/700 and tracked in. Atkinson Hyperlegible still sets every word, for the kids. One face now does headings and numbers, so the big date, the money and a time in a sentence are the same digits.

**Surface.** Cream paper, beige lines and warm card fills are now a cool grey page, white cards and sidebar, and crisp hairlines. Radii are tighter (12/8/6), buttons are rectangles, and there are no warm shadows.

**Colour does jobs.** Ink is for pressing and picking. Green means only today and Vera. Forest-and-sun is gone: the Ask card is the brand's charcoal glass with a phosphor Send. People keep their eight colours, and a one-person plan on the calendar is now a solid block in that colour with white words. Plans for several people stay grey with faces.

**Dates as objects.** Date tiles are pages off a wall calendar: an ink weekday band and a Manrope 800 day. Today gets a green disc, a green top rule and a wash on the calendar. On the desktop Home it also gets a green tile beside the greeting, with the same date as the line above it. It is hidden on the phone, to keep the first screen.

## Where it is weaker

- Some warmth is gone. It sits closer to generic productivity software, and the warmth now rests on the people's colours and Vera's words.
- Solid calendar blocks are loud. A busy month can outshout today's green.
- Ink tile bands repeat down every list and are heavier than the old grey ones.
- The amber "set this up" card is the last warm fill, and it stands out more on white.
