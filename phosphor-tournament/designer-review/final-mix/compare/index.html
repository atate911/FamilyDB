Six palettes for FamilyDB

The merged layout (House Log with The Board’s navy panel) in six palettes: the first three, and three new ones. Layout and type are the same in all six. Every palette avoids hot pink, keeps red for late only, and leaves Vera’s green screen as the one lit thing. At the top, each palette’s sheet sits above its Home page. Each shot shows the first screen; press “Whole page” to see it all.

Palette 1

Rail yellow

Like good station signage: a deep rail-blue panel, a crisp light page with blue-black ink, and one signal yellow, always a solid plate, marking what you act on next.

People
Cobalt, violet, plum (Maya), tangerine, then petrol, sky, sand and chestnut. Each avatar letter is white or ink, whichever reads best.
Signals
Yellow only as a plate: today, the Leave by label, “you are here” in the panel. Setup and warnings move to copper so they can’t be mistaken for the yellow. Links and the main button are the panel’s blue.
Weaker
At night cobalt and violet stripes are the closest pair for red-green colour blindness. Sky, sand and tangerine avatars fade at the edges on white.

Palette 2

Metro lines

The family as a transit map: a white sheet, black ink, a deep navy key panel, and each person one flat line colour, the same on an avatar, a route stripe and a rule.

People
Blue, teal, violet (Maya), amber (Theo), then sky, plum, bronze and petrol: flat, confident line colours.
Signals
Green stays today and Vera’s; red only late; set up is a dark amber. The cleanest and most graphic of the three.
Weaker
The closest colour-blind pair (teal and plum) is the tightest of the three. At night the panel is close to the page in lightness and is held apart by hue.

Palette 3

Enamel

The colours of old enamel signs and good painted interiors: a warm grey plaster page, a deep petrol panel, earthy enamel colours for the people, and Vera’s green glass still the one lit thing.

People
Slate blue, heather, plum (Maya), ochre (Theo), then teal, duck-egg, lichen and mustard, alternating light and dark round the wheel.
Signals
Late is a brick red; set up a deep honey; today and Vera green. The warmest of the three without going cream.
Weaker
Softer and less crisp than the other two. Theo’s stripe had to be a dark umber, not his ochre, to stay apart for colour-blind eyes.

Palette 4

Amber Terminal

A clean light page, navy-black ink and a near-black navy panel, with amber phosphor lighting the next thing you act on, like an old amber terminal, while Vera keeps the green one.

People
Cobalt (Sam), violet (Alex), teal (Maya), olive (Theo), then plum, sky, walnut and petrol.
Signals
Amber is only ever lit: amber letters on navy for today, Tomorrow and Leave by, and an amber plate for the page you’re on. Setup moves to copper, always printed with ⚠, so the two never meet. The main button is navy with an amber label by day.
Weaker
By day the panel and Vera’s glass are both near-black; her green rim and round corners keep them apart. Late red and copper are close for one kind of colour blindness; words carry them.

Palette 5

Ultramarine

One saturated fountain-pen blue for everything the family does, on a bright white page under a midnight panel, with people in every colour but blue and Vera alone in phosphor green.

People
Cerulean (Sam), teal (Alex), violet (Maya), cinnamon (Theo), then ochre, aubergine, sage and slate.
Signals
The blue marks links, the main button, the page you’re on, today and a ticked box. Green now means only Vera and done; red only late.
Weaker
The boldest of the six, and the most like a modern product. Sam and Alex are both cool colours, the closest pair of the four. Theo goes from orange to a dark cinnamon by day.

Palette 6

Fjord

A pale northern sky over deep slate water: a cool, quiet page where the only colours are the family’s eight, fjord blue marks what you act on, and Vera’s green glass is the one thing that glows.

People
Cornflower (Sam), heather (Alex), cloudberry (Maya), sea (Theo), then pine, lichen, rye and lupin.
Signals
Fjord blue for links and the main button; every neutral sits on one blue-grey line from white to the slate panel, so nothing competes with the people or Vera.
Weaker
The quietest of the six. Cornflower and heather are the closest pair for red-green colour blindness. The setup panel is the one warm thing on a cool page.

Telling them apart

Character: Rail yellow and Amber Terminal give the page one bright signal colour for what to do next; Ultramarine gives it one bold blue for everything the family does.

Calm: Enamel is the warm one, Fjord the cool one, and Metro lines the crisp, graphic one in between.

History: Amber Terminal echoes the old terminals most, with amber beside Vera’s green.

Pieces mix well: one palette’s people with another’s page and signal colour.
