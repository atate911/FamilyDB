# Designer's review, group D

Reference: today's Phosphor (`00-current`). It is quiet and generic: rounded dark cards, one radar monitor on Home and Ideas, green used sparingly. It has no strong idea, but it has no camel either. Each design below is judged against that baseline. The question is whether it found one idea, or only added things.

---

## Patch Bay (`r17-wild-1`)

**The idea in one line**
The page as a studio equipment rack. Every panel is screwed in at the corners, page titles hang on a patch cable with a jack at each end, and the monitor is bolted into the rack.

**What works**
- The idea is legible within a second. Screw heads at the corners of the ask panel, the Next up monitor, the Coming up, To do and Lately added panels, and even the calendar cells all say "hardware". You can name the metaphor.
- The page-title cable is a good piece of structure. On Status, Plans, To do and Ideas the cable sags under the title, with ring terminals at each end, and its colour changes by page: mint on Status, blue on Plans, amber on To do, violet on Ideas. It is the owner's "thin track with stops", and it says which page you are on without a badge.
- Coming up uses green segment-display numerals (9, 17, 24) in black tiles under small OCT and day labels. They read as instruments and scan faster than today's tiny date chips.
- The Home grid is tighter than today's. Coming up, To do (with the kids' wishes under it) and Lately added sit in three columns above the fold, with Finish setting up as one strip across the bottom. More is visible with less scrolling.
- The Status cost monitor shows `$0.00` with a bracket bar `[..........] 0%` and "OF THE $2.00 DAILY LIMIT". That is the CRT used for a number that matters.

**What doesn't**
- The bar and footer are brown: a dark wood or leather grain. The brief names browns as off-motif. The first thing on every page is the one colour family the owner ruled out.
- The orange coiled cable between the ask panel and the monitor on Home reads as decoration. It connects nothing that means anything, and it is a warm orange accent where the brief asks for cool companions.
- Uppercase condensed type is everywhere: VERA, IDEAS, STATUS, THINGS TO DO, every panel label, every nav item, every button (SEND, FILTER, EARLIER, LATER), even "EDIT, COMPLETE, OR SNOOZE". With everything shouting, nothing does. Labels set in tracked caps are rack engraving, but a sentence-length action in caps is just hard to read.
- Screws on every calendar cell turn the Plans month into 35 small faceplates. The event chips then become another layer of plate on top of the plate.
- Too many accents. Each idea card has its own coloured top edge (amber, orange, mint, blue), the to-do cards have amber tops, each page has its own cable colour, and Settings has a green LED on every card with one amber. The phosphor green stops being special.

**What's beautiful**
- The mint cable under STATUS on Status: one thin, slightly sagging line between two small ring terminals, the full width of the title block. It is fine line art, it draws structure, and it is calm.
- The segment-display "17" in its black tile on Coming up, with "SAT" under it. A real instrument numeral.
- The Status cost readout: green `$0.00` with the dotted bracket bar under it, inside the dark bezel.

**What's ugly**
- The brown wood-grain bar. It makes the app look like a 1970s hi-fi catalogue, not a green CRT.
- The orange coil of cable on Home, top centre, looping out of the ask panel's corner. It looks like a stray earphone lead.
- The Plans month: a screw in the corner of every day cell. Thirty-five extra dots of noise on the one page that needs to be quiet.
- "EDIT, COMPLETE, OR SNOOZE" in tracked caps under every to-do.

**Committee check**
The rack idea is one idea, but it has been applied to everything, and then extra ideas sit on top of it. There are screws, cables, segment numerals, LEDs on the Settings cards, coloured top borders on cards, per-page colours, wood grain, the radar monitor and the cost monitor. The idea-card colour edges and Settings LEDs look like answers to "make the kinds easier to tell apart" and "show status at a glance". The orange cable looks like an answer to "connect the ask box to the monitor". None of them needed the rack.

**What I'd do**
1. Replace the brown bar and footer with the page's own green-black. This one change does the most.
2. Delete the orange cable on Home.
3. Take the screws off the calendar cells, the to-do cards and the idea cards. Keep them only on the outer panels on Home and on the monitor bezels, where they mean "a bolted-in instrument".
4. Set the nav, buttons and the to-do action line in sentence case. Keep tracked caps for panel labels only.
5. Give every page cable one colour, the phosphor or a single cool companion. Drop the coloured card tops and use one small kind label instead.
6. Remove the per-card LEDs on Settings except where one needs a look.

**Verdict**
5/10. A real idea with two lovely parts (the title cable and the segment numerals), buried under brown wood, orange wire and screws on everything.

## Green Desk (`r18-wild-1`)

**The idea in one line**
An early Macintosh desktop seen in green: striped window title bars, a bitmap display face, desktop icons, and a vertical "NOW" spine down the left of every page.

**What works**
- The striped title bar (a close box at the left, hatched rules either side of a centred title) is a precise period reference. On Home's Coming up, To do and Lately added it frames each section crisply.
- Lately added on Home is drawn as desktop icons: a framed glyph above a centred label (Oaks Park roller rink, Pho Oregon, Silver Falls hike, Cannon Beach weekend). It is the strongest single moment, and it suits "things you might open".
- The chat is cleanly laid out. Every message is its own small window with the sender's name in the title bar, and the avatar and time sit in a narrow column at the left. You can read a day's conversation down one edge.
- The phone Home is honest: a big full-width Send, the chips below, the monitor after.

**What doesn't**
- Two references are fighting. The early-GUI desk (title bars, icons, bitmap face) and the green CRT (radar, phosphor bloom) belong to different machines. The Next up monitor has a beige, platinum-plastic bezel, which is tan: off-motif by the brief, and a third object on the desk.
- The pixel display face is used for the page titles and also for most of the small text: card titles, labels, to-do names. At body size it is tiring, and the bitmap zeros make times read oddly ("19:00", "10:00 to 14:00" with crossed zeros in the chat).
- The NOW spine with its tag sits beside every page title, even Settings and Status, where "now" means nothing. The mauve square stops on it (beside each section) are pink, which the brief rules out, and they repeat the pink OCT headers on the date tiles and the pink squares by Maya and Theo.
- Every nav item fills with a different solid colour when active: green for Vera, lilac for Ideas, blue for Plans, yellow for To do. Big filled blocks in four hues are the loudest thing on each page, and none of them is the signature green.
- Settings has three frames per card: a striped title bar, an icon box, and the card itself, plus an arrow. That is a lot of box for a line of status text.

**What's beautiful**
- The striped title bar on "Coming up" on Home: the hatched lines left and right of the label, the tiny close box, all at a hairline weight. It is drawn structure, and it is exact.
- The desktop-icon treatment of Lately added: four framed glyphs in a row, centred captions, nothing else. Calm, and funny in the right way.
- The pixel "Status" title next to its framed pulse icon, with the mint NOW tag and stop to the left, in the close crop. As a single heading it is handsome.

**What's ugly**
- The beige monitor bezel on Home, Ideas and Status. Tan plastic on green-black looks like a pasted-in photo.
- The mauve-pink stops and the pink OCT headers on the Coming up dates.
- The active nav pills: a solid yellow "To do" block on To do, a solid lilac "Ideas" block on Ideas. They look like highlighter on a screen.
- Body copy in the bitmap face, especially to-do titles like "Book the campsite at Silver Falls".

**Committee check**
This is the clearest camel in the group. There is a Mac desktop (title bars, icons, bitmap type), a timeline (the NOW spine with stops), a CRT (radar, glow), a beige computer (the bezel), a per-page colour code (the nav pills), and pink as a fourth companion. Each is fine on its own. Together they never agree on what machine we are looking at. The NOW spine on Settings is there only because the spine was made a page-level feature.

**What I'd do**
1. Choose the desk or the CRT. If the desk: keep the title bars and icons, drop the radar monitor and its beige bezel, and let a title bar's inner field carry the phosphor.
2. Set body text, card titles and labels in a plain sans. Keep the bitmap face for page titles only.
3. Remove the NOW spine from every page except where time runs down it (Chat, To do).
4. Make the spine stops, the date-tile headers and the kids' markers the phosphor or one cool companion, not pink.
5. Make the active nav one treatment in one colour.
6. On Settings, keep the title bar and drop the icon box, or the reverse.

**Verdict**
4/10. Two good period ideas (the title bar and the desktop icons) inside a design that is four machines at once.

## Trued, Yard Lit (`x14b-trued-cross`)

**The idea in one line**
Everything is a line with stops on it. Time on Home, the conversation in Chat, distance on Ideas, due dates on To do and health on Status are each drawn as a lit track with rings where things are.

**What works**
- This is one idea, carried through. Home's Coming up has a horizontal track from "now" through 4 Oct, 9 Fri, 17 Sat and 24 Sat, with the plan cards hanging from it. Chat runs down a vertical time spine (19:47, 19:48, 19:52, 19:53) that ends in a green stop labelled "your turn" at the message box. Ideas puts every idea on "the line from home" by minutes of driving. To do has a due track, with a lit "Now" bar dividing late from to come.
- It is the owner's fine line art put to work: thin tracks, ring stops, tick ends (⊢ ... ⊣) on section rules. It draws structure; it is not decoration.
- The page titles are well made. A ringed icon, a big grotesque title, and a thin rule that drops from the icon and runs under the subtitle. "Ideas 12 in all · 10 on the line" puts the count beside the title, small.
- Settings is a list, not a grid of cards: icon, name, a status dot, one line of state, an arrow. It is the calmest page in the group and the easiest to scan.
- The phone Home turns the monitor into a one-line ticker ("NEXT Oaks Park roller rink · Sun 13:00") and the start chips into a short track of stops. The idea survives the small screen.
- "Tell Vera what to plan." with only "plan." lit in phosphor bloom. The green is used where it means something.

**What doesn't**
- The track has been applied where it is only a pattern. "Around the house" on Home draws Wishes, Memory, Family and Settings as stops on a line. They are not in any order, so the line means nothing. The thin status strip ("Ready · OpenAI gpt-6-luna · $0.00 of $2.00 today") is a track too, and it repeats on To do.
- Plans has the track above the month grid and the month grid. Both show the same four dates. One is enough.
- Status has a needle gauge for cost, a row of lit dots per service, and then the Who answers list again as a vertical track. Three readouts for one state.
- Home still carries the radar monitor beside the Coming up track. Both say "what is next". The monitor is the old design's prop sitting next to the new design's idea.
- The Chat is a little crowded: the times on the spine, the bubbles on both sides, and the little avatar marks on the spine all sit within a narrow column.

**What's beautiful**
- The Chat spine ending in a green ring and the words "your turn", at the top edge of the message box. The structure says whose move it is, without a sentence.
- The Ideas "line from home": a hairline from "home" to 3 h, with ticks at 15 min, 30 min, 1 h, 2 h, and the idea numbers stacked as rings above it (7, 10, 6, 2, 8, 1 crowd near home; 4 sits alone at the end). It is a real chart, and it is lovely.
- The To do "Now" bar: one lit green line that crosses the due track and the table, with three amber stops above it and one grey ring below.
- The title rule under "Status": it drops from the ringed icon and runs to the right under "Is it working, and what is it costing?". One stroke, two jobs.

**What's ugly**
- "Around the house" on Home. Four unrelated pages strung on a line, with a lit dot under Settings for no reason you can tell.
- The Plans page's track above the calendar, with rings at 3, 4, 9, 17 and 24, then the same days ringed again in the grid.
- The Status needle gauge. A gauge for $0.00 of $2.00 is a lot of drawing to show zero.

**Committee check**
The idea is single and good. The accretion is at the edges: the radar monitor kept from the old page, the cost gauge, the dot rows on Status, the status strip, the Around the house track, and the extra track on Plans. Each looks like something a judge asked to keep or to add. Take them out and the line is stronger, not weaker.

**What I'd do**
1. Remove "Around the house" from Home. Those pages are in the bar.
2. Remove the track above the Plans month. The grid with its ringed days is the plan.
3. On Home, drop the radar monitor or make it the start of the Coming up track (the "now" stop as the lit screen). Do not have both.
4. On Status, keep one readout: the lit-dot rows or the vertical tracks, not both, and the cost as a number with the limit, not a gauge.
5. Keep the status strip on Home only, or drop it.
6. Give the Chat spine more room by moving the times into the bubbles' headers.

**Verdict**
7/10. The only design here that is one idea from page to page, and the idea is the owner's own: thin lit tracks with stops. It needs a pruning, not a rethink.

## Waypoint, Patched (`x14b-waypoint-cross`)

**The idea in one line**
An editorial page with numbers. Each page leads with a big title and its count ("Ideas 12", "Status 5", "Plans 4", "Things to do 4"), then tables and rules, with the CRT kept to a few lit readouts.

**What works**
- The type system is the best in the group. A large grotesque title, a numeral beside it in a lighter tone, a small two-line caption ("ideas / last added Sun 27 Sep"). It reads like a well-set magazine contents page.
- Tables are honest tables. Ideas lists number, idea, kind, for, the drive time as a big green numeral ("18 min", "19 min", "21 min") and a heading ("NW", "S"). Sorted nearest first, the drive column becomes the chart.
- Leader dots on Status ("Today ........ $0.00 of the $2.00 limit", "Last 30 days ........ $1.26, about $0.04 a day") are a quiet, correct typographic device for a statement.
- Hairline section rules with a short lead stroke ("—— Coming up", "—— Who answers", "—— Each part") give every page the same skeleton.
- Ideas' filters as segmented rows (Any, Activity, Day trip, Event...) are clearer than today's dropdowns.
- The Home stat column (Today Sat 3 Oct, Plans 4, To do 4, Late 3, Ideas 12) uses big light numerals with leader rules. On the phone it becomes a four-cell strip. It is useful and handsome.

**What doesn't**
- Home says "what is next" four times. The stat column counts the plans, the Coming up hop-arc track draws them, the list under it lists them with big date numerals and a "Next" tag, and the radar monitor beside the list shows Oaks Park roller rink again, connected by a line to the list row.
- Three CRT props: the radar on Home, the radar on Ideas (top right of the filter block), and the needle gauge on Status. Each is lovely, but three monitors on three pages turn the CRT into a widget.
- The hop-arcs (Home's Coming up, and Plans above the month) are another drawing language next to the rules and leader dots. On Plans they duplicate the grid below.
- Ideas' kind column uses a colour per kind: orange Restaurant, magenta Show, violet Event, amber Activity. In a table with a strong green numeral column, the extra hues fight. Magenta is a pink.
- The footer carries a legend ("in / yours / the page / needs a look") and a status strip above it. If the marks need a legend, they are too many.

**What's beautiful**
- "Ideas 12" at the top of Ideas: the big "Ideas", the lighter "12", and "ideas / last added Sun 27 Sep" tucked small at the baseline. Exact.
- The drive-time column on Ideas: "18 min" in phosphor green at a larger size, then the rest in grey below it. The nearest one is the one lit. That is light where it means something.
- The Status leader-dot statement, "Last 30 days .... $1.26, about $0.04 a day (the faint needle)".
- The To do "NOW" rule: a lit green dot and hairline across the table between the three late items and "Renew library cards".

**What's ugly**
- The radar monitor on Home, connected to the "Next" row by a thin line with a ring at its end. A wire from a list row into a television.
- The radar on Ideas, squeezed into the filter block's corner, next to a search field it has nothing to do with.
- The Status gauge's amber zone at $2 and the amber "Needs a look" bar above it, in a design that otherwise holds back.
- The kind colours on Ideas, especially the magenta "Show".

**Committee check**
Under the patches is the cleanest base here: titles with counts, rules, tables, leader dots. The "Patched" part shows. The hop-arcs, three CRT monitors, the wire from list to monitor, the per-kind colours and the footer legend have each been added to an editorial design that did not need them. Home is where it piles up most: the stat column, arcs, list and monitor all answer one question.

**What I'd do**
1. On Home, keep the stat column and the Coming up list. Remove the hop-arc track and the wire. Keep one monitor, the Next up, or none.
2. Remove the radar from Ideas. The drive-time column already is the radar.
3. Remove the hop-arcs above the Plans month.
4. Replace the Status gauge with the leader-dot lines it already has. If one CRT element must stay on Status, make it the `$0.00` figure alone, lit.
5. Set the kind column in grey with its icon. Let green alone carry meaning in the tables.
6. Drop the footer legend.

**Verdict**
6/10. The best typography and tables in the group, with too many patches on top. Take four things away and it would score higher than anything here.
