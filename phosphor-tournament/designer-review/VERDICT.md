# Verdict

I read the four reviews. Then I looked for myself at the home and lists cards of the seven designs marked 7 or more, at the phone card of the two front runners, at the home cards of three others (Desk Terminal Joined and Even Dock, Waypoint, Quote Board), and at today's page.

## 1. The short answer

Yes, mostly it is a camel. But a camel built from good horses. Nearly every design has one clear idea and then three or four extras bolted on. The extras came from different rounds and nobody took them off. They are always the same kinds of thing: an instrument parked on a page that did not need one, a colour for every kind of thing, a numbered key, a legend, a status footer repeated on every page. The design closest to one idea done well is **Lit Manual, Dialled** (`x14b-manual-cross`). Its idea is simple: titles that carry their count, and fine rails with stops that draw time and order. The idea reaches its peak on Home, where the lit stem of the "next plan" dial drops straight down into the Coming up rail, with NOW at the top and a stop for each date. That is the owner's brief in one stroke. Its faults are all extras, so they can be taken out without touching the idea. **Trued, Yard Lit** (`x14b-trued-cross`) is a close second with the same idea. Its two best drawings (the Chat spine that ends in "your turn", and Ideas' "line from home") should be borrowed.

## 3. What went wrong

- **An instrument on every page.** At some point a monitor became the way to give a page "character", and then every page got one. Folio has a CRT on five pages. Desk Terminal has one in every left column. Dessau has a dial in every corner. An instrument earns its place only when it shows something the list beside it cannot. The Ideas radar (where things are) and the Status gauge (spend against a limit) pass that test, sometimes. Most of the others fail it: the To do bar tallies (`desk-cross`, `r13-idea-1`, Folio), Folio's "9 parts" Settings screen, the Plans dial in Manual, the Plans spike chart in Desk Terminal Joined, and the "LATE 3" To do dial in Dessau.
- **Saying it twice, or four times.** Dessau's To do: the dial says 3 late, the strip says LATE 3, and the list shows three amber rows. On Waypoint's Home, "what's next" is answered by the stat column, the hop-arcs, the list and the radar monitor. Folio's Fastext tiles repeat the section counts next to them. On Plans, Trued and Waypoint draw a track of dates over a calendar that already shows the dates.
- **Colour coding everything.** Kinds get their own hue (Sill's and Waypoint's kind words, r20-mut-2's hatched swatches, Manual's coloured kind chips). Sections get their own tab or rule colour (r20-mut-2, Dessau, Blueprint, Patch Bay's cables, Green Desk's nav). Counts change colour from page to page for no reason (Quote, Track). The result is confetti, and much of it lands on the tan, orange and pink the brief ruled out.
- **Legends.** When marks need a key they are too many. Sill's To do footer legend, Dessau's "Light · The family's · The page · Late", Waypoint's footer key, Manual's "What a lamp says", and the Ideas radar captions that explain the numbering.
- **Repeated readouts.** "Ready · OpenAI · gpt-6-luna · $2.00 a day" at the foot of every panel (all three Desk Terminals), or as a strip on Home and To do (Trued, Sill). The family does not need to know the model on the To do page.
- **Wayfinding apparatus.** Numbered keys (Grid Console's 00–05 with C and D, r20-mut-2's D1–D9), ringed section numbers and "On this page" indexes (Even Dock), P-numbers and a Contents block (Folio), sheet numbers (Blueprint). A family home page is six pages deep, not a manual.
- **Costume.** Teletext page numbers, blueprint grids, screws, wood grain, a Mac title bar, a Tron floor. Each says "look, a period reference" instead of using the period's discipline. A real CRT terminal was sparse because it had to be.
- **Glow as volume.** r20-mut-2's 150px scanlined "MIND?", Grid Console's lit rails, the glowing full-width add bars on Track Diagram. Light that does not point at anything.

## 4. What is genuinely good

- **The dial-to-rail wire** on Manual's Home. The lit stem leaves the dial and becomes the Coming up rail, with NOW and a stop per date. Instrument and list as one drawing. Keep it, and keep it as the only dial.
- **The NOW line across To do.** Nearly every design has it, and the best are Manual's (dates on a rail at the left, amber stops, a lit NOW node) and Trued's (one green line that crosses the due track and the table). Light used once, for the present.
- **The Chat spine ending in "your turn"** at the message box (Trued, Sill). Structure that says whose move it is.
- **Titles that carry their count.** "Things to do 4 OPEN · 3 late", "Settings 9 PARTS · 1 needs a look" (Manual), "Ideas 12" (Waypoint). The heading becomes information, and you no longer need a stat strip.
- **Settings as one numbered rail** (Manual) or one quiet list with a status dot (Trued, Sill), with the one amber stop where something needs a look. It reads from across the room.
- **One lit word in the headline.** "Hand Vera anything to plan." (Manual), "Tell Vera what to plan." (Trued), "Tell Vera. Let's plan." (Dessau). Sentence case, one word glowing.
- **Ideas as a table, with drive time as the chart.** Waypoint lights only the nearest drive time. Trued's "line from home" is a simpler radar. Folio's "2h45 MIN" numerals are beautiful.
- **Quote Board's countdown ruler.** "1 DAY", then ticks for the month's plans along a NOW to +4W track. If one CRT screen survives on Home, this is what it should show.
- **The Status gauge** (Even Dock, Desk Terminal Wired): fine ticks, a needle and a hatched limit. Good drawing. Whether it belongs at all is a separate question (see below).
- **Amber kept for late and "needs a look".** Where designs held that (Manual, Trued's Settings, r20-mut-2's single amber rule on Sign-in and security), amber works like a voice raised once.

## 5. If I were art-directing this

**Start from** Lit Manual, Dialled (`x14b-manual-cross`). It has the best hierarchy, the least colour and the strongest single gesture. Bring in two drawings from Trued: the Chat spine ending in "your turn", and the "line from home" on Ideas (on a stepped or log scale so the near places spread out).

**Strip out:**
1. The Plans dial. The calendar with today ruled in light is enough.
2. The round spend lamp on Status. Put "$0.00 of $2.00 today" in the title count, and keep FIG. 1 only if it is redrawn in the site's own grey and green.
3. The bezelled radar on Ideas. Use the line from home instead, drawn on the page, not in a box.
4. The Home figure strip (NEXT, PLANS, TO DO, LATE, IDEAS). The dial, the rail and the titles already say all of it.
5. The coloured kind chips and squares. Kinds in grey with their line icon.
6. The lamp legend, and the "Ready · model" block in the footer.
7. The slate selected state in the nav. Use a lit underline.

**The rules, as few as possible:**
1. **Light means now or yours to act on.** Phosphor goes on the present moment (NOW, today, the next plan), the one live action (Send, the lit word in the headline), and nothing else. Amber means late or needs a look. Everything else is grey on green-black.
2. **One drawing language: a hairline with stops.** Time, order and state are drawn as rails with rings. No second language (hop-arcs, hatching, grids, gauges) unless a rail truly cannot show it.
3. **One instrument in the whole app, and it lives on Home.** Every other page is a title, a rail and a table.
4. **Say each fact once per screen.** If the title has the count, nothing else does.
5. **No key.** If a mark needs a legend, remove marks until it doesn't.

**What it should feel like:** a well-set operating manual lit from inside. Quiet grey type on near-black, thin lines doing the structural work, and on each page one or two points of green light that tell you where now is. Squint at it and you should see the light only where something is happening. It should feel like the work of one patient person who kept removing things until what was left could not lose anything more.

## 6. A note on the panel

A committee of lenses rewards additions, because each lens can point at a new feature that answers its concern ("is it working?" gets a footer, "make it navigable" gets keys, "more CRT" gets a monitor). Nobody is rewarded for a deletion. A single designer judges the whole and can see that removing four good things makes the fifth better. A panel scores each thing it sees on its own merits, so it never sees what all of them together cost.
