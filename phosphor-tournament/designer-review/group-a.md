# Designer's review, group A

Reference: today's page (`00-current`) is a soft dark app with rounded cards, one green CRT panel on Home and one on Ideas, and orange used for warnings. Each design below is judged against that and against the owner's brief: light the phosphor only where it means something, cool companions in measured amounts, fine line art for structure, and simplicity where it works just as well.

---

## Grid Console, Keyed (`r20-mut-3`)

**The idea in one line**
A lit console laid out on a grid. Every section hangs off a thin vertical bus with a numbered key, and the whole thing sits on a perspective floor that glows at the bottom. It is several ideas, not one.

**What works**
- The To do timeline on Home and on Things to do is the best line art in the group. A vertical track with amber dots for "6 days ago", "5 days ago", "2 days ago" and a bright green "now" node. It shows how late things are without a word of explanation.
- The Plans month view is solid. Days with plans get a raised cell with a cool blue left stripe, today (Sat 3) gets an amber outline, and days outside the month are hatched. It reads at a glance.
- The Next up monitor on Home is a properly made object: bezel, scanlines, a radar, underlined phosphor links. The close crop at 100% holds up.
- On the phone the bottom tab bar, the full-width Send button and the wish cards are clean and easy to hit.

**What doesn't**
- The keys do not form a system. The nav says 00 HOME to 05 WISHES. On Home the sections then run 05 wishes, 03 coming up, 04 to do, 02 lately added: out of order on the page that should teach the code. Status is keyed "C" and Settings "D", so letters and numbers are mixed with no legend saying why.
- There is too much glow and it does not mean anything. The hero headline (WHAT'S ON YOUR MIND?), the monitor, every section rail, the floor grid and the "UP" node are all lit. When everything glows, the glow stops pointing at anything.
- The perspective floor grid with its glowing "UP" bead at the foot of every page, login and 404 included, is a Tron decoration. It draws no structure from the app.
- Status leads with a big VU-meter gauge for $0.00. The page's question ("is it working?") gets the smaller type underneath.
- Avatars are orange (S, A) and pink (M, T) squares, and Ideas uses lavender for its count. That is the brown and pink the brief warns against, plus a third accent, all on one screen.

**What's beautiful**
- The four amber dots stepping down the To do rail to one green "now" node. Bright green used once, for the present moment. That is the brief done right.
- "9 OCT FRI" on Home: a big condensed numeral with a tiny mono month and weekday stacked beside it, and a boxed "IN 6 DAYS" tag under it. A good calendar unit.
- The hatched out-of-month cells in Plans against the plain in-month cells. A quiet texture that does real work.

**What's ugly**
- The UP bead and the vanishing floor under the footer. A screensaver, not a page.
- The Chat page. A huge "Vera" on the left with a pixel avatar, a "2 REPLIES" counter, and the kids' conversations stuck under it with pink chips. Then the thread to the right, each message in a boxed row with a coloured square. It reads like a sidebar that lost its page.
- The orphan "C" above "Status" and "D" above "Settings". Single letters floating in space.
- The headline "WHAT'S ON YOUR MIND?" in grey and green condensed caps, crammed into a bordered box with the form under it, beside a second bordered box (the monitor). Two boxes fight for top-left.

**Committee check**
This one is the camel. The keyed numbers, the vertical bus, the floor grid, the gauge, the radar, the corner brackets on the hero, the card afterglow, the hatching and the timeline each arrived as an answer to something. None of them was removed when the next came. The numbering scheme in particular looks like it was asked for ("make it navigable"), half done on the nav, then partly applied on pages.

**What I'd do**
1. Remove the perspective floor and the UP node everywhere.
2. Drop the numbered keys, or make them strict: same code on the nav and the page, in nav order, digits only. I would drop them.
3. Keep one lit object per page (the Next up monitor on Home, the radar on Ideas) and let section rails be a dim line, not a glowing one.
4. Replace the Status gauge with the plain spent line and give the working/not working list the top.
5. Make avatars one cool neutral and drop the pink and orange.
6. Keep the To do timeline and the Plans month exactly as they are.

**Verdict**
5/10. Good parts (the timeline, the calendar) buried under a pile of glowing ideas that were never edited out.

## Lit Index, Ledger Rail (`r20-mut-2`)

**The idea in one line**
An editorial index: huge condensed headings, a ruled ledger under each section with a short coloured tab at the right end, and phosphor kept to one hero word and one monitor. It is one thing.

**What works**
- It has a real typographic voice. "Ideas", "Plans", "Things to do", "Settings" set in a tall condensed grotesque, black-on-dark, with a 2px rule and a coloured tab on the right. That one move runs through every page on the desktop sheet.
- Settings is the best Settings page in the group. Nine parts as a two-column index, D1 to D9, each a big condensed title with two lines of small grey under it and an arrow. Sign-in and security, the one that needs a look, gets a single amber left rule and a boxed "NEEDS A LOOK". Restrained and clear.
- Home below the fold works as a table of contents: 01 Vera, 02 Ideas 12 saved, 03 Plans 4 coming. Big word, a number, the content to the right.
- Things to do as a dotted ledger (title, dotted leader, who, a boxed "was due 6 days ago" in amber) is simple and readable. The left bracket line joining the four boxes is a nice touch of structure.
- The nav is plain text: 00 HOME to 05 WISHES on the left, A MEMORY to D SETTINGS on the right. The two series are explained by their position, so the keys make sense here.

**What doesn't**
- "MIND?" is far too big. It is about 150px of scanlined bright green, the largest thing on any screen in the app, and it says nothing. The brief says light it where it means something. This is lit for show.
- The hatched squares beside each idea (yellow for Activity, orange for Restaurant, green for Outing, blue for Trip) are four colours of diagonal stripes. On Ideas they make a column of warning tape. The orange and yellow are the off-motif warm colours.
- The phone is broken in places. On the phone restaurants page, "Everything we know" and "Map" collapse into a one-word-per-line column ("Every / thing / we / know / Map"). On phone setup-family a text column collapses to one letter per line down the page. The phone setup pages also show doubled step numbers ("1. ≡ ALL", "3. 2 PASSWORD").
- The coloured tab at the right end of each rule changes colour per section (green, purple, blue, yellow). Once it is a code it needs to mean something. Here it is just variety.
- The Plans month view is plain to a fault. Today (3) is a small amber square in a dull cell; it does not stand out.

**What's beautiful**
- The Settings index: "D8 Sign-in and security" with one thin amber rule down its left edge. One warm accent, used once, for the one thing that needs attention.
- "Plans / 4 / COMING" on Home. A big word, a big numeral and a tiny tracked label, stacked. The number does real work.
- The Chat thread: a thin vertical line with ring stops at each timestamp, ending in a bright ring at "Message Vera". The conversation is drawn as a track. It is the owner's fine line art, done well.
- The Next up monitor as a grey hardware bezel with a green screen inside. It is the only object with a bezel, so it reads as an object.

**What's ugly**
- The scanlined "MIND?" at full size (close crop). Thick horizontal bars cut through a 150px letterform. It looks like a broken JPEG, not a CRT.
- The warning-tape hatched squares in Ideas.
- "Every / thing / we / know" stacked on the phone. A shipped page cannot do that.

**Committee check**
Mostly one hand. The accretion shows at the edges: four hatch colours for idea kinds, four tab colours for sections, and the scanlined headline. These look like answers to "it needs more colour" and "it needs more CRT". The pixel avatar beside "Vera" and the corner crop marks around the hero are leftovers too.

**What I'd do**
1. Cut the hero to one size: "What's on your mind?" on one line at the size of the other page titles, with only "mind" in phosphor, no scanlines.
2. Replace the hatched squares with the kind's small line icon in one neutral colour.
3. Make every rule tab the same colour (dim green), or remove the tabs. Keep amber only for "needs a look" and "late".
4. Fix the phone: the restaurant row, the setup column, the doubled step numbers.
5. Give today in Plans a clear outlined cell.

**Verdict**
7/10. The clearest single idea of the four, held together by type, let down by one shouting word and a phone build that is not finished.

## Desk Terminal, Wired (`x14b-desk-cross`)

**The idea in one line**
Every page is a desk: a lit instrument on the left (a radar, a gauge, a dial, a bar readout) that sums up the page, and the work on the right. It is one idea, applied strictly.

**What works**
- The idea is clear and it holds on every page. Home has the Next up monitor with the message box inside it. Ideas has the radar. Status has the spend gauge. Plans has a "next in 1 day" dial. To do has a phosphor bar chart (Late, This week, Later, No date). You always know where the summary is.
- The instruments are the best line art in the group. The Ideas radar labels each idea by its number on rings for 15 min and 2 h by road, with "home" at the centre. That is a real chart, not decoration.
- The right side is quiet and dense: Coming up as a list with big numeral dates, times in a column, "in 6 days" right-aligned. The To do table with a "NOW" line across it.
- Finish setting up has become one slim strip with three numbered rings and "Next: choose your own password". Much better than three cards.
- Status's left panel ("Who answers 3 of 4 on", "Keys 1 of 4 set" with little dot rows) is a real index of the page.

**What doesn't**
- An instrument on every page becomes a formula. The Plans dial ("NEXT IN 1 DAY then 3 more") repeats what the calendar beside it already shows. The To do bar chart counts four rows you can see next to it.
- The same "Ready · OpenAI · gpt-6-luna · $2.00 a day · Status →" footer sits at the bottom of every left panel. On Chat, Plans and To do it is noise.
- The pale tan rules across the top of the right-hand panels (Coming up, Status's three sections, To do) are the warm off-motif colour, and they look like a highlight with no meaning.
- Type is small. The right panels are mostly 13px with grey labels, and the Ideas filter grid on the left is twelve small buttons in a 3-column grid. It feels busy.
- The chat page splits into a narrow Conversations panel and a thread. Fine, but the left monitor there just says "Vera / Listening" in a big box.

**What's beautiful**
- The Ideas radar: numbered blips clustered by drive time, "2 h by road" on the outer ring, "N" with a small arrow. Line art that carries data.
- The To do "NOW" line: a bright green track running across the table between "Thu 1 Oct" and "No date", with a node on the date rail. The present moment drawn once.
- "4 Oct Sun / Oaks Park roller rink / 13:00 / tomorrow" on Home: a big numeral, a tiny stacked date, dotted leaders to the time. Plain and exact.
- The Status gauge: the needle resting at 0, fine ticks along the arc, and the 2.00 limit marked by a short hatched band. A properly drawn meter, all in one green.

**What's ugly**
- The tan top rules. A cream line on green-black looks like a printing mistake.
- The "Give Vera something to plan. ▮" headline inside the monitor in bright white type. It mixes app type into the CRT screen, so the screen stops reading as a screen.
- The Ideas filter as a wall of twelve boxed chips stacked under the radar.

**Committee check**
The idea is single, but the instruments were added page by page until every page had one, whether it needed one or not. The repeated Ready footer, the tan rules and the Home strip "Wishes Maya 0 · Theo 0 open" crammed under the box are later additions. The left column has become the place where things are put.

**What I'd do**
1. Keep instruments only where they add something the list cannot show: the radar on Ideas, the gauge on Status, the Next up monitor on Home. Remove the Plans dial and the To do bar chart, and give those pages their full width.
2. Delete the Ready footer from every panel but Status.
3. Change the tan rules to dim green or plain grey hairlines.
4. Take the headline out of the monitor: put the screen on top, the box under it in app type.
5. Replace the Ideas filter wall with three selects or a single row of chips.

**Verdict**
7/10. A strong, consistent idea with the best instruments in the group, which needs a third of its instruments taken away.

## Quote Board, Lit (`x14b-quote-cross`)

**The idea in one line**
A quote board: every page title carries a big light numeral (Status 6, Ideas 12, Things to do 4, Settings 9), Home is a row of columns each with its count, and one monitor shows the next plan as a countdown. It is one idea, close to today's page.

**What works**
- It is the calmest of the four and the nearest to today. Rounded panels, normal-size headings, one lit monitor.
- The Next up monitor is the best one in the group. "Oaks Park roller rink", a huge "1 DAY" countdown at the right, "THEN Fri 9 Mount St. Helens day trip", and a ruler along the bottom with 4, 9, 17, 24 marked at NOW, +1W, +2W, +3W, +4W. The month's plans drawn as ticks on a track. That is exactly the fine line art the owner likes.
- Home's three columns (Coming up 4 plans, To do 4 open, Lately added 12 ideas) under one rule, each with a light numeral at the right, is a clear and compact summary.
- The bottom strip on Home ("Wishes Maya 0 Theo 0 · Memory · Family 4 people · Status ● Answering · Settings 3 to finish") puts everything else in one line.
- Things to do is one quiet table with an amber left rule on the late rows and a small "NOW" line under them.
- Ideas as a table (#, Kind, Idea, From home, For) is easy to scan.

**What doesn't**
- The numerals are coloured at random: 4 plans in cyan, 4 open in white, 12 ideas in lavender, 3 setup in orange, 6 Status in green, 9 Settings in grey. A count is a count; the colour change suggests a meaning that is not there.
- Active buttons and tabs are filled with a pale steel blue with a tiny green dot in the corner (the Home nav item, "All ideas", "All 12", "Open 4", "Month"). Steel blue is the loudest colour on the page and it is not phosphor. It looks like a different app's selected state.
- Ideas has two rows of chips (All ideas, Restaurants, Filter, Save a thought; then All 12, Activity 2, Day trip 1, Event 1 and so on). Too many controls before the list.
- The Status page has two money displays: the $0.00 monitor and "$1.26 in 30 days, 6 calls" with an "Answering the family … 6 calls $1.26" bar beside it. Plus the "Answers 3 of 4 / Keys 1 of 4 / Links 2 of 5" summary, then the same three sections again in full below. The page says things twice.
- "Keyboard" in the footer of every page. Nobody asked the family about a keyboard.

**What's beautiful**
- The countdown monitor's week ruler: five tick marks, the plan dates set above them in small mono, NOW at the left. Simple, true, lit.
- "Status 6 working · 7 off": a large green 6 with the small grey "7 off" under a hollow dot. The bright number is the good news; the bad news is dim. Light means something.
- Home's "Lately added" with the newest idea larger ("Oaks Park roller rink, for Maya, Theo") and the rest as one-line rows.

**What's ugly**
- The steel-blue active pills with the green corner dot. Every page has two or three.
- The rainbow of numerals across the Home columns: cyan 4, white 4, lavender 12, orange 3.
- Two rows of filter chips on Ideas sitting on a lavender rule.

**Committee check**
The base is calm, but the counts were given colours by section, the active state was given a separate colour, and Status was given both an instrument and a summary bar and a dot index. The "Keyboard" link and the corner dots on active buttons look like features a judge asked for once. Less accretion than the Grid Console, more than it needs.

**What I'd do**
1. Make every big numeral the same colour (light grey), with phosphor only for the one that is good news now (Status's working count). Amber only for late and needs a look.
2. Replace the steel-blue fill on active controls with a dim green outline or an underline.
3. Cut Ideas to one row of controls: the kind as a select or a single chip row, the rest folded behind Filter.
4. On Status keep the monitor or the summary bar, not both, and drop the "3 of 4" index that repeats the sections below.
5. Remove the Keyboard link.

**Verdict**
6/10. A calm, well-mannered page with the best single instrument, spoiled by colours used as decoration rather than meaning.
