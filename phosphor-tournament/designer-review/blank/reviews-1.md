# Reviews 1: D01 to D05

## D01
**What it is** A dark green-phosphor console of rounded cards and small tables, with one glowing CRT monitor per page: an oscilloscope of plans on Home, a radar on Ideas, a deadline histogram on To do and a spend readout on Status.

**What works**
- The monitors are the idea and they are rendered well: bezel, scanlines, a power LED and a caption plate ("PLANS · 1 WK/DIV", "DUE · 1 WK/DIV", "PLACES · 15 MIN, 45 MIN, 2 H"). Each one carries a real fact, not decoration.
- One top bar on every page, icon plus word, with the page's own tab lit, and a matching bottom tab bar on the phone.
- The Home ask box is clear: big "What's on your mind?" with "mind" in green, the From select, mic and a bright green Send that is the only filled button on the page.
- Focus is unmistakable: a thick green double ring on fields, the select, "Skip to the page", the mic and the starter chips.
- Squinted, Home reads as hero, amber setup strip, then two lists.

**What doesn't**
- The active state is pale blue (the lit "Status", "Plans" and "Settings" tabs, "All ideas", "Month", the highlighted option in the open select). In a green phosphor world it reads as a different product.
- Below the fold Home becomes six near-identical small tables (Coming up, To do, Lately added, The kids' wishes, Something to eat) with tiny grey type; nothing leads.
- Status is cramped: three narrow "LAMP / WHAT / STATE" tables with wrapped two-line values ("Claude (Anthropic)", "nowhere else to go: one key, or the fallback is switched off"), and then a lot of dead space before "What it has cost".
- The oscilloscope's bars have no labels, so "3 more on the scope" asks the reader to decode a chart to find what the list below already says.
- Kind chips in yellow, orange, grey and blue (Activity, Restaurant, Outing, Trip) add a fourth and fifth colour that compete with the green and amber.
- On the phone the monitor's caption plate runs under the tab bar.

**What's beautiful** Home, the "Next up" monitor: the "NEXT UP" inverse tag, the underlined phosphor title "Oaks Park roller rink", the vertical sweep line and grid, all inside a convincing bezel.

**What's ugly** Status, the "Who answers / Keys / Connected to" tables: three cramped grids with a LAMP column, wrapped labels and uneven heights side by side.

**What I'd do**
1. Make the active state green (a lit underline or green-tinted fill), including the select's highlighted option.
2. Give Home below the fold a lead: one larger block for what is next, the rest as lighter lists, fewer table headers.
3. Turn Status's three tables into one list of lamps with full-width rows so nothing wraps.
4. Label the oscilloscope's blips, or let the link be the list.
5. Calm the kind colours to one tint with the icon doing the work.
6. Keep the monitor's plate above the phone tab bar.

**Verdict** 6/10: a strong, well-made central motif sitting on a fairly ordinary and crowded admin layout with an off-palette blue.

## D02
**What it is** D01 with more instruments: an analog needle gauge for spend, an odometer for cost and calls, a ruler for the cache share and a token bar on Status, a small ruler in the Spending row of Settings, and short lit segments on the section rules.

**What works**
- Status gains a real top: the gauge monitor ("SPENT TODAY, ABOUT $0.00", "0% OF $2.00", needle resting at 0 with a hatched red-line past 2.00) and three instrument tiles ("001.26 US$ / 0006 CALLS", "SENT BACK FROM THE CACHE", "IN 24,000 / CACHE 0 / OUT 1,800") turn the cost question into something you read at a glance.
- The odometer digits in their little cells are charming and legible.
- The lit segment at the start of each section rule (under the page intro, between sections) is a quiet way to tie the pages together.
- Everything good in D01 (monitors, focus rings, ask box) is kept.

**What doesn't**
- The same pale-blue active state and select highlight as D01.
- The instruments are only on Status and one Settings row; the Spending row's ruler ("0 .50 1.00 1.50 2.00") makes that single row taller and different from the eight around it.
- The cache ruler shows a marker at 0 with no value printed, so the tile says less than its label.
- Home below the fold and the three Status tables are as crowded as D01's.

**What's beautiful** Status, the spend gauge monitor: the phosphor arc with ticks, the needle at zero, the hatched zone beyond 2.00 and "US$" under it.

**What's ugly** Settings, the Spending row: a miniature ruler squeezed into a table cell, breaking the rhythm of the list.

**What I'd do**
1. Fix the active state colour as in D01.
2. Move the Spending ruler out of the Settings table (or give every row a small state glyph so it is a system, not an exception).
3. Print the cache share on its ruler ("0%").
4. Then the same Home and Status table work as D01.

**Verdict** 6.5/10: the gauge and odometer make Status the best page of the pair, but the extras are applied in spots rather than as a system.

## D03
**What it is** A keyboard-driven terminal: a prompt breadcrumb ("> HOME ▌"), a second bar of numbered destinations ("ALT⇧ 1 Home 2 Vera ... 9 Status", "0 Settings"), huge condensed page titles each with a large coloured count, and lists rather than tables.

**What works**
- The page titles are the strongest typography in the set: "Ideas 12 IN ALL ▸ LAST ADDED SUN 27 SEP", "Things to do 4 OPEN ▸ FIRST DUE SUN 27 SEP 19:56", "Settings 1 TO LOOK AT", "October 2026 4 AHEAD". Each title answers the first question about the page.
- Home is calmer: one huge "What's on your mind?", the setup reduced to one amber line ("3 Choose your own password..." with "All 3 steps"), and a three-column digest (Coming up 3, To do 4, Lately added 12).
- Ideas as a list (kind tag over the title, a mono line "about 27 min south of home (estimate) · for Maya, Theo") reads better than a table.
- Settings as a 3x3 grid of cards with their state is easy to scan, and "Sign-in and security" is marked by an amber left edge and a "NEEDS A LOOK" tag.
- The open select is dark with a green dot on the chosen item, in palette.
- Focus rings are green and the 4th-tab focus shows the number chip and the live dot together.

**What doesn't**
- Numbers everywhere: nav numbers, "ALT⇧", "0-1 ... 0-9" on the Settings cards, numbers on the phone tab bar. It is clever once and clutter after that, and the family is not going to drive this by keyboard.
- Four count colours (blue 3, amber 4, purple 12, orange 1) with no meaning shared between pages.
- Chat drops bubbles for a transcript, then sets Vera's latest line, "Done: Silver Falls hike, Saturday 10:00 to 14:00.", in large type. The jump in size reads like a heading, not a message.
- To do does the same with "Call the dentist about Theo" at headline size above three normal rows.
- The Ideas radar sits in a tall phone-shaped monitor to the right of the list and pushes it into a narrow column.
- Status's LED strip (a row of thirteen dots, "6 working 7 off") is nice but tiny next to the big dot-matrix "$0.00".

**What's beautiful** Ideas, the title block: the big condensed "Ideas", the purple "12", and the stacked mono "IN ALL / ▸ LAST ADDED SUN 27 SEP".

**What's ugly** The second nav bar: "ALT⇧ 1 Home 2 Vera 3 Ideas ..." with a number chip before every item, duplicated again on the Settings cards and the phone tabs.

**What I'd do**
1. Drop the visible numbers and "ALT⇧" (keep the shortcuts, show them on a help key or tooltip), and fold the two header bars into one.
2. Give the counts one colour, or tie colour to meaning (amber for late, green for fine).
3. Keep every chat message one size; emphasise the latest by a marker, not by type size. Same for the first task.
4. Let the Ideas list take the width and put the radar above it or in a narrower monitor.

**Verdict** 7/10: the best titles and the cleanest Home so far, held back by a keyboard gimmick and arbitrary colour counts.

## D04
**What it is** Teletext meets a lab console: every page and row has a page number (Home 100, Vera 200, P300 Ideas, 901 Chat messages, 991 General), serif display type and old-style figures beside phosphor monitors, and a lot of information on every screen.

**What works**
- The concept is the most complete in the set and it is carried everywhere: nav ("Home 100", "Vera 200"), page heads ("P900 Status 6 OF 13 ON · $0.00 TODAY OF $2.00"), starters ("see p400 Plans"), the "Continue with Vera p200" link, Settings 991 to 999.
- The serif with old-style numerals is warm and very good for dates and counts: the calendar's "29", "1", "3", "17", the Ideas drive times "2h45 min" with the small superscript h, "27 SEP" in To do.
- Home's radar plots the plans by day ("Each mark is a plan, by its day") beside a vertical timeline with a lit "NOW", which is the same idea as the list told twice in a way that helps.
- To do uses a vertical timeline with a green "NOW" rule between overdue and undated, plus Open / Late 3 / Done / Cancelled / All tabs.
- Every page has a monitor that summarises it in words ("HOW IT STANDS 9 parts ... 1 needs a look", "OPEN, BY WHEN" with bars, "NEXT TOMORROW Oaks Park roller rink 0d 20h").
- Settings puts "Sign-in and security" first with "Needs a look" in amber.

**What doesn't**
- Too dense: Home above the fold has the ask monitor, From, Send, a checkbox, three numbered starters, a setup strip, a radar, a timeline and the start of To do and Ideas. Squinted, it is a uniform texture.
- Three type voices (serif display, mono, sans) plus the page numbers on everything; the numbers in the nav compete with the words.
- The headline "What's on for us?" is set inside the CRT with a glow, and "on" in green is soft and blurred at 100%.
- The ask field sits inside the monitor while From and Send sit outside it, so the form is split across two surfaces.
- "A select" shows two text fields and no open select, so the select's styling cannot be judged.
- On the phone, the prompt inside the monitor is a dim bordered box and "4 plans · 3 late · Vera is listening" wraps awkwardly.

**What's beautiful** Plans, the month calendar: large serif day numerals, dimmed for other months, today "3" lit in green with a green cell, events as small dark chips.

**What's ugly** Home above the fold: the left column stacks monitor, form, checkbox and three boxed numbered starters with grey "see p400 Plans" notes, with no space between ideas.

**What I'd do**
1. Cut Home in half: the ask and the next plan above the fold, the rest below; drop the starters' "see pNNN" notes.
2. Keep page numbers on page heads and Settings rows, remove them from the nav.
3. Put the whole ask form on one surface (field, From and Send together) and set the headline without the blur.
4. Show the open select so the control set is complete.
5. Let the serif do more and the mono less: two voices, not three.

**Verdict** 7.5/10: the richest and most original idea with beautiful numerals, but it needs a third of its content taken off each screen.

## D05
**What it is** A two-panel instrument desk: on every page a left console (a monitor that reads the page, then its controls and a "Ready" line) and a right panel of content under a thin gold rule, with timelines threading through the lists.

**What works**
- The monitors are the best in the set because each one shows data spatially: the Ideas radar plots every idea as a numbered blip by direction and drive time ("15 min", "2 h by road", "home" at the centre, N arrow), the Plans dial says "NEXT IN 1 DAY then 3 more" with day numbers round the rim, the Status gauge swings from 0 to 2.00 with "-- AVERAGE DAY $0.04", and To do has a bar list ("Late ▮▮▮▮ 3, This week 0, Later 0, No date 1").
- The layout is the same on every page, so you always know where the summary and the controls are.
- Home is well ordered: monitor with Next up and the headline "Give Vera something to plan." with a block cursor, the ask form below, then Coming up and To do as timelines with a lit "NOW".
- Setup becomes a slim stepper ("1 2 3 · All 3 steps · Next: choose your own password").
- Chat gets a conversation list (Family, Maya, Theo) on the left and a threaded transcript with timeline nodes on the right; the composer ends the thread with a lit node.
- Status opens with an index of its sections with numbered circles and lamp dots ("Who answers 3 of 4 on ●●●○"), and Settings has a legend (On, Waiting, Off or not set, Needs a look).
- Focus rings and the active nav underline are green; Home squinted shows two clear columns.

**What doesn't**
- The gold top rule on every right panel is a third accent beside green and amber, and on Home it sits next to an amber setup strip and amber dates.
- The "Ready · OpenAI · gpt-6-luna · $2.00 a day" footer repeats in the left panel of Home, Chat, Plans, To do and Ideas: system status on pages about family plans.
- Ideas' left panel turns into a wall of buttons: nine kind buttons, five "For" buttons, five "Status" buttons in a grid.
- The open select still highlights in pale blue.
- "Where I am" is an icon-only button with a tiny caption under it, easy to miss on desktop and unlabelled on the phone.

**What's beautiful** Ideas, the radar monitor: numbered phosphor blips round "home", rings at 15 min and 2 h by road, and beside it "10 places from home, Nearest #7 [18 MIN NW], Furthest #4 [2 H 45 MIN W]". It is a chart and a joke and useful.

**What's ugly** Ideas, the filter grid in the left panel: three blocks of equal-weight boxed buttons that bury the radar above them.

**What I'd do**
1. Swap the Ideas filter grid for compact selects or a single row of chips, keeping only Kind visible.
2. Drop the repeated "Ready" footer to Status (and Home at most).
3. Make the top rule green or neutral so amber means "late or needs a look" and nothing else.
4. Colour the select's highlight in the palette.
5. Give "Send where I am" its words back, at least on the phone.

**Verdict** 8/10: the most coherent system and the most useful monitors in the set; a few heavy control panels and a stray accent are all that stand between it and excellent.

## With the concept

The concept is close to what I had read from the images, since every design is plainly built on green phosphor monitors. So it mostly confirms my reviews. Where it changes things is the "modern, easy-to-use family app" half. That half makes density and gimmicks weigh more against a design, and it makes colours outside the phosphor palette a clearer fault.

### D01
**Does it change my reading?** No, apart from one point that sharpens. I had already read it as a phosphor console.
**What now reads differently** I had faulted the pale-blue active state. Now I see it as a break from the concept, not only an off-palette choice: the one control state you use most is the one place the phosphor character disappears.
**Revised mark** unchanged (6/10)
The monitors carry the character well, but the page around them is an ordinary admin layout, so the "old screen" lives only in a box.

### D02
**Does it change my reading?** No.
**What now reads differently** Nothing. The gauge, odometer and rulers are exactly the kind of instrument character the concept asks for, which is why I praised them. They are still applied in spots rather than as a system.
**Revised mark** unchanged (6.5/10)
It adds more of the right character to Status but does not spread it to the rest of the app.

### D03
**Does it change my reading?** Slightly.
**What now reads differently** The numbered keyboard navigation and the "> HOME" prompt are a real attempt at an old-computer feel, so they are more on-concept than I allowed. But "easy to use" for a family is exactly why I faulted them, and the concept confirms that call rather than overturning it.
**Revised mark** unchanged (7/10)
Its terminal touches fit the brief's character, but they cost the ease of use the brief puts first.

### D04
**Does it change my reading?** Yes, a little.
**What now reads differently** I had praised its teletext page numbers as the most complete concept. Against this brief, teletext is a television-text reference rather than a green phosphor computer screen. Its serif display also pulls further from that screen than any other design. Its density, already my main fault, now plainly works against "modern, easy-to-use".
**Revised mark** 7/10 (from 7.5)
It is still the most original design, but its originality partly answers a different brief, and it is the hardest of the five to use.

### D05
**Does it change my reading?** No; it confirms it.
**What now reads differently** Nothing. The monitors show family information in phosphor form (places on a radar, the next plan on a dial), while the rest of the page stays a plain, modern two-panel layout. That is the balance the concept describes. The gold rule is still a stray accent.
**Revised mark** unchanged (8/10)
It is the clearest example of a modern, usable app that has the character of an old phosphor screen without being ruled by it.
