## D21
**What it is** A full green-phosphor command deck: condensed all-caps headlines, numbered tabs (00 HOME, 01 VERA...), circuit-trace rails running down the left of every page, and CRT monitors with radar sweeps and a needle gauge set into the layout.

**What works**
- The theme is committed everywhere: the numbered nav, the rail with a glowing node at "now" on To do and Coming up, the "UP" pill in the footer, the CRT "NEXT UP" monitor. It reads as one world.
- The To do and Coming up timelines are genuinely useful: "6 days ago / 5 days ago / 2 days ago" dots before the green "now" node make lateness visible without reading.
- Big bright counters beside each title (Ideas 12, Plans 5, Things to do 4, Settings 9) give every page an instant number.
- The Status gauge ("$0.00 TODAY", needle, "LIMIT 2.00") is a clear, legible picture of spending.
- Focus states are strong: thick green double ring on the Send button and the "01 VERA" tab, a solid green "Skip to the page".

**What doesn't**
- Status's header is pushed far down the page by the gauge monitor, leaving a large empty dark block top-left; the title "Status 6 working / 7 off" floats beneath it with a stray "C" kicker. Settings likewise has a "D" kicker while other pages have "02", "03", "04": the numbering breaks.
- Idea card titles are half-bolded at random: "Pho **Oregon**", "Silver **Falls hike**", "Cannon **Beach weekend**", "Mount **St. Helens day trip**". It looks like a bug rather than emphasis.
- "The kids' wishes" shows "Maya 0 OPEN" and "Theo 0 OPEN" in pink in wide empty cards: a lot of chrome for nothing.
- The hero headline "WHAT'S ON YOUR MIND?" with "MIND" in a different, glowing green face is loud and a little crude next to the quiet input below it.
- The Chat page has a huge left column (Vera emblem, "2 REPLIES", the kids' conversations) that eats almost half the width while the conversation is squeezed.
- Background grid plus vignette plus glowing borders on every card makes Home squinted a uniform dark mush with no clear focal point except the hero.

**What's beautiful** The "NEXT UP" CRT monitor on Home: curved bezel, scanlines, the radar sweep and the underlined monospace "Oaks Park roller rink / Sunday 4 October, 13:00 [TOMORROW]", with "3 more on the radar" and its block cursor. It is the best-made object in the design.

**What's ugly** The Status page header: the empty top-left void, the lonely "C" label and the title crammed under the gauge's bottom edge.

**What I'd do**
1. Fix the random partial bolding in idea titles; use one weight.
2. Put the Status gauge beside the title rather than above it, and give Status and Settings the same numeric kickers as the other pages.
3. Narrow the Chat page's left column to a slim header strip so the conversation gets the width.
4. Tone down the hero: one typeface for the headline, green only on the word, not a second glowing face.
5. Collapse empty wish cards into a single line ("Maya, Theo: nothing open").
6. Remove the background grid or the card glows, one of the two, so the page has a hierarchy.

**Verdict** 6/10. A vivid, consistent sci-fi console with a lovely monitor and useful timelines, let down by layout slips (Status, Chat) and visible bugs in type weight and numbering.

## D22
**What it is** The app drawn as an engineering blueprint: a gridded sheet with column numbers 1-6 and row letters A-C in the margins, a title block on each page ("SHEET / NOTE / NO. FD-04"), a wide extended display face in cream, and revision clouds for the family's chat messages.

**What works**
- The conceit is clever and carried through with care: the title block on every page, sheet numbers (FD-01 Vera, FD-02 Ideas, FD-09 Status, FD-10 Settings), and a footer that is a drawing's title block (PROJECT, MOTTO, DRAWN, SHEET FD-00).
- The chat's family messages as scalloped revision clouds with a triangle revision mark holding the initial (S, A) is a genuinely witty idea and makes who-said-what obvious.
- Home's To do table with a "NOW · SAT 3 OCT" tick ruler cutting between late and upcoming tasks is clear.
- Settings as a numbered two-column schedule (01 General, 02 AI model...) with "SHEET · HOW IT STANDS" headers fits the drawing idea and reads well.
- The Plans calendar with circled weekday heads and circled week numbers down the side, and a green filled "3" for today, is tidy.
- Hatched swatches beside Lately added kinds (Activity, Restaurant, Outing, Trip) are a nice drafting touch.

**What doesn't**
- The display face is very wide; at the size used in "THINGS TO DO" and "SETTINGS" inside the title blocks it's fine, but it gives every page the same weight so nothing stands out; page titles are small, boxed and alike.
- The Chat page is mostly empty grid: the conversation sits in a narrow band with lots of dead blueprint around it.
- The To do page shows raw ISO dates ("2026-09-27 19:56") where every other page speaks in human dates.
- Ideas puts the radar monitor beside a large wall of filter chips (Kind: Any, Activity, Day trip, Event, Other, Outing, Restaurant, Seasonal, Show, Trip; Status; For); the filters dominate and the ideas themselves are pushed below the fold.
- On the phone the Send button loses its word and becomes a bare arrow, and the "Focus, 14th Tab" crop shows only a ringed empty checkbox with no context.
- The desktop nav has no Home item; only the logo goes home.
- The green CRT monitor is a different world from the blueprint: it sits on the sheet like a sticker.

**What's beautiful** The Chat page's revision clouds: dashed scalloped outlines around Sam's and Alex's messages with the triangle revision marks beside them. Also the footer title block on Home with "SHEET FD-00".

**What's ugly** The Ideas page's filter block: three rows of small outlined chips with dot radios, crowding the radar and pushing the list down.

**What I'd do**
1. Collapse Ideas' filters into three selects or a single row, and let the list start above the fold.
2. Give the conversation more width on Chat, or put the sheet's title block and the kids' conversations alongside it to use the space.
3. Human dates on To do, matching Home.
4. Restore the word "Send" on the phone.
5. Restyle the monitor as a drafted inset (outlined, blue-green, hatched) so it belongs to the blueprint, or drop the bezel.
6. Add Home to the desktop nav.

**Verdict** 7/10. The most original idea of this set, executed with real wit in the title blocks and revision clouds, though the grid leaves pages sparse and the filters and monitor fight the concept.

## D23
**What it is** A restrained, editorial dark interface in a Plex-style sans, with green kept to accents and a few precise data instruments: a four-week bar chart on a CRT, a dial for spending and for the next plan, and a wiring diagram explaining Status.

**What works**
- Calm hierarchy: big plain titles ("Status 3 of 4", "Ideas 12 KEPT", "Things to do 4 OPEN · 3 late") with a short sentence under each; the eye knows where to start on every page.
- Status is the best in this set: a "FIG. 1" wiring diagram (keys left, connected right, lit wire to gpt-6-luna) with a caption and a "What a lamp says" legend, then a three-column list where each item says Working, Off or Needs a look in words as well as colour.
- Home's Coming up timeline (numbered stops on a rail from NOW, with times and "tomorrow / in 6 days") beside a To do list with orange late lines is dense but clear.
- The "3 MORE PLANS / 4 TO DO / 3 LATE" counters under the input give an instant read.
- To do adds a margin rail with dated dots and a bright NOW line, plus a Late/No date legend; Settings is a numbered stepped list with the one needing a look ringed in orange.
- The Ideas list is a real table with Kind, Idea, Drive, Way, For, and kind filters with counts ("Activity 2", "Restaurant 2").
- Focus states are clear green rings; the phone layout keeps the full input and labelled Send.

**What doesn't**
- The "Next in 1 day" bar chart is hard to read: four thin bars of different heights numbered 1-4 over dates 3/10/17/24, with "weekend of 10 Oct free". It takes study to understand what height means.
- Plans repeats the same idea as a round dial ("Next in 1 day") beside the calendar, which costs a column and says what the calendar already shows.
- The Chat thread is plain to the point of flat: thin rule lines with arrows from the time stamp, small bubbles, much empty space on the right.
- Home's below-the-fold sections (Finish setting up, kids' wishes, Lately added) are just thin text lists; the page loses its energy after the hero.
- Calendar's out-of-month days are hatched only in corners, which looks unfinished.

**What's beautiful** The Status page's "FIG. 1" wiring diagram: a small monospace schematic with FamilyDB at the top, Keys and Connected columns, the lit green wire into "gpt-6-luna", captioned like a figure in a manual. Clever, honest and useful.

**What's ugly** The Plans page's round dial "Next in 1 day": a big glowing circle beside the calendar that adds nothing and unbalances the page.

**What I'd do**
1. Replace the four-week bar chart with something legible at a glance (a strip of four weeks with the plans marked as dots, labelled), or label the bars' meaning.
2. Drop the Plans dial; give the calendar the full width.
3. Give the Chat bubbles a bit more presence (a tinted fill for Vera, wider measure).
4. Bring a little of the Home hero's craft to Lately added and the kids' wishes.
5. Finish the out-of-month hatching across whole cells.

**Verdict** 8/10. The most usable and grown-up of these five, with a superb Status page; its instruments are sometimes decoration that needs a second look to decode.

## D24

## D25
**What it is** A dense, terminal-flavoured dashboard in Plex sans and mono: compact headers with a small connector glyph to a key number ("Status ○—● $1.26 in 30 days"), tables everywhere, and small CRT panels as side instruments (radar, gauge, a text bar chart of lateness).

**What works**

**What doesn't**

- The hero mixes a sans "Give Vera something to" with a mono "plan." and block cursor; it's a nice idea but at that size the two faces jar.
- The Plans calendar puts time before the title in tiny type ("17:00 Pho Oregon", "09:00 Mount St. Helens day trip") and truncates "Cannon Beach..."; the left column (This weekend panel, Show, Turn the month) is a lot of controls for a calendar.

## With the concept

### D21
**Does it change my reading?** Yes, a little. It is the most literal phosphor screen of the five: green glow, scanlines, block cursors and radar sweeps on every page. That is the concept's "character" half done fully. But the brief also asks for a modern, easy-to-use app, and that is the half my faults were about.
**What now reads differently** The glowing second face on "MIND" in the Home headline is clearly meant as phosphor glow, not a stray choice, so I fault it less as crude. The background grid, the vignette and a glow on every card still flatten the hierarchy, and that now counts against "easy to use" rather than only against taste. The Status header, the "C" and "D" kickers and the half-bold idea titles are faults under any concept.
**Revised mark** Unchanged (6/10).
It has the character in full, but the layout slips are exactly where it falls short of the "easy to use" half of the brief.

### D22
**Does it change my reading?** Yes. The blueprint sheet, the title blocks and the revision clouds are a different concept from a green phosphor screen. What I praised as its originality is mostly a departure from the brief.
**What now reads differently** I faulted the green CRT monitor for sitting on the blueprint "like a sticker". Seen against the concept, the monitor is the on-brief part and the blueprint is what doesn't belong. The cream wide display face and the blue-grey drafting grid also pull away from phosphor green. The revision clouds are still witty, but they are a drafting joke, not a phosphor one.
**Revised mark** 6/10 (was 7).
Well made and clever, but it answers a blueprint brief, with the phosphor character confined to one monitor.

### D23
**Does it change my reading?** Yes, in its favour. The restraint I read as "editorial" is the "modern, easy-to-use" half of the brief. The green instruments (the four-week CRT chart, the dials, the monospace FIG. 1 schematic) carry the phosphor character in measured doses.
**What now reads differently** The Plans dial and the hard-to-read bar chart still cost clarity. But I now see them as the places where the design spends its phosphor character, not as decoration for its own sake, so the fault is in how they read, not in their being there. Its plain Chat thread is where the character is thinnest.
**Revised mark** Unchanged (8/10).
It is the closest balance of modern usability and phosphor character, and the faults are legibility fixes rather than conceptual ones.

### D24
**Does it change my reading?** Somewhat. The transit line is its own idea, layered on top of the concept. The phosphor character comes through the CRT panels (Next up, the line from home, the lamp board and gauge), the green route glow and the status footer, and it stays easy to use.
**What now reads differently** The tiny green-on-green lamp labels on Status now read as a deliberate phosphor look rather than a careless one, but they still fail legibility, and the brief puts ease of use first. The line metaphor is not part of the phosphor brief, but it doesn't fight it: a glowing trace on a dark screen suits the concept.
**Revised mark** Unchanged (8/10).
It wears the phosphor character naturally and stays usable, with only crowded labels and phone overflows against it.

### D25
**Does it change my reading?** Yes. Its terminal idioms read as on-concept: the monospace "plan." with a block cursor, the "########" bar chart of late tasks, the green mono status cards ("Ready · OpenAI gpt-6-luna · $2.00 a day") and the numbered radar.
**What now reads differently** I faulted the hero for mixing a sans with a mono "plan." and cursor. With the concept, that mix is the point: a modern sans with a phosphor voice breaking through. I now count it as a strength, though the size jump is still awkward. The density reads as a nod to a terminal screen packed edge to edge, but it still works against "easy to use".
**Revised mark** Unchanged (7/10).
The concept makes its terminal details read as intended, but the crowding still weighs against the usability the brief asks for.
