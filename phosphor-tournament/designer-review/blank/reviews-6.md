# Reviews 6: D26, D27, D28, D29

## D26
**What it is**
A dark console dashboard with tall condensed grotesque headings and thin outsized numerals, where each page gets one green CRT monitor (Next up, Spent today, On the radar) set in a framed bezel.

**What works**
- The condensed display face ("What should Vera plan?", "Status 6", "October 2026 4") is confident and gives every page a clear, loud head with the count beside it.
- On Home the hero card works well: the composer on the left and the Next up monitor on the right, with its 4 / 9 / 17 / 24 timeline under NOW +1W +2W +3W +4W, put the main action and the next plan side by side.
- The three columns below (Coming up, To do, Lately added), each with a big thin numeral (4 plans, 4 open, 12 ideas), read like a well-set newspaper section.
- To do: the amber left rule on late rows and the small NOW marker between the late and undated rows make lateness visible without shouting.
- Status: the $0.00 monitor with its 0 / 0.25 / 1.00 / 2.00 scale and "avg $0.04 · limit $2.00" footer is a good instrument, and the dot meters beside Answers 3 of 4, Keys 1 of 4, Links 2 of 5 are compact.
- Focus states are strong and consistent: a thick green ring with glow on Skip to the page, Vera in the bar, the select and Send.
- The bottom tab bar on the phone is clean and the active Home tab is clear.

**What doesn't**
- Too many accent colours compete: green for actions, cyan for the plans count, lilac for the ideas count and the Ideas rule, amber for late, and a slate blue fill for the active nav tab and the active filter pills. The slate blue in particular reads as a different product from the green.
- The little green dot on the corner of every active tab and pill (Home, Status, All ideas, All 12, Month, Open) looks like an unread badge, not a selected state.
- Settings is a 3x3 grid of cards with uneven text lengths; Connections' three lines and Sign-in's "Needs a look" chip make the grid lumpy, and the rows do not line up.
- The Chat page puts "Vera 2 replies" as the heading count, which is an odd thing to count, and the thread sits in a heavy bordered box that adds a frame within a frame.
- Ideas has two rows of filter pills plus a monospace filter field ("any words · any but dropped · anyone") that is hard to read at that size.
- On the phone the suggestion buttons run off the right edge ("Remind me to") with no visible sign that they scroll.

**What's beautiful**
Home, the Next up monitor: "Oaks Park roller rink" underlined in phosphor, the big "1 DAY" countdown, and the timeline ticks NOW to +4W, all inside a rounded bezel with a small power light.

**What's ugly**
The navigation bar's active tab: a slate blue filled block with a green corner dot, which clashes with the green of the rest of the page and looks like a notification.

**What I'd do**
1. Make the active tab and active pill states green-tinted (or an underline) and drop the corner dot.
2. Cut the accent count: keep green and amber, let counts be the text colour, drop cyan and lilac.
3. Turn Settings into a list of rows with the state on the right, so uneven text does not break a grid.
4. On Chat, remove the outer box around the thread and count something useful (or nothing) beside "Vera".
5. Give the phone's suggestion row a fade or wrap it.

**Verdict**
7/10: a strong type voice and good instruments, let down by a scatter of accent colours and a nav state that fights the theme.

## D27
**What it is**
A dark, rounded, geometric-sans design built around circular dials: a clock face of dates on Home, a radar on Ideas, a gauge on Status and a ring counter on every other page, with dotted-leader statistic strips under each heading.

**What works**
- The Home headline "Tell Vera. Let's plan." in a heavy geometric sans with "plan." in glowing green is the most inviting opening of this set.
- The Home dial is a genuinely new idea: the plan dates 4, 9, 17, 24 sit round a clock face at 1w, 2w, 3w, with "Next up Oaks Park roller rink, Sun 13:00 · tomorrow, 3 more this month" in the centre.
- The statistic strips ("TODAY Sat 3 Oct · PLANS 4 · TO DO 4 · LATE 3 · IDEAS 12", "IDEAS 12 · NEAREST 18 min NW · FURTHEST 2 h 45 min W") with dotted leaders are tidy and give a calm rhythm to every page.
- Status' three numbered columns (01 Who answers, 02 Keys, 03 Connected to) with "working" / "off" in small green or grey after each name are very readable.
- To do: the vertical timeline with amber triangles on late items and a green NOW line before "Renew library cards" is clear.
- Settings as a single list with dotted leaders running to the state ("Vancouver, WA", "Needs a look" in amber) is the best Settings layout here.
- Chat bubbles with name and time in small caps above the text and a coloured top edge (tan for the family, green for Vera) are quiet and easy to follow.
- Footer legend (Light, The family's, The page, Late) explains the colour code, a thoughtful touch.

**What doesn't**
- A dial on every page head (Plans "OCT 4", To do "LATE 3 TASKS", Settings "8 of 9 IN ORDER", Chat's glyph orb) turns the idea into a stamp; on Settings and To do the ring says less than the strip right under it.
- The Plans calendar has very small event text ("Pho Oregon 17:00", "Silver Falls hike 10:00") under circled day numbers, leaving cells mostly empty and the events hard to read.
- Numbers in the strips are in a tan/beige, while headings are off-white and actions green; the tan reads slightly muddy against the green glow.
- The Ideas list leaders ("about 27 min south of home ........ for Maya, Theo") are long and faint, and the coloured kind labels in tiny caps (ACTIVITY, RESTAURANT) are hard to read.
- On the phone the Home dial shrinks to a small "4 in 1 d" ring that says little.

**What's beautiful**
Home, the Next up dial: a glowing round screen with a needle at the top pointing to the circled 4, the later dates 9, 17 and 24 orbiting, and the plan name in phosphor mono at its centre.

**What's ugly**
Plans, the month grid: day numbers in circles with the events as hairline-thin, tiny text beneath them, so the month reads as empty boxes.

**What I'd do**
1. Keep the dial for Home, Ideas and Status, where it shows data in space or on a scale; replace the rings on Plans, To do and Settings with the strip alone.
2. Make the calendar events larger chips with the time first, and reduce the circled day numbers to plain numerals.
3. Make the strip numbers the text colour (or green for good, amber for late) rather than tan.
4. Shorten the Ideas leaders and set the kind as a normal-case label.
5. On the phone, give the Home dial the full width or drop it for a Next up line.

**Verdict**
7/10: the most distinctive and friendly identity of these four, with an excellent Home dial and Settings list, but the circle motif is repeated until it means nothing and the calendar is too small to use.

## D28
**What it is**
A two-column console: a left rail holding a green CRT monitor (with the page's instrument, and on Home the headline and composer itself) and the page's controls, beside a right column of dark panels set in IBM Plex.

**What works**
- The system is consistent: every page has the monitor at the top of the left rail (Next up with radar, the US$ needle gauge, the "NEXT IN 1 DAY" clock, the TO DO bar chart, the ideas radar), the page's controls below it, and the content on the right.
- Home puts the next plan, the greeting "Give Vera something to plan." and the message box in one screen, so the first thing seen is both what is next and where to type.
- Coming up and To do on Home are vertical timelines with a NOW node; late items are amber rings, the undated one a dashed ring. Clear and elegant.
- The Status gauge (an arc from 0 to 2.00 US$ with a needle at 0, "0% OF LIMIT, AVERAGE DAY $0.04") is a beautiful instrument, and the "On this page" numbered index below it is useful on a long page.
- The Ideas radar with numbered blips (1 to 10 by drive time, rings at 15 min and 2 h by road) beside "Nearest #7 [18 MIN NW], Furthest #4 [2 H 45 MIN W]" ties the list to space.
- Chat's left rail with conversations (Family, Maya, Theo) is a sensible structure for the kids' conversations.
- To do's monitor ("Late ##########  3, This week ...... 0") is a charming text-mode chart.
- Settings is a clean list with the state in a middle column and the amber left rule on Sign-in and security.

**What doesn't**
- The Home composer is split: the text field sits inside the monitor's screen, but From, the mic and Send sit outside it below the bezel, so one form crosses a hard frame.
- The "Ready, OpenAI · gpt-6-luna, $2.00 a day" block is repeated at the foot of the left rail on every page; it becomes noise.
- Home below the fold leaves the left rail sparse (only the kids' wishes), while the right column carries everything.
- Mono and green text inside the monitors is small in places (the radar labels, "[10 MIN NW]", the Plans clock's "then 3 more").
- The blue-grey fill of active tabs (Vera, Ideas, Status, Month, Open) is a third accent beside green and amber.

**What's beautiful**
Status, the spend monitor: a needle gauge from 0 to 2.00 with a green arc, hatched end zones and "US$" under it, a real piece of instrument design.

**What's ugly**
Home, the composer: the message field inside the CRT and its From, mic and Send controls outside the bezel, which breaks one action in two.

**What I'd do**
1. Bring the whole composer under the monitor (or the whole composer inside it), so field and Send share one frame.
2. Show the Ready block once (Status, and Home) and remove it from the other pages.
3. Make the active tab green-tinted to match, and drop the blue-grey.
4. Raise the smallest monitor text a step on the radar and the Plans clock.
5. Use the left rail below the fold on Home for something worth reading (the wishes, or Lately added).

**Verdict**
8/10: the most coherent system of this set, with beautiful instruments and timelines; a split composer and a repeated status block are the main faults.

## D29
**What it is**
A close sibling of D28: the same left-rail CRT and right-column panels in IBM Plex, but with the greeting in mono inside the monitor ("What's on your mind?"), numbered sections down the right, and a monospace data column for times and states.

**What works**
- The Home monitor is the richest of the set: "VERA · SAT 3 OCT", the blinking "What's on your mind?", a rule, then NEXT UP Oaks Park roller rink, the date, [TOMORROW] and "3 plans on the radar" beside a radar sweep.
- The composer sits wholly below the monitor in its own panel (field, From, mic, Send, Send where I am, then the suggestions), which fixes the split seen in D28.
- Numbered sections on Home (1 Finish setting up, 2 Coming up, 3 To do, 4 Lately added, 5 The kids' wishes) with an "On this page" index in the rail make the long page navigable.
- Monospace for times, drive times and "was due 6 days ago" gives the tables a crisp right column.
- Status' text-mode meter "[....................] 0% OF THE $2.00 DAILY LIMIT" is witty and fits the screen.
- Settings uses the left rail as a settings menu, with the overview list on the right; Ideas puts every filter as buttons in the rail.
- Focus rings and the select are as strong as D28's.

**What doesn't**
- Home starts with Finish setting up as section 1, three long underlined sentences in amber, so the top of the right column is a to-do list for the admin rather than the family's plans.
- Ideas' filter rail is a long stack of small buttons (Any, Activity, Day trip, Event, Other, Outing, Restaurant, Seasonal, Show, Trip, then For, then Status), busy and tall.
- The Plans monitor's day histogram has a run of tiny numerals under it that cannot be read.
- Settings' left nav repeats the right list item for item, so the page says everything twice.
- Coral open circles for "no key" on Status are a fourth colour, and the "Ready" block again repeats on every page.
- The monitor greeting in mono is less inviting than a large sans headline; the page has no real head on Home.

**What's beautiful**
Home, the monitor: the mono greeting with its cursor block, the dashed rule, the NEXT UP tag and underlined plan name, and the radar sweep in the corner, composed like a real terminal screen.

**What's ugly**
Ideas, the filter rail: a grid of a dozen small grey buttons for kind, then person, then status, which crowds the radar above it and reads as a control panel rather than a filter.

**What I'd do**
1. Move Finish setting up below Coming up on Home, or fold it into one line with its steps.
2. Collapse the Ideas filters into three selects (kind, for, status) or a single row of pills.
3. On Settings, drop either the rail menu or the right list.
4. Replace the Plans histogram labels with a readable "4 · 9 · 17 · 24".
5. Use amber or grey for "no key" instead of coral, and show the Ready block once.

**Verdict**
7.5/10: a well-made refinement of the console idea with the best monitor and a whole composer, but its Home order and crowded filters make it busier than it needs to be.

## With the concept

### D26
**Does it change my reading?** Partly. The monitors were already the phosphor element; knowing the concept makes the rest of the palette look less like variety and more like drift away from it.
**What now reads differently** My fault with the extra accents (cyan for plans, lilac for ideas, slate blue for the active tab) is now sharper: a phosphor screen is near-monochrome, and those colours pull against it. The condensed headings and thin numerals, which I praised, sit outside the screens rather than inside the character, so the page is a modern dashboard with phosphor inserts, not a phosphor app.
**Revised mark** unchanged (7/10)
The monitors carry the concept well, but the colours around them dilute it.

### D27
**Does it change my reading?** Yes, a little. The dials now read as phosphor instruments (an oscilloscope, a radar, a meter), not just a circle motif.
**What now reads differently** The dial on every page head looks more deliberate, as the screen that each page owns. It still says less than the strip under it on To do and Settings, so the repetition fault softens without going away. The tan numbers and the warm geometric sans are further from a phosphor screen than I noticed: outside the dials, little of the page carries the character.
**Revised mark** unchanged (7/10)
The most inviting design and the dials fit the idea, but the phosphor stays inside the circles.

### D28
**Does it change my reading?** Yes. Putting the message field inside the Home monitor is the concept taken literally: you type into the green screen.
**What now reads differently** I called the composer split a fault. As an idea it is right: the screen is where you talk to Vera. What is still wrong is the execution: From, the mic and Send fall outside the bezel. The small mono text in the monitors is more in character than I allowed, though the smallest sizes are still too small for an easy-to-use app.
**Revised mark** unchanged (8/10)
It carries the concept furthest and most consistently; finishing the composer inside the screen would make it the clear leader.

### D29
**Does it change my reading?** Yes. The mono greeting in the monitor ("What's on your mind?" with a cursor) is the phosphor terminal prompt, and I had wrongly counted it against the design.
**What now reads differently** I faulted the mono greeting as less inviting than a large sans headline. With the concept it is the right choice, and the warmth comes from the wording. The text-mode meters ("[.......] 0%", the To do bar chart) also now read as the concept done well rather than as a joke. The busy Ideas filter rail, the setup section first on Home and the repeated Settings menu are still faults, because they work against "easy to use".
**Revised mark** 8/10 (from 7.5)
The best phosphor screen in the set, joined to a usable composer; tidying Home's order and the filters would lift it further.
