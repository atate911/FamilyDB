## D16

**What it is**
A dark, green-phosphor dashboard set in a plain grotesque, held together by a glowing wire: a round "NEXT IN 1 DAY" dial on Home drops a lit line straight down into the Coming up timeline.

**What works**
- The Home hero is one idea, carried through: the dial sits on the same vertical wire as the Coming up dates (4, 9, 17, 24 Oct), and "NOW" is a node on it. The plan list and its countdown read as one thing.
- Status has a real figure: "FIG. 1", a small wiring diagram of keys on the left, connections on the right and the lit wire running to gpt-6-luna, with a caption and a lamp legend. It explains the page rather than decorating it.
- To do uses the same timeline idea well: big day numbers (27 SEP, 28 SEP, 1 OCT) on a rail, late rows marked with an amber left edge, then a green "NOW" node, then "No date".
- Settings is a numbered list of nine parts in two columns, with the one that needs a look marked in amber (8, Sign-in and security). Easy to scan.
- Plans' month grid has diagonal hatching for days outside the month and a green line across the current week at Saturday, a quiet "you are here".
- Focus states are strong and consistent: a thick green ring with an inner dark gap on Send, the select and the skip link.
- On the phone the hero stays legible: "Hand Vera anything to plan." over a roomy input, a big green Send, and a bottom tab bar.

**What doesn't**
- The dial is skeuomorphic (bezel, gloss, tick marks, a lit "free" label) while everything else is flat lines and type; it looks borrowed from another product, and Plans repeats it at the same size beside the calendar, where it competes with the grid.
- Home above the fold is crowded: hero, input, three starters, a stats strip with five numbers, the dial and the next-plan text all fight for the first screen, and To do starts cut off at the bottom.
- Chat is thin: small message boxes hung off a long left rail, family messages right-aligned in narrow outlined boxes, and a lot of empty width. It does not feel like the main way in.
- Ideas spends a row each on "NEAREST" and "FURTHEST" with long dotted leaders and the answer pushed far right; the eye travels the whole width for two facts.
- The kind filter chips (Activity, Day trip, Event, Other...) each carry a tiny coloured square; at that size the colours do not separate the kinds.

**What's beautiful**
Home: the "NEXT IN 1 DAY" dial dropping its green wire down through the Coming up dates, ending at the "NOW" node. The best single image in the set of five.

**What's ugly**
Plans: the same glossy dial pasted beside the month grid with its caption beneath, a second copy of Home's centrepiece squeezed into a sidebar.

**What I'd do**
1. Flatten the dial to match the rest (no bezel gloss, fewer ticks) and use it once, on Home; give Plans a lighter "next" line above the grid.
2. Give Home's first screen fewer jobs: drop the stats strip or move it under the fold, so the hero and the dial breathe.
3. Make Chat a wider column with fuller message blocks and bring the rail closer to the text.
4. Fold Ideas' Nearest and Furthest into the header line next to "12 KEPT".

**Verdict**
7/10. A clear, well-made idea (the lit wire as time) that is let down by a glossy dial that does not belong and a cramped Home.

## D17

**What doesn't**

- Chat's family bubbles are a muddy brown with tan initial squares; next to the green-edged Vera bubbles they look dirty rather than warm.

**What's beautiful**

**What's ugly**
Every page header: the flat grey and purple square icon tiles beside the titles ("status", "ideas", "plans").

3. Replace the brown chat bubbles with a neutral dark surface and a thin coloured edge per person.

**Verdict**
6.5/10. Lively type and a good per-page gauge idea, undercut by off-palette tiles, muddy chat colours and wide, zigzagging lists.

## With the concept

I had already read all five as green-phosphor designs (the word is in each "What it is"), so the concept mostly confirms my reading rather than changing it. I now weigh two things more: whether the old-screen character sits in screens (glowing green type on a dark ground) rather than in borrowed props, and whether it stays out of the way of the easy-to-use part.

### D16
**Does it change my reading?** A little. The close-up labels the round dial "Next up's monitor, switched on", so it is meant as a round phosphor screen, like a radar scope, not a watch face.
**What now reads differently** I called the dial "borrowed from another product". As a round tube with green numerals it fits the concept better than I allowed. The glossy bezel and the copy repeated on Plans are still the problems, so the fault is smaller but still there. The lit wire through the Coming up dates now reads even more clearly as a phosphor trace, and I like it more for that.
**Revised mark** unchanged (7)
The dial belongs to the concept more than I thought, but the crowded Home and the second copy on Plans still cost what they cost.

### D17
**Does it change my reading?** Yes, slightly against it. Knowing the concept makes it clearer that most of this design's character is not phosphor.
**What now reads differently** The radar and the round corner gauges are the only real phosphor screens. The big rounded lowercase display face, the grey and purple icon tiles and the brown chat bubbles are a modern look with green added, not a phosphor one. I had faulted the tiles and bubbles on palette grounds; with the concept they also miss the brief. The gauges, which I praised, are now its strongest tie to the concept.
**Revised mark** unchanged (6.5)
It is easy to use and has a good gauge idea, but the old-screen character stays in a few small screens and does not run through the page.

### D18
**Does it change my reading?** Somewhat. The CRT panels (Next up, the money meter, the line from home) are the concept done literally and well, which I had already praised.
**What now reads differently** The ideas' multicoloured icon circles (yellow, red, purple, blue), which I only noted as "a lot of colour", now read as pulling against a mostly one-colour character. The heavy framing, my main fault, still matters most, because it works against the "easy to use" half.
**Revised mark** unchanged (7)
It renders the phosphor screens convincingly, but it frames them too heavily to feel easy.

### D19
**Does it change my reading?** No. It already read as the best balance of plain, usable lists with phosphor screens kept for the moments that earn them, which is what the concept asks for.
**What now reads differently** nothing; the radar wedged beside the Ideas table is still the one placement I would change.
**Revised mark** unchanged (8)
It is the clearest version of the brief: a modern family app whose glowing green screens carry the character without getting in the way.

### D20
**Does it change my reading?** A little. The Status instrument panel is the concept at its fullest, and the footer legend, a key to the timeline marks, reads like an old screen's legend strip.
**What now reads differently** The footer legend has a reason to exist once you see the concept. It is still in a place where nobody will look, so the fault stands. The condensed headline face still does not read as an old-screen voice. The green mono inside the screens does that, so the clash with the body type remains.
**Revised mark** unchanged (7.5)
The concept explains its boldest pieces but does not fix the type clash, the narrow Chat or where the legend sits.
