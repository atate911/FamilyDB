# Reviews: D06 to D10

## D06
**What it is** A dark utility app where each page puts a bezelled green-phosphor CRT monitor at its top left, with numbered cards beside it.

**What works**
- The monitor appears on every page and each one does a different job. On Home it holds Next up and the question. On Status it is a needle gauge. On Plans it is a bar chart of the month, on To do a block-bar tally ("Late ███ 3"), and on Ideas a radar with Nearest and Furthest. Repeating the device this way ties the app together.
- Home's numbered cards (1 Finish setting up, 2 Coming up, 3 To do, 4 Lately added, 5 The kids' wishes) give it a clear reading order. The NOW marker in Coming up and between the To do rows is a good small idea.
- Status has an "On this page" index with counts (3 of 4 on, 1 of 4 set, $0.00 today). It helps on a long page.
- Focus states are consistent: a thick green ring with a soft glow, on the field, Skip to the page, the Ideas tab and Send.
- On the phone the monitor holds the question and textarea, and From, mic and Send sit underneath. The six-item bottom bar is clean.

**What doesn't**
- On Home the monitor is crowded. Next up, a radar, the big "What's on your mind?" and the textarea are all inside one bezel. The From row, Send and the starters sit outside it, so one form is split in two.
- The Plans monitor's bar chart has an axis row of tiny run-together numbers ("3 4 5 6 ... 24"). It can't be read at the size shown.
- The Settings list only fills about the left two thirds of its column and leaves dead space on the right. The Ready strip above it is a different width.
- The "Ready · OpenAI · gpt-6-luna $2.00 a day" footer is repeated under the monitor on Home, Ideas, Plans, To do and Status, which adds noise.
- In the Plans month view, the plans are small grey chips that are hard to tell apart from the empty cells.

**What's beautiful** The Status monitor: "$0.00 TODAY" in pixel type over a curved needle gauge marked 1.00, 2.00 and US$, with the hatched red zone past the limit. It reads like a real instrument.

**What's ugly** The Plans monitor's bar chart. Thin spikes over an illegible strip of numbers, so the device looks like decoration rather than information.

**What I'd do**
1. Keep Home's whole form in one place. Either move the textarea out of the monitor or bring From and Send inside it, and take the radar off that monitor so the question has room.
2. Drop the Plans bar chart, or redraw it with a few labelled ticks (week starts only).
3. Show the Ready footer once, on Home or in the bar, not under every monitor.
4. Let the Settings list span its column, and give month-view plans a coloured edge so they stand out from the grid.

**Verdict** 7/10. A coherent and characterful instrument-panel system, held back by crowded monitors and a few unreadable charts.

## D07
**What it is** A framed dark dashboard with condensed display headings, where each page's CRT is a timeline strip that charts its data along the time axis.

**What works**
- The timeline strips are the best idea here. On To do, "3 LATE" sits with ticks at 27, 28 and 1 before a bright Now. On Plans, the ticks run from Sep 28 to Oct 26. On Home, the plans sit against 1 wk to 4 wk. Each one shows the shape of the time at a glance.
- The Home starters are cards tagged by where they lead (PLANS, TO DO, IDEAS), with a coloured left edge. That is informative, and on the phone they become clean list rows.
- Page titles carry their counts: "Status 5 working · 1 needs a look · 7 off", "Things to do 4 open 3 late", "Plans 4 coming". The late count is in amber.
- Settings is grouped into The family, The machine and Sign-in, with "1 to look at", which is more helpful than a flat list.
- Large date numerals (4 Oct, 27 Sep) in Coming up and To do make dates easy to scan.
- On the phone, the full-width green Send is a clear primary action.

**What doesn't**
- There are frames inside frames: the page sits in a bordered panel, the title in another, the monitors in another. The edges pile up and eat width, and Chat ends up a narrow column in the middle.
- On Home and in the close crop, the Next up radar is pushed half out of the monitor's corner and clipped by the bezel. It looks like an accident, not an overlap.
- The small text on the monitors ("0% OF THE $2.00 LIMIT", "PLANS · 1 WK/DIV", "PLACES · 15M · 45M · 2H") is very low contrast.
- Status's dot rows (Chat, Digest, Lookups, Fallback and so on) are tiny dots with tiny labels. The legend under them asks the reader to decode the dots.
- In Chat, the sender header strip ("27 Sep, 19:47 SAM") sits on top of each bubble, with a letter badge floating outside. It is heavy for two lines of text.

**What's beautiful** The To do page's timeline strip: amber ticks at 27, 28 and 1 under "3 LATE", stepping down to a bright green Now line, with +1 wk and +2 wk empty to the right. It tells the whole state of the list in one glance.

**What's ugly** The Next up monitor on Home: the radar is shoved off the bottom-right corner and cut by the bezel.

**What I'd do**
1. Remove one level of framing, starting with the outer page panel, so content gets the width back and Chat is not squeezed.
2. Put the Next up radar fully inside its screen, or drop it.
3. Lift the contrast of the monitor captions and the Status dot labels. If a legend is needed to read the dots, write words instead.
4. Slim the Chat bubble headers to a name and time inline.

**Verdict** 7.5/10. The timeline strips are the smartest data design in this group, though the boxed-in framing and a clipped radar make it less polished than it should be.

## D08
**What it is** A glowing dark interface with high-contrast serif titles and accent-coloured counts, held together by vertical rails with nodes that thread through every list.

**What works**
- The serif titles with an underlined coloured count ("Ideas 12 in all" in violet, "Things to do 4" in yellow, "Status 6 working") are elegant and feel editorial.
- The rails are used sensibly. Chat hangs on a spine with nodes. To do has a dated rail ("was due 6 days ago" in amber down to a green "now"). Lately added hangs its cards on a rail.
- The Plans page's round week dial is a fresh idea: "3 plans" in the middle, the days around it, and dots for past, today and ahead.
- Finish setting up as a three-step amber track across the card reads as progress.
- The 3x3 Settings grid with Sign-in and security outlined in amber points to the one card needing attention.

**What doesn't**
- Too many ideas compete. There are serif, sans, mono and a pixel font (the glowing "mind?"). Rails, nodes, glows and dotted pills all appear on the same page.
- On Ideas every filter pill carries its own status dot. The result is rows of dots that mean "on/off" and look like noise.
- On desktop, Memory, Family, Status and Settings are icons only. A family won't know what a heart and a pulse mean.
- To do's "Add something to do" is a full-width glowing green slab, by far the loudest thing on the page, louder than the list.
- On Status, the title sits inside a large empty bracket outline (clear in the close crop). It is a lot of frame for one line of text.
- On the phone, "who is asking?" wraps onto two lines, and the textarea shows its own scrollbar arrows inside the box.

**What's beautiful** The Plans page's week dial: a thin circle with the days around it, a big serif "3 PLANS" at the centre and green dots on today and the planned days.

**What's ugly** The Ideas filter block: four rows of pills (Kind, For, Status), each with a little dot, packed into a box. It is the busiest area in the set.

**What I'd do**
1. Cut to three type families: drop the pixel "mind?" or keep it for the monitors only.
2. Remove the dots from the filter pills. Selected-state colour is enough.
3. Put words beside the right-hand nav icons on desktop.
4. Shrink To do's add button to a normal button by the title.
5. Fix the phone's who-is-asking select width and hide the textarea's inner scrollbar.

**Verdict** 6.5/10. Lovely serif headings and a clever week dial, but it piles on too many devices and typefaces to feel calm.

## D09
**What it is** A brutalist, editorial treatment: huge condensed uppercase type, a numbered nav (00 HOME ... D SETTINGS) and colour-coded rule bars marking each section.

**What works**
- It has the strongest identity in this group. The scanlined green "MIND?" filling half the Home hero is unmistakable.
- Each section has a colour (green Vera, violet Ideas, blue Plans, amber To do), shown as a short thick bar on the end of its rule. It carries across Home's below-fold index and the page titles.
- Home below the fold is an index: "01 Vera", "02 Ideas 12 saved", "03 Plans 4 coming", each with its content to the right. It is clear and confident.
- On Ideas, the condensed titles with hatched coloured squares for each kind (amber Activity, green Outing, blue Trip) make a handsome list.
- On To do, dotted leaders run from each title to its person, and the late items have amber outlined badges.
- Settings as a numbered index (D1 General ... D9 What has changed) is a strong typographic grid, and the amber edge marks Sign-in and security.

**What doesn't**
- The hero eats the screen. On the phone, "WHAT'S ON YOUR MIND?" takes most of the first view, and on desktop it pushes Finish setting up to the very bottom.
- The question field is just an underline with an italic grey placeholder. For the main thing to do on the page, it is the weakest affordance in the design.
- The nav's "A MEMORY B FAMILY C STATUS D SETTINGS" letters, and the matching tiny "c" and "D" above titles, read as code more than help.
- The Settings titles are huge for one-line answers, so the page runs long and "What has changed" falls off the end.
- The small monitors (Home's Next up, Status's spend) look like stickers in a corner rather than part of the grid.

**What's beautiful** The Ideas list: "#1 ACTIVITY" in small amber caps above "Oaks Park roller rink" in heavy condensed type, beside a yellow hatched square. It looks like a magazine contents page.

**What's ugly** Home's message field: an italic grey placeholder over a thin rule, with a small From select and Send below. It is the least visible form on the most important page.

**What I'd do**
1. Make the question field a real, bordered box with readable placeholder text, and give it as much weight as the headline.
2. Cut the hero type by about a third, especially on the phone, so the form and Next up show in the first view.
3. Drop the letter and number codes from the nav, or keep the numbers only.
4. Reduce the Settings titles a step so the nine parts fit in a screen or two.
5. Set the monitors into the grid with the same rule-and-bar treatment as the sections.

**Verdict** 7/10. The most striking and memorable design here, but its poster-scale type and underline field put style ahead of the one thing the page asks the family to do.

## D10
**What it is** A refined, quiet dark dashboard in a plain sans and mono, with headline counts, a stats strip and thin timeline threads tying plans and tasks together.

**What works**
- Home's hero puts the question beside Next up, with a ruled stats strip underneath (Today Sat 3 Oct · Plans 4 · To do 4 · Late 3 · Ideas 12). It is the clearest summary in the group, and it works as a 2x2 grid on the phone.
- Setting up is a single slim amber bar ("Finish setting up 3 steps left: Choose your own password ...") instead of a big card, which gives the space back to content.
- In Coming up, the arcs hop between dated nodes (4, 9, 17, 24) on a Now to 4 wk scale. It is a light, legible picture of spacing.
- The Status gauge reads $0, $1 and $2, has a faint needle for the 30-day average, and is captioned in words ("Spent today, against the $2.00 limit"). Below it, the three columns of rails light up for what is working.
- Each Settings card carries a state line (Set up, Partly set up, Off, Needs a look), so the grid doubles as a checklist.
- Late tasks have an amber left bar and "▲ Was due 6 days ago" with the date under it. The Whose filter shows lates per person.
- The footer has a legend (lit, yours, the page, needs a look) that explains the marks.

**What doesn't**
- In Chat, horizontal connector lines run from the left rail across the full width to each bubble. They read as rules cutting through the conversation.
- In the Plans month view, a dashed thread with tick marks runs across each week row. It is pretty, but the meaning isn't clear without the legend.
- The kids' wishes on Home are squeezed into one line ("Maya 0 · Theo 0 · All") under a full section heading. That is under-designed next to the others.
- The Ideas Kind filters are tiny checkbox squares in low-contrast grey text. They are small targets.
- Overall it is the safest of the five. Apart from the monitor, little is memorable.

**What's beautiful** Home's stats strip under the hero, from "Today ● Sat 3 Oct" to "Ideas 12". Each cell has a dotted leader to a right-aligned number, with the Late figure and its ▲ in amber.

**What's ugly** The Chat thread: long horizontal hairlines from the S and A nodes across the page to the bubbles, cutting the conversation into stripes.

**What I'd do**
1. In Chat, shorten the connectors to a short stub from the rail, or drop them and let the nodes do the work.
2. Give the kids' wishes a proper small list (name, open count, newest wish), or fold them into the stats strip.
3. Make the Ideas Kind filters real chips with bigger hit areas and brighter text.
4. Label the Plans month thread ("Next" is already marked; mark the others) or simplify it to the highlighted next plan only.
5. Allow one bolder moment, such as a larger Coming up timeline, to give it a signature.

**Verdict** 8.5/10. The most usable and best-resolved design of the five, informative without noise, though a little reserved.
