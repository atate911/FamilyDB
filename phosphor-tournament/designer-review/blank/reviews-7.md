## D30

**What it is**
A dark "transit map" treatment where every page hangs off a thin line with stations: plans on a timeline, the chat on a central spine, ideas on a "line from home" strip, and a green CRT monitor for the next plan.

**What works**
- The one metaphor runs through everything and explains the data. On Home, the plans sit above a "now · 4 Oct · 9 Oct · 17 Oct · 24 Oct" axis. On To do, a green "Now" line cuts the list between the three late items and "Renew library cards". On Ideas, "The line from home" plots the ten places by drive time (15 min to 3 h).
- Chat is the strongest page. Family messages sit right and Vera's sit left of a central spine with the times on it, and it ends in a "your turn" node feeding straight into the message box.
- The hero reads at once: "Tell Vera what to **plan.**" in a big grotesque, with only the last word glowing green. The squinted Home keeps the headline and the monitor as the two clear masses.
- The Status gauge ("SPENT TODAY, ABOUT $0.00" with a needle dial, "average day $0.04 · 0% of $2.00") is a good instrument. The lamp row beside it (Who answers / Keys / Connected) packs a lot in readably.
- Focus states are thick, unmistakable green rings: Skip to the page, the Vera tab, the "who is asking?" select and Send. The ticked box and the open select are clean.
- On the phone, Home gets a one-line ticker ("NEXT Oaks Park roller rink · Sun 13:00") instead of the full monitor, which is a sensible cut.

**What doesn't**
- The rails go everywhere, even where nothing is going on: section headings end in a bracket "|" at the far right ("Around the house", "Find", "Who answers", "Keys"), and "Or start with" has connector lines. They come across as decoration, not structure.
- Plans repeats itself: a day-number strip with nodes (3, 4, 9, 17, 24) sits right above a month grid that shows the same plans.
- Secondary text (the "usually lands in Plans" hints, "Google not connected…", the dates under the axis) is small and low in contrast against the near-black.
- On Home, the "Coming up" plan cards and the monitor are crowded into one band, and the "4 coming · next tomorrow 13:00" line is cramped against the heading.
- The Home below-the-fold "Around the house" line strings Wishes, Memory, Family and Settings onto a track, but they are not a sequence, so the metaphor stops meaning anything there.

**What's beautiful**
The Chat page's central spine: timestamps sitting on the line, little Vera glyph nodes at her messages, and the line ending in the green "your turn" node that drops into the message box.

**What's ugly**
The orphan bracket terminators ("—|") at the right end of the section rules on Home below the fold, Ideas ("Find") and Status. They look like stray glyphs.

**What I'd do**
1. Keep the line only where it carries time or distance (Chat, Home's plan axis, To do's Now, Ideas' line from home). Drop the end brackets and the connector rails on "Or start with" and "Around the house".
2. Remove the day strip above the Plans month grid, or make it the only view on a narrow screen.
3. Lift the contrast of the hint and meta text by a step.
4. Give the Home "Coming up" band more room. Put the monitor above the cards on medium widths instead of beside them.

**Verdict**
7/10. A real idea, carried with discipline on Chat and To do, but over-applied until the rails turn into ornament.

## D31

**What it is**
A sober two-column dashboard: a label column on the left (section name plus a monospaced count), rows of content on the right, and the green CRT radar monitor as the one show-piece.

**What works**
- The structure is very legible. Every Home section ("Finish setting up", "Coming up", "To do", "Lately added", "The kids' wishes") has its title and summary ("3 more / next in 6 days") on the left and its rows on the right, so scanning down the left edge tells you the whole page.
- Home's "Finish setting up" block, with numbered amber circles 1 to 3 and one sentence each, is clear and calm.
- The Home monitor is well made: an inset CRT with "NEXT UP" in a reversed tag, an underlined "Oaks Park roller rink", "[TOMORROW]", a radar sweep, and "> 3 more on the radar" with a block cursor.
- Ideas gets a proper sidebar: a "12 IDEAS" count, All ideas and Restaurants, then the kinds, each with its own coloured icon. The list rows carry drive time, compass direction and "#N" in mono columns.
- The Status rows ("Who answers", "Keys", "Connected to") pair green or red lamps with right-aligned values. The red hollow ring for "no key" reads as off without shouting.
- The phone version stacks the box, the three starter chips and the full monitor sensibly, and the focus rings are clear.

**What doesn't**
- The selected state is a pale steel-blue fill: the bar's active tab, "All ideas", "Month", "Open". It belongs to no other colour on the page, it fights the green, and the white label on it is low in contrast.
- On Settings, the summaries are pushed to the far right of very wide rows with dotted leaders between, so the eye has to travel a long way from "General" to "Vancouver, WA".
- The Plans calendar chips (a light-blue block with a left bar) are heavy for what they hold and repeat the odd blue.
- Plenty of meta is set in tiny monospace ("3 more", "12 ideas", "SAT 3 OCT"), which is hard to read at this size.
- The Status cost section reuses a smaller CRT with a "[.....] 0%" text bar. It sits awkwardly beside a plain sentence and loses against the Home monitor.

**What's beautiful**
The Home "NEXT UP" monitor at 100%: the rounded bezel, the scanlined screen, the radar with its range rings, and the blinking-cursor link.

**What's ugly**
The pale steel-blue selected fill, worst on Ideas' "All ideas" sidebar item and the bar's active tab, where it looks like a disabled default control.

**What I'd do**
1. Replace the blue selected state with the design's own green: a dim green fill or a green edge with a bright label.
2. On Settings, bring each summary next to its label, or cap the row width.
3. Raise the size of the monospace meta, or set it in the sans and keep mono for figures only.
4. Make the calendar chips lighter: a text line with a coloured dot or edge, not a filled block.

**Verdict**
6.5/10. Orderly and easy to use, with one lovely monitor, but the stray blue and the small mono make it feel unfinished.

## D32

**What it is**
A heavy industrial "control console": a wood-grain bar, riveted panels with screw heads, condensed all-caps titles, brass cables, and big CRT screens.

**What works**
- It has the most character of the four. The riveted panels, the wood bar and footer, and the brass cable linking Vera's box to the Next up monitor on Home make a consistent physical object.
- The Home "Coming up" date blocks (a green "9" with "OCT" and "FRI" in a small dark tile) are nice to read.
- The "Lately added" cards on Home and Ideas have coloured top edges and kind labels (ACTIVITY in amber, RESTAURANT in orange, OUTING in green, TRIP in blue), which sorts ideas by colour at a glance.
- Settings as a 3x3 grid of panels, each with a lamp on the right edge and "Sign-in and security" in amber with a "NEEDS A LOOK" tag, makes the one problem stand out.
- The Status page in three panels (Who answers, Keys, Connected to) with lamps is clear. The cost CRT ("$0.00" big, "[....] 0%", "OF THE $2.00 DAILY LIMIT") suits the theme.
- "Send where I am" is a real toggle switch rather than a checkbox.

**What doesn't**
- Home below the fold is not rendered, so a large part of the main page cannot be judged.
- It is decoration first. Screws sit at the corners of every panel, at both ends of the bar, and even in each Plans calendar cell (a small ring at the top right of every day), which turns the month into visual noise.
- The title underline "cables", a curved rule with terminals under each page title, change colour per page (blue on Plans, purple on Ideas, yellow on To do, teal on Status and Settings) with no reason the reader can see.
- To do cards show raw timestamps ("Due 2026-09-27 19:56") and "No active reminder." in place of the human "was due 6 days ago", and the two-column card grid wastes space for four one-line tasks.
- On the phone, the Next up monitor with its full radar takes most of the screen below the box.
- In the "Focus, 14th Tab" crop, the toggle shows no visible focus ring.
- The tracked uppercase in the bar (VERA, IDEAS, PLANS) and the panel heads is tiring at small sizes.

**What's beautiful**
The Ideas "ON THE RADAR" monitor: a wide riveted bezel around a deep green screen with "10 places from home", the nearest and furthest links, and a large radar with range rings and blips.

**What's ugly**
The Plans month grid, with a screw ring in the corner of every day cell and the amber outline on "3". The calendar reads as rows of hardware, not dates.

**What I'd do**
1. Render Home below the fold, and fix the missing focus ring on the toggle.
2. Strip the screws from everything smaller than a page panel, the calendar cells above all, and keep the cable for the one Home link it makes.
3. Bring back relative dates on To do and lay the tasks out as a list.
4. Give the title cables one colour, or drop them.
5. Shrink the phone's monitor to a strip, as D30 does.

**Verdict**
6/10. The strongest mood of the set, but the hardware gets in the way of the reading, and part of Home is missing.

## D33

**What it is**
A refined version of the dashboard-with-a-monitor approach: big numerals in a left column ("4 OPEN", "12 IDEAS", "9 PARTS"), tight table rows, and CRT instruments that carry real data.

**What works**
- The instruments mean something here. The Home monitor carries a bar chart of the coming plans ("A bar is a plan · weekends shaded"), and a green line runs from it down into the "Coming up" list's "NOW" marker. That is the cleverest join between the show-piece and the content in the four designs.
- The Plans page has a countdown dial ("Next in 1 day, then 3 more") beside a clean month grid. Status has a round "$0.00 of $2.00 today" dial with proper mono tables under it (by kind of call, by model, with calls, sent, cached, written, back and US$).
- The left numeral column gives each page a quick answer: "4 open, 3 late", "12 ideas", "9 parts, 1 to look at".
- Ideas has the best filters of the set: a grid of kind facets with coloured icons, "For" names as toggles, and a table with Drive, Way, For and # columns.
- The Home setup progress strip ("3 left (1)(2)(3) Next: choose your own password →") and the "Also on" section on Home below the fold are economical.
- To do keeps its "NOW" line, and late items show "was due 6 days ago" in amber mono.
- The "line up." headline in green glow, and the phone layout with the bar-chart monitor, carry the identity well. The focus rings are clear.

**What doesn't**
- It shares D31's pale steel-blue selected state (the bar's tab, "All", "Anyone", "Open", "Month"), and it clashes with the green just as much.
- The page heads break at full size: on Ideas and Status the icon tile sits far left and the title starts far to the right, leaving a big empty gap.
- In the Home top-left crop at 100%, the "who is asking?" select sits over the bottom edge of the message box.
- The CRT chart's axis labels collide ("NOW4"), and the Status "What a lamp says" legend is cut off at the fold.
- The Status tables are set in very small mono and look like a debug readout more than something a family would read.

**What's beautiful**
Home's Next up monitor with its plan bar chart, and the green line that drops from it into the "NOW" node of the "Coming up" list.

**What's ugly**
The Ideas header at 100%: a lone lilac icon tile at the far left, then a wide gap before "Ideas" and its description, so the head looks broken.

**What I'd do**
1. Swap the steel-blue selected fill for a green one, as for D31.
2. Fix the page-head grid so the icon sits next to the title, and fix the select overlapping the Home box.
3. Space the chart's axis labels so "now" and "4" do not collide, and let the lamp legend fit.
4. Make the Status tables larger, and put the plain-language rows first.

**Verdict**
7.5/10. The most thoughtful of the four, with instruments that carry real data, held back by the off-palette blue and a few loose alignments.

## With the concept

### D30
**Does it change my reading?** Yes, a little. I had read the transit lines as the design's main idea. Under the Phosphor Interface they look like a second idea competing with the first. Only the green, glowing parts of the lines read as phosphor traces: the "now" segment on Home and To do, and the Chat "your turn" node. The grey rails and the end brackets do not.
**What now reads differently** The Ideas "line from home" strip and the Status needle gauge now read as the design's true phosphor instruments, not as one part of a map theme. The grey rails I already faulted as ornament now also look off-concept.
**Revised mark** unchanged (7/10)
It is modern and easy to use, with real phosphor moments, but the map metaphor takes attention away from the screen character the concept asks for.

### D31
**Does it change my reading?** Yes. Keeping the phosphor to the one Home monitor, inside a plain modern dashboard, now reads as a deliberate balance and not a lack of nerve. But the steel-blue selected state is now plainly outside the concept.
**What now reads differently** The small monospaced meta ("3 more", "SAT 3 OCT", "12 ideas") reads as an intended terminal accent, not a careless choice. I still find it too small. The Status cost CRT now reads as a second phosphor screen that the page does not commit to.
**Revised mark** unchanged (6.5/10)
It is the right balance in principle, but the phosphor is too thin outside one monitor, and the blue works against it.

### D32
**Does it change my reading?** Yes, for the worse. The concept asks for a modern app with the character of a phosphor screen. D32 builds the whole old machine around the screen: wood grain, rivets, brass cables, and condensed all-caps panel labels. That is hardware nostalgia, not screen character, and it is not modern.
**What now reads differently** Its Next up and On the radar monitors are still the best-made phosphor screens of the four. But the "strongest mood" I praised is mostly a mood the concept did not ask for, and the screws and wood now count as off-brief, not just noisy.
**Revised mark** 5.5/10
Superb phosphor screens set in a cabinet the brief never wanted.

### D33
**Does it change my reading?** Yes, it confirms it. D33 matches the concept most closely: the interface is a modern table-and-numeral app, and the phosphor is kept to instruments that carry real information: the plan bar chart, the countdown dial and the spending dial.
**What now reads differently** The mono tables on Status now read as an intended phosphor readout and not as a debug dump. I still want them larger and placed after the plain-language rows. The green line from the monitor into "NOW" now reads as the clearest expression of the concept in the set: the screen and the app joined.
**Revised mark** 8/10
This is the Phosphor Interface as described: a modern family app with old-screen character where it earns its place, held back only by the blue and a few alignment slips.
