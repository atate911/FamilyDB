# Reviews 3: D11 to D15

## D11

**What it is** A dark green-phosphor dashboard: condensed display headings, a big number in a left gutter for every section, and one CRT monitor ("Next up") set into Home.

**What works**
- One clear system everywhere: section label and big count on the left ("4 OPEN", "12 IDEAS", "3 ON · 1 OFF"), rows on the right. Status, To do and Settings all read the same way.
- The Home hero is well composed: Vera's tile and date, the input, three starter chips, and the Next up monitor all fit in one band.
- Status uses a green connected line for the chain of "Who answers" and hollow red rings for what is off; state is readable without words.
- The "NOW" marker in the to-do list (between the late items and "Renew library cards") and in Coming up is a good, small idea.
- Focus states are consistent: a bright green glow ring on the field, the skip link, the nav item, the chip and the checkbox.
- The phone Home keeps the hero intact and moves the nav to a six-item bottom bar.

**What doesn't**
- Ideas: the tall "On the radar" CRT in the left column clips its own text at the left edge ("chest #4 Cannon ... h weekend"), and sits under the kind grid where it reads as an afterthought.
- Late dates ("was due 6 days ago") are in orange monospace on every row; three of them in a row shout louder than the tasks.
- The nav item "Vera" uses an empty circle as its icon, which reads as a missing icon.
- Chat is a narrow column in a wide dark field; the page carries a lot of empty black on both sides.
- Below the hero, every section is the same grey panel with the same gutter, so Home flattens into a list of equal boxes.

**What's beautiful** Home, the Next up monitor: the curved bezel, scanlines, the radar sweep and the little bar chart of the coming weeks with "FRI 9", "17", "24" under it.

**What's ugly** Ideas, the left-column radar CRT: tall, cramped and its text cut off at the edge.

**What I'd do**
1. Move the Ideas radar out of the filter column into a space wide enough for its text, or shorten its copy so nothing clips.
2. Tone the late dates down: amber in the body face, mono only for the number.
3. Give Home a hierarchy below the hero (Coming up larger, wishes smaller) instead of equal panels.
4. Give "Vera" a real icon in the nav.
5. Widen the chat column or put something useful in the side space.

**Verdict** 6/10: a tidy, consistent dark dashboard with one charming monitor, but uniform below the fold and with a visible clipping fault on Ideas.

## D12

**What it is** A close refinement of the same phosphor dashboard, with more monospace, a blinking cursor after the logotype, and a second CRT for spending.

**What works**
- The cursor block after "FamilyDB" is a small, witty brand touch that ties the logo to the monitors.
- Ideas is better arranged than D11: a vertical "Kinds" list on the left, search and filters together, and the radar CRT moved up beside the search where its text fits.
- Status gains a "What it has cost" monitor ("SPENT TODAY, ABOUT $0.00" with a text progress bar), which makes the money visible.
- The Next up monitor drops its bar chart and is calmer and easier to read.
- To do puts search above the list, where you expect it.

**What doesn't**
- The counts are now lowercase monospace ("3 on / 1 off", "12 ideas", "3 more / next in 6 days"), which reads like log output rather than a number at a glance; the big numerals of D11 were stronger.
- Status "Who answers" is split into two columns, so the chain of who answers next is harder to follow than D11's single connected line.
- To do repeats "Edit, complete, or snooze" on every row: four times the same long link.
- Below the hero, Home is still a stack of equal grey panels.
- The Plans page is unchanged from D11 and as plain.

**What's beautiful** Ideas, the "On the radar" monitor at top right: "10 places from home", nearest and furthest as underlined links, and the sweep, sitting neatly beside the filters.

**What's ugly** Things to do, the column of "> Edit, complete, or snooze" links repeated on every row.

**What I'd do**
1. Bring back large numerals for the counts and keep mono for the labels only.
2. Put Status "Who answers" back into one vertical chain.
3. Replace the repeated row link with a short "Edit" or an icon button.
4. Do something with the Plans page (today, weekends, plans as more than small boxes).

**Verdict** 6.5/10: a few real improvements on Ideas and Status, offset by monospace counts that are harder to scan and a noisier to-do list.

## D13

**What it is** The phosphor app organised along a timeline: a glowing spine with nodes ("Now", "Next", "Later") runs down the left of every page and everything hangs off it.

**What works**
- A strong single idea that carries meaning: Home goes Now (the input), Next (the next plan), Coming up, Later (lately added); To do puts dated tiles on the spine with amber nodes for late items and a green "Now" line; Settings hangs its rows off it with "Sign-in and security" first on an amber node.
- Status has a real moving-coil gauge for spend ("$0.00 of $2.00 today", the needle, "Average day $0.04"), plus a summary line "5 working · 7 off · 1 needs a look" and a legend of what the marks mean.
- Plans is an agenda grouped by week with dated tiles, and a small CRT month calendar "on the glass beside it", with weekends shaded and pulse marks on plan days.
- Home has a right rail (To do, The kids' wishes, Something to eat) so the page uses its width.
- The Next up monitor is wide, with four weeks of pulses and "weekends shaded" written under it.
- Chat puts the times and the initials on the spine between the two sides of the conversation: clear who said what.
- The phone version replaces the icon row with a single "More" button and stacks the starter prompts full width; the spine continues on the phone.

**What doesn't**
- The spine is everywhere, including places with no time in them (the Ideas filters, the Settings list), where it becomes decoration.
- A lot of very small type: the "Vera · 3 Oct Sat" tab above the input, the mark legend on Status and Settings, the CRT calendar's numbers on Plans.
- "Finish setting up" on Home is squeezed into one line ("3 Choose your own password, then add a Telegram bot and connect Google Calendar"), losing the steps.
- Ideas packs the kinds as small chips with counts, filters, the radar and a "12 ideas" node into a busy top half before any idea appears.

**What's beautiful** Status, the spend gauge: an analogue needle on a phosphor dial, with the figures laid out as a tidy table beside it.

**What's ugly** Home, the "Finish setting up" line: a number, an orange link and grey text run together on one row with the spine node beside them.

**What I'd do**
1. Keep the spine where time is the subject (Home, Chat, Plans, To do) and drop it from Ideas and Settings.
2. Raise the floor of the small type, especially the mark legends and the CRT calendar.
3. Give Finish setting up its numbered steps back.
4. Simplify the top of Ideas: one row of kinds, then the list.

**Verdict** 7.5/10: the most coherent idea of this group, with a lovely gauge and a good Plans page, held back by a spine that is applied a little too everywhere and by tiny type.

## D14

**What it is** A polished, product-like phosphor design: large headlines with counts beside them, ledgers with dotted leaders, a hop-arc timeline of the weeks, and CRTs used only where they say something.

**What works**
- Home's hero is the clearest of the set: "Send Vera anything to plan." with the input, and beside it a ledger (Today Sat 3 Oct, Plans 4, To do 4, Late 3 in amber, Ideas 12).
- Coming up has an arc timeline hopping between plan dates over week ticks, a list with large date numerals, and a line drawn from the "Next" row to the Next up monitor: the screen is visibly tied to its row.
- Ideas is sorted "Nearest first" with the drive time as a large green number ("18 min"), and kind and status as segmented controls; this is the most useful Ideas list in the group.
- Headings carry their counts ("Ideas 12", "Status 5 working", "Things to do 4 open, 3 late"), so the number lands first.
- To do filters by who, with counts per person ("Alex 2 late", "Sam 1 late").
- Settings is a 3x3 grid of cards, each with a state ("Set up", "Partly set up", "Off", "Needs a look").
- Status has a spend gauge monitor and three columns (Who answers, Keys, Connected to) with "3 of 4 on" counts.
- A status line in the footer ("Ready · OpenAI · gpt-6-luna · $0.00 of $2.00 today") is a good permanent signal.
- The phone keeps the ledger as four tiles and adds the next plan as one line.

**What doesn't**
- Status's three columns are set in small text with small dots; it is harder to read than the rest of the site.
- The section headings with a leading rule ("— To do", "— Lately added") are quiet; the page relies on the headline counts for its structure.
- The phone starter chips run off the right edge ("We" is cut); if it scrolls, nothing says so.
- The "A select" crop shows a closed select and a text area, not an open menu, so the select's open state cannot be judged.
- The logotype is in a light monospace and looks thinner than the nav beside it.

**What's beautiful** Home, Coming up: the arc timeline over the weeks with the line running from "Next" into the Next up monitor.

**What's ugly** Status, the three columns of small text and dots under Who answers, Keys and Connected to.

**What I'd do**
1. Make Status's three columns larger, or one list with the same row style as To do.
2. Show that the phone's chips scroll (a fade at the edge) or wrap them.
3. Give the section headings a little more weight.
4. Set the logotype in the same weight as the nav.

**Verdict** 8/10: the most usable and best-composed of the group, with its CRTs and timelines used for meaning rather than decoration.

## D15

**What it is** A teletext-and-phosphor take: page numbers in a header strip (P300 Ideas, P900 Status), a serif display face with old-style numerals, and a CRT panel on nearly every page.

**What works**
- The teletext idea has wit and is followed through: each page has its strip ("P300 · FamilyDB · Ideas ... Sat 3 Oct"), and Home ends with a "Contents" index with page numbers (310 Restaurants, 700 Memory, 990 Settings).
- Home's starter prompts are a numbered list that says where each lands ("lands in Plans", "lands in To do", "lands in Ideas").
- Home's Plans section is a radar dial where each plan is a blip by its day ("Each mark is a plan, by its day"), next to the list with a "NOW" node.
- Ideas has big serif drive times ("27 min", "2h45 min") and a tall radar monitor with "10 of 12 from home".
- Plans has a "Next tomorrow" monitor with a countdown bar ("0d 21h"); To do has an "Open, by when" text bar chart (late, this week, later, no date).
- Status shows what is waiting ("10 looked up · 2 waiting", "next to be looked up") and spending on a scale from $0.01 to $2.00.
- Chat gives Vera her screen of unreadable glyphs with "4 lines in this conversation" and the kids' conversations under it.

**What doesn't**
- A CRT on every page (Home, Chat, Ideas, Plans, To do, Settings, Status) stops being special; the monitor becomes wallpaper.
- Three type voices (serif display, sans body, mono screens) plus colored ticks and dots make each page busy.
- Home's headline "Tell Vera what's on." is serif inside a scanlined screen; at 100% it is soft and glowing, less crisp than the rest.
- On Plans, the large serif day numbers are as loud on empty days as on days with plans, so the plans themselves are small grey boxes under big numerals.
- On the phone, "Choose your own password→ then" runs the arrow into the next word, and the boxed bottom tabs are heavy.
- The "A select" crop shows text fields, not an open select.

**What's beautiful** Home, the Plans radar: the dates as blips on the dial ("4", "9", "17", "24") with rings for the weeks.

**What's ugly** Plans, the month calendar: big serif numerals on every day, empty ones included, crowding the small event boxes.

**What I'd do**
1. Keep the CRT where it shows something no list does (Home's radar, Ideas' radar, the spend scale) and drop it from Settings and To do.
2. Use two type voices, not three: keep the serif for headlines and numbers, set the screens in mono, and drop the serif from inside the CRT headline.
3. On Plans, dim the empty days' numerals and give events more room.
4. Fix the arrow spacing on the setup link and lighten the phone tab bar.

**Verdict** 7.5/10: the most characterful and inventive of the group, with excellent small ideas, but overloaded with monitors and type voices.
