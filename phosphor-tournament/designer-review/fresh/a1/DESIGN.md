# Kitchen Table

**Idea:** FamilyDB should feel like the family's kitchen table: Vera is the first thing you can talk to, and everything else is a short pile of plain-language cards you can take in at a glance.

## The look

- **Type:** Fraunces (a warm, soft serif) for headings and the big numbers. Atkinson Hyperlegible for everything else, because it was designed so letters can't be confused, which matters for kids and tired parents.
- **Colour:** paper cream with ink-dark text. One deep green belongs to Vera. A sunny yellow marks "set this up", and brick red marks only things that are late or need a look. Each person has their own colour (Sam blue, Alex plum, Maya rose, Theo orange), so a kid can find "mine" without reading.
- **Layout:** a calm sidebar on desktop, which becomes a top bar and a six-icon tab strip on the phone. With no JavaScript, everything is an ordinary form.

## What each page puts first

- **Home:** the Ask Vera box with ready-made questions, then tomorrow's plan, overdue to-dos, new ideas. The setup checklist, wish lists and today's spending sit on the side.
- **Chat:** one thread, with Vera's actions shown as cards ("Added to Plans"). The kids' own chats sit next to it, with a plain note that parents can read them. "Writing as" uses big avatar pills.
- **Ideas:** a "save a thought" line first. Cards show kind, who it's for, and drive time with a small arrow that points the way.
- **Plans:** the month, plus one-tap "How did it go?" faces.
- **To do:** quick add, then the overdue ones in red, with "6 days late" rather than a bare date. It warns that reminders need Telegram.
- **Status:** a one-sentence verdict first, then spending, which AI does each job, and a plain "no backup" warning.
- **Settings:** the one thing that needs a look goes in a banner with its fix. Each section gets one sentence, and a tag only where something is off.

## Why it suits this family

Every status is a sentence, not a code. Colour carries meaning (people, kinds, urgency) rather than decoration. The other pages follow the same pattern: a sentence on top, cards below, and a person's colour wherever they appear. A kid's view hides Status and Settings and starts with their own wish list.
