# Kitchen Terminal

**Idea:** the family's shared kitchen-counter screen, drawn in the light of an old green phosphor monitor, with Vera as the friendly face on it.

## The look

- **Type:** *VT323* (pixel CRT) only for big moments: titles, times, dates, numbers, the `>` prompt. *Atkinson Hyperlegible*, which was made for low-vision readers, for everything people read, so kids can read it too. *IBM Plex Mono* for small facts. Vera's replies are set in glowing mono, so you can tell when the screen is talking.
- **Colour:** phosphor-black and one glowing green. A second phosphor, **amber**, means only "this needs you": overdue, setup, settings to fix. Each person has their own colour (Sam green, Alex blue, Maya pink, Theo lilac), so kids can find "me" at a glance.
- **Layout:** a sidebar on desktop, with the grown-up pages grouped separately. On a phone the five main pages become tabs in a sticky header. Touch targets are big, focus shows as a thick amber ring, and there's no JavaScript: ticks are form buttons, "writing as" is radio buttons, and the tabs are links.

## What each page puts first

- **Home:** writing to Vera, with ready-made prompts. Then the setup strip, the next plan, and late to-dos. On a phone: the next plan, then late to-dos.
- **Chat:** the conversation. Threads are tabs. Vera's replies link to what they mention.
- **Ideas:** "Save a thought" comes before the filters. Each card has its kind, who it's for, and a compass showing the drive direction and time.
- **Plans:** a month grid with today ringed and the next plan glowing. Past plans are struck through, and beside them Vera asks "How did it go?" with one-tap answers.
- **To do:** Overdue first ("6 days late"). Each has a tick, an owner and its reminder.
- **Status:** a one-line answer first ("Vera is working", plus what isn't set up). Then spend, 30 days of use, models (flagged "no fallback") and connections.
- **Settings:** one row per section, each saying in a sentence how it stands. Amber rows need attention, and "Do these first" puts them in order.

## Why it suits this family

The retro screen gives the app a character the kids will enjoy without making it look technical. Because amber has a single meaning, nobody has to learn a colour code, and every label is in plain words.
