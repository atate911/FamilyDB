# Kitchen Table

**Idea:** FamilyDB is the family's kitchen table: Vera sits at it, and everything the family keeps track of is laid out on warm paper cards, in plain words, with one bright colour for "this needs you".

## Look

- **Type:** Fraunces (a soft serif) for headings, so it feels like a family notebook. Figtree for everything else: round and legible, with plain digits so times read cleanly for kids. Text 16 px+, targets 44 px.
- **Colour:** Warm paper and ink, with tomato red as the only accent: Vera, the main button, anything late. Green means fine and amber means "have a look". Each person has a colour (Sam blue, Alex plum, Maya orange, Theo green) on their initial everywhere, so a kid can find "mine" without reading.
- **Vera** is a small drawn tomato with a sprout: friendly, easy to spot, not a robot.
- **Layout:** A quiet sidebar on a laptop, with the "Grown-ups" pages set apart. On a phone, a tab row under the header and a menu for the rest. There's no JavaScript: filters are forms, ticks are buttons, "who's writing" is radio pills, and the menu is a `<details>`.

## What each page puts first

- **Home:** a one-sentence summary of today, then a big "Ask Vera" box with starter chips. Below it: tomorrow's plan as a tear-off calendar leaf, overdue to-dos, the dark "Finish setting up" card, what's coming up, new ideas and wish lists.
- **Chat:** the conversation, with the threads beside it (or as pills on a phone). "Who's writing?" sits above the box.
- **Ideas:** "Save a thought for later" comes first. Each card has a compass arrow that points the way to drive.
- **Plans:** the month, with today ringed, then "How did it go?"
- **To do:** quick add, then the overdue to-dos in their own group, showing "6 days late".
- **Status:** one sentence that answers "is it working?", then spend against the limit, 30 days of spending, who answers what, and the connections.
- **Settings:** one row per section saying how it stands now. Rows that need a look are tinted amber.

## Why it fits

Nobody here is technical, and two of them are children. So the pages talk like a person, show people as faces and colours, and make overdue and unfinished things obvious without alarm.
