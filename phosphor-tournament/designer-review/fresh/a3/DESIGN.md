# Kitchen Table

**Idea:** the website is the family's kitchen table on a Saturday morning: Vera is there to talk to, today's stuff is on top, and everything else is one tap away.

## Look

- **Type:** *Fraunces*, a soft serif, for headings and figures. *Atkinson Hyperlegible* for the rest. It helps the kids read.
- **Colour:** warm paper with ink. Vera's deep teal marks her avatar, the main buttons and the next plan. Tomato red is used only for what's late, and amber only for what needs a look. Each person keeps one colour (Sam blue, Alex green, Maya plum, Theo amber), so a kid can spot "mine" without reading.
- **Layout:** on a laptop, a sidebar with the everyday pages, then the reference pages, then Status and Settings. On a phone, a sticky row of five big tabs, with the rest behind the avatar menu. Every control is at least 44 px and has a thick amber focus ring.

## What each page puts first

- **Home:** one sentence on what matters today, then a big "Write to Vera" box with quick starts. Then the next plan as a tear-off date card, overdue to-dos, the setup steps that are left, what's coming up, new ideas and the wish lists. On a phone these stack in that order.
- **Chat:** the family conversation, with the kids' chats beneath it marked "parents can read these". When Vera does something, her reply links to the result. The writing box has "Writing as" and a "Send where I am" switch.
- **Ideas:** "Save a thought for later" comes first. Each card shows its kind, who it's for, the drive time with a compass arrow, and where it stands.
- **Plans:** the month, with today ringed. Under it are tomorrow's plan and "How did it go?" with three faces a kid can tap.
- **To do:** quick add, then the overdue ones grouped in red. Big tick boxes.
- **Status:** a plain verdict ("Vera is working"), then money, then models and connections.
- **Settings:** nine rows, each saying how it stands. The ones that need a look are tinted amber.

## Why it suits them

The family mostly talks to Vera on Telegram, so the site doesn't try to be another inbox. It answers "what now?" at a glance, in the family's words, not a database's.
