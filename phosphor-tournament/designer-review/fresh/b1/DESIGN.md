# Afterglow

**Idea:** a calm, dark family screen with the glow of an old green terminal: what Vera keeps shines soft green, and only what needs a person lights up amber.

## Look

- **Colour:** deep green-black glass with faint scanlines. Phosphor green marks Vera's world (links, the next plan, live status). Amber means "needs you": late to-dos, setup, settings to check. Each person has a colour (Sam green, Alex cyan, Maya pink, Theo lilac), so kids can spot "mine" without reading.
- **Type:** *VT323* pixel type only for big moments (titles, dates, money, "1 DAY"). *IBM Plex Mono* for small labels and data. *Atkinson Hyperlegible* for everything you actually read, so the retro look never costs readability.
- **Layout:** a sidebar on desktop. On phones, a sticky top bar with five big tabs and a menu. Targets are 44 px, focus shows as a bright ring, and there's no JavaScript: filters are GET forms and ticks are POST buttons.

## What each page puts first

- **Home:** the box to write to Vera, under "Hi Sam" and a blinking cursor. Then setup steps left, the next plan with a countdown, and the late to-dos. On a phone the to-dos move up, just after the next plan.
- **Chat:** the conversation. "Writing as" and "Send where I am" sit inside the compose box. The kids' chats are listed beside it, marked as readable by parents.
- **Ideas:** "save a thought" as a terminal prompt, then filters and the list. A radar maps looked-up ideas by drive time and direction from home.
- **Plans:** a month grid with today outlined and the next plan glowing. Below it, what's next and "How did it go?".
- **To do:** quick add, then late items in amber. A note explains that reminders need Telegram, so none would arrive yet.
- **Status:** a plain verdict on an oscilloscope trace. Then spend as an LED bar against the limit, the 30-day numbers, which model does which job (with "no backup" flagged), and connections.
- **Settings:** one line per section saying how it stands. The three needing attention are in amber.

## Why it suits FamilyDB

A home server running an assistant is a bit of computer-room magic, and the phosphor glow makes that feel warm, not technical. One colour rule (green is fine, amber needs you) reads the same to an eight-year-old and a tired parent.
