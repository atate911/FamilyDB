# Designer's review, group C

Today's page, for reference: dark cards with soft rounded corners. A small CRT with a radar sits beside the ask box on Home, and another on Ideas. Status, Plans, To do and Settings are plain dark cards with small coloured dots. It is tidy and a little generic. The phosphor only appears in two framed screens.

## Desk Terminal, Joined (`r13-idea-1`)

**The idea in one line**
Every page is a desk: a bezelled CRT monitor at the top of a left column, with that page's tools under it, and the page's records as a plain table on the right.

**What works**
- The layout is the same on every page: monitor and controls on the left, list on the right. You learn it once.
- On Home you type straight into the screen. "What's on your mind?" is in pixel mono inside the CRT, with the text box under it. The page's main action is the lit thing, which is right.
- The lists became real tables. On To do, WHO and DUE are in columns and "was due 6 days ago" is right-aligned in amber mono. Ideas has DRIVE, WAY, FOR and # columns ("27 min S", "#1"). These read fast.
- Settings is one quiet list with arrows. It is the calmest page in the set.
- The Status monitor holds a needle meter: "$0.00 TODAY", a 0–2.00 scale, a hatched zone past the limit. Here the instrument is the data.

**What doesn't**
- The monitor's job changes from page to page. Home has next-up and a radar. Ideas has a radar again. Plans has next-up again, over a spike chart. To do has a bar tally. Status has a meter. Chat has a small glyph screen. It is one container holding six different ideas.
- The Plans spike chart can't be read. The day labels under it run together ("3 4 5 6 ... 17 ... 24").
- The left column turns into a filter sidebar of chunky buttons. Ideas has thirteen kind buttons in a 3-wide grid, and To do has Open/Done/Cancelled/All as big tiles. That grid is heavier than the list it filters.
- Selected states are a flat slate-grey fill: the nav's "Status", "Vera" and "Ideas", "Open", "Month", "Any". It is the one cold, dead colour on the page, and it fights the phosphor.
- "Ready · OpenAI · gpt-6-luna · $2.00 a day" is repeated at the foot of the left column on Home, Chat, Ideas, Plans and To do, and again on Settings. It is the same fact six times.
- On Home, "Finish setting up" leads the right column with an amber top rule. It outranks Coming up and To do.

**What's beautiful**
- The Status meter: a thin arc of ticks, "1.00" and "2.00" in mono above it, a thick needle resting on 0, and a hatched band past 2.00. That is real instrument drawing.
- Home's Coming up: a thin vertical track with a lit "NOW" dot at the top and hollow stops for each plan. Times ("09:00") and "in 6 days" sit in two mono columns.
- On Ideas, "Nearest #7 Pumpkin patch at Bi-Mart farm [18 MIN NW]" is set in green inside the screen with a blinking block cursor. It works because it is short.

**What's ugly**
- The To do monitor's tally of "########" and "........" next to "Late / This week / Later / No date". It is ASCII cosplay that gives a bar chart for four items.
- Hollow amber checkboxes on late tasks. With the amber text beside them they read as warnings, not as boxes to tick.
- The Plans calendar's grey date chips ("Pho Oregon 17:00" in a grey slab) feel like a different, older app.
- Squinted, Home is two bright patches (the monitor, the Send button) and a grey field.

**Committee check**
The monitor is a good idea that has been made to do everything. Radar, next-up, meter, spike chart and tally are five instruments from five rounds, each put in the same bezel. The repeated "Ready" status block looks like an answer to a judge who asked "is it working?" on every page. The numbered circles on Home's sections (1 to 5) and on Status's index (1 to 8) add a second ordering system that nothing uses.

**What I'd do**
1. Give each instrument one job and one page. Keep the Status meter and the Home ask-screen. Drop the Plans spike chart and the To do tally; those pages' lists already show the same thing.
2. Remove the repeated "Ready" block from every page but Status.
3. Replace the slate selected fill with a thin phosphor underline or a lit rule. One selected-state treatment across nav, tabs and filters.
4. Collapse the Ideas kind grid into a single select or one row of small text chips.
5. Drop the radar from Home. It is already on Ideas.
6. Move "Finish setting up" below Coming up, or to a one-line strip.

**Verdict**
6.5 / 10. The bones are good: one monitor, one table, a fine meter. But the monitor has become a drawer where every feature was stored.

## Blueprint, Lit Scale (`r18-mut-7`)

**The idea in one line**
Every page is an engineering drawing sheet. It has a teal grid, column numbers 1–6 and row letters A–C in the margins, a title block ("SHEET / NOTE / NO. FD-09") and a drawn footer block, with a lit "NOW" scale running across the lists.

**What works**
- The title block is a strong, honest system. "SHEET · STATUS · NOTE · Is it working… · NO. FD-09" is the same on every page, and the sheet numbers (FD-01 Vera, FD-02 Ideas, FD-03 Plans, FD-04 To do, FD-09 Status, FD-10 Settings) are a nice joke that also orders things.
- The lit scale is the best single motif in this group. On Home's Coming up, and again in To do, a glowing "NOW" tab sits on a tick ruler beside "SAT 3 OCT", cutting across the table at today's place. It is fine line art that means something.
- The Plans month view looks like a drawing: weekday names in small rings, week rows numbered 1, 2, 3 in rings down the left margin, today's "3" a lit green disc.
- Settings in two columns with numbered rows (01–09) and "SHEET · HOW IT STANDS" headers is orderly.

**What doesn't**
- The grid is everywhere: behind the chat, behind forms, behind the Status cards. A blueprint grid is a background for a drawing. Here it is wallpaper, and teal-on-black competes with the phosphor for "this is the lit colour".
- Each page has its own frame colour: yellow on To do, lavender on Ideas, cyan on Status and Plans. Section headers end in short coloured bars in cyan, yellow, pink and lavender. That is a rainbow coding system nobody asked for, in a brief that asked for measured cool companions.
- The display type, "WHAT'S ON YOUR MIND?" in a heavy extended face in warm cream, belongs to a poster, not a drawing sheet. Cream is close to the tan the brief rules out.
- All caps, letterspaced, is used for nav, tabs, headings, labels and buttons ("ADD AN IDEA", "FILTER", "ALL PLANS"). With everything shouting, nothing is a heading.
- Ideas carries three filter rows of outlined pill buttons (Kind, Status, For) next to a CRT radar. On Home there is also a CRT next-up monitor. Is this a drawing or a terminal? It is both.

**What's beautiful**
- "NOW ─ SAT 3 OCT ─┼┼┼┼┼┼" in To do, sitting between "Buy Maya's birthday present" and "Renew library cards". The bright lit tab on a thin ruler is exactly the owner's brief.
- The footer title block: "PROJECT FAMILYDB · MOTTO A little less planning… · DRAWN FamilyDB © 2026 · SHEET FD-00". It is witty and correctly made.
- The calendar's ringed week numbers in the left margin.

**What's ugly**
- The user's chat bubbles have scalloped, cloud-shaped borders, with a triangle badge holding "S" or "A". They are absurd on a drawing sheet and clash with Vera's square boxes.
- The hatched kind swatches in Lately added: yellow diagonal hatch for Activity, Restaurant and Outing, and blue for Trip. At this size the yellow hatch reads as brown, which is off-motif and looks like hazard tape.
- The dashed-border suggestion buttons ("→ What should we do today?") next to the solid ask box. They look like placeholders.
- The "NEEDS A LOOK" warning tag on Settings is in a hand-drawn tilted amber box.
- Squinted, Home is a noisy mid-grey texture with no clear lead.

**Committee check**
This is the camel. A blueprint sheet, a heavy poster headline, a CRT monitor with a radar, hatched swatches, cloud bubbles, a colour per page, dashed chips, a lit scale. Each could hold a design on its own. Stacked, they cancel out. The coloured end-bars on every section header look exactly like something added after a judge asked for "more colour".

**What I'd do**
1. Keep the title block, the margin numbers, the lit NOW scale and the drawn calendar. Those are the idea.
2. Keep the grid only in the page margin and title block. The content area should be flat dark.
3. One accent for structure (the teal line) and phosphor for what's lit. Remove the per-page frame colours and the coloured end-bars.
4. Replace the cream extended headline with the sheet's own sans in sentence case. Drop most of the caps.
5. Square the chat bubbles. Remove the scallops and the triangle badges.
6. Replace hatched swatches with small kind icons in one colour.
7. Decide between the CRT and the drawing. Either drop the next-up monitor and radar, or draw them as line figures on the sheet.

**Verdict**
5.5 / 10. It has the sharpest single motif in the group, the lit scale, buried under the most accretion.

## Dessau, Dialled (`x14b-dessau-cross`)

**The idea in one line**
A Bauhaus editorial page: a big geometric sans headline, a strip of dotted-leader figures under it, and one round phosphor dial per page in the top right corner that shows the page's key number.

**What works**
- One dial per page is a real system, and each dial says one thing. Status shows a spend gauge with "$0.00 TODAY". Plans shows "OCT 4" and "Sun · tomorrow". To do shows an amber "LATE 3 TASKS". Settings shows a segmented ring with "8 of 9 IN ORDER". Ideas shows a radar. You can read each one in a second.
- The stat strip is consistent. "OPEN ····· 4 | ▲ LATE ····· 3 | NO DATE ····· 1" on To do, "WORKING ··· 6 | OFF ··· 7 | LAST 30 DAYS ··· $1.26" on Status, and Today/Plans/To do/Late/Ideas on Home. It is a confident, Swiss device.
- The Settings list is clean: name left, state right-aligned after a dotted leader, one arrow.
- To do has a thin amber left rule on late rows, dates in a column, and a green NOW line across the list before "Renew library cards".
- The big headlines ("Status", "Things to do", "Ideas") have real scale and authority. Today's page has none.

**What doesn't**
- The Home dial does too much. A next-up face is in the middle, with plan dates 4, 9, 17 and 24 orbiting in rings, "1w 2w 3w" labels, and a lit stem at 12 o'clock. At page size it is hard to read. The Coming up list below it says the same thing.
- The colour coding is on everything. Coming up, To do and Lately added have teal, blue and lavender top rules. The suggestion chips have coloured squares and "usually Plans / usually To do / usually Ideas" captions. Kind tiles are tan and orange. Page icons are coloured squares. The footer needs a legend ("Light · The family's · The page · Late") to explain it. A page that needs a key has too many colours.
- Browns and tans: the kind tiles (Activity is a tan square with a bike), the users' avatar squares "S" and "A", and the tan top edge on their chat bubbles. The figures in the stat strip ("12", "4", "$1.26") are in a warm cream-gold. The brief names browns and tans as off-motif.
- Home's setup strip ("Finish setting up 1 Choose your own password 2 Telegram 3 Google Calendar") is a fine compact idea. But it sits between the stat strip and the three columns as one more horizontal band.

**What's beautiful**
- "Tell Vera. Let's plan." The cream sans with "plan." in glowing phosphor is the best headline in this group. The glow lands on the one word that matters.
- The To do dial: a black disc with a huge amber "3", "LATE" above and "TASKS" below. It is one number, one colour, one meaning.
- Home's Coming up list puts each date in a thin ring (4, 9, 17, 24), which echoes the dial without copying it.
- Status's "01 Who answers / 02 Keys / 03 Connected to" in large light numerals, with three clean columns of dot-lamps.

**What's ugly**
- The tan "S" and "A" avatar squares in Chat. They are the warmest, muddiest thing on the page, next to Vera's phosphor glyphs.
- The Ideas radar dial with ten numbered blips and the caption "10 on the map, north up, numbered as the list, rings 15m · 45m · 2h by road". When a picture needs that caption, the picture is too much.
- The three coloured top rules over Home's columns look like tabs on a filing folder.
- The coloured square page icons beside each headline (green, lavender, slate). The headline is big enough on its own.

**Committee check**
The skeleton is one hand: headline, figure strip, dial, list. Two things are stacked on it: a colour-coding scheme (section rules, chip squares, kind tiles, page-icon squares, the legend) and a few dial faces that tried to hold a second idea. The "usually Plans" chip captions and the footer legend read as fixes for confusion that the colours caused.

**What I'd do**
1. Remove the colour coding. Section rules go to one neutral line. Chips lose their squares and captions. Page icons go. The footer legend goes with them.
2. Replace tan and brown with the cool companions: neutral avatar initials, kind icons in one grey. Set the strip's figures in the off-white of the headlines, with amber kept for Late only.
3. Simplify the Home dial to the next plan and "tomorrow". Let the Coming up list carry the other dates.
4. Make the Ideas radar a plain picture without numbered blips and caption, or remove it.
5. Fold the setup strip into the figure strip as one more cell ("Setup 1 of 3 →").

**Verdict**
7 / 10. It is a clear editorial idea with the best headline and the most legible dials here, wearing a colour-coding coat it does not need.

## Lit Manual, Dialled (`x14b-manual-cross`)

**The idea in one line**
A lit operating manual. Each title carries a count ("Things to do 4 OPEN · 3 late", "Settings 9 PARTS"), structure is drawn in thin rails and stops, and a "NEXT IN 1 DAY" dial on Home has a lit wire that runs down the page into the timeline.

**What works**
- The joined dial is the one idea in this group that really connects two things. The dial's lit stem drops out of the bottom of the ring and becomes the vertical rail of Coming up, with "NOW" at the top and stops at 4, 9, 17 and 24 Oct. The instrument and the list are one drawing.
- Thin rails with stops are the shared language of the site. To do's left margin has dates on a rail with a lit NOW dot. Lately added's kinds hang off a rail. Settings is numbered 1–9 down a rail, and "8" is amber where it needs a look. Chat's thread has arrows coming off a rail. This is the owner's "fine line art that draws structure", used to mean order and time every time.
- Status has a "FIG. 1" wiring diagram: keys on the left, connections on the right, and the lit wire runs from OpenAI to "gpt-6-luna". There is a caption and a lamp legend. It explains the page in one picture.
- Colour is restrained: off-white, greys, phosphor for what's live, amber for late. Kind colour is reduced to tiny dots.
- Titles with counts ("Ideas 12 KEPT", "Plans 4 PLANS AHEAD", "Status 3 of 4 ANSWERING") turn the heading into information without adding a band.
- Chat is the best of the four. Vera's lines are plain text by her glyph. The family's lines are outlined boxes with a green edge, aligned right. Times sit on the rail.

**What doesn't**
- There are too many instruments. Home has the dial, Plans has the same dial again beside the calendar, Status has a round "$0.00 of $2.00 today" lamp, and Ideas has a square bezelled radar CRT. Only the Home dial is joined to anything.
- The Home figure strip ("NEXT Sun 4 | PLANS 4 | TO DO 4 | LATE 3 | IDEAS 12") repeats the dial (next), the timeline (plans) and the To do header (4, 3 late).
- The small numbers inside the dial ("24", "9", "17", "4", and "free") are cryptic. "free" means a free weekend, but nothing says so.
- Ideas has two ways to filter by kind: "Show: All ideas / Restaurants" tabs and a row of ten kind chips with counts, as well as the Status/For dropdowns.
- The selected nav item is a slate-grey block, like Desk Terminal's. It is the one dead patch on a lit page.

**What's beautiful**
- The wire. A green line falls from the bottom stop of the dial, through "NOW", past each plan's stop. That is the whole brief in one gesture.
- On Plans, a lit line runs across the calendar at today and ends in a glowing dot on Saturday 3. Days outside the month get a fine diagonal hatch in the corners.
- "Hand Vera anything to plan." in a condensed sans, with "plan." lit. It is quieter than Dessau's headline and it suits a manual.
- Status's "FIG. 1": a thin diagram with a boxed "FamilyDB" at the top and a caption set like a real manual.

**What's ugly**
- The wiring diagram on Status is drawn in a slate-lavender line, while the rest of the site's rails are grey and green. It looks borrowed.
- The Ideas radar sits in a heavy square bezel, top right, beside a page of hairlines. It is the only chunky object in the set.
- The row of ten kind chips on Ideas, each with its own coloured square, is the one place the restraint breaks.
- The To do footnote "at its deadline's … amber" is cut off at the bottom of the left rail and hangs there.

**Committee check**
The core (counts in titles, rails and stops, one lit wire) is one hand. The accretion is in the instruments. A second copy of the dial on Plans, a round spend lamp on Status and a square radar on Ideas are each a separate "add a CRT object here" from separate rounds. The figure strip on Home is a leftover from the Dessau parent, and the line art already does its job.

**What I'd do**
1. One dial, on Home, joined to its timeline. Remove it from Plans; the lit today line in the calendar is enough.
2. Draw the Ideas radar as a hairline figure on the page ("FIG. 2"), with no bezel, or remove it. Do the same for the Status lamp: put "$0.00 of $2.00" in the title count.
3. Remove the Home figure strip.
4. Ideas: one kind filter. Keep the chip row without the colour squares, and drop the Restaurants tab or the dropdowns.
5. Draw the Status FIG. 1 in the site's own grey and phosphor.
6. Replace the slate selected nav with a lit underline or a stop on a rail.

**Verdict**
7.5 / 10. It has the clearest single idea in the group, line art that carries time and order up to one lit wire. It needs its three extra instruments removed, and then it would be the best page here.
