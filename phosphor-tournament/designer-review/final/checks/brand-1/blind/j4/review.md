# Review: P and Q

The two designs share almost everything: the same layout, grid, cream and deep-green palette, serif headings over a sans body, person colours (Sam blue, Alex purple, Maya pink, Theo orange, Everyone grey with a house), the same cards, chips, sidebar and phone tab bar, and the same text on every page. They differ in identity and in a few details. This review is about those differences, plus the faults they share.

## P

**What it is**
A warm, calm family dashboard with a technical character added on top. The logo is a smiling computer monitor on a black tile, and "FamilyDB" is followed by a green block like a text cursor. The status pill is black with green monospace text ("Vera is ready"). Vera's avatar is "a little pane of glass full of glyphs", a black square of tiny green characters. Her timestamps and the "Added to Plans · Thu 1 Oct, 10 am–2 pm · for Sam and Alex" confirmation cards in Chat are set in monospace. The Ideas page adds a dark radar chart, "How far each idea is from home", with rings for 30 min, 1 h, 2 h and 3 h, numbered green dots and a numbered list beside it. Section labels are spaced capitals ("OVERDUE · 3", "NO DATE · 1"). Dark mode is a cool near-black with bright green accents.

**What works**
- The basic system is sound and the same on every page. Home leads with a plain sentence that links its key words ("Roller rink tomorrow, and three to-dos are late"). Next up has a tear-off date block. To-dos have a red left edge and red "6 days late", and the late items are grouped above "No date". The surprise chips ("Surprise · hidden from Maya") are easy to read.
- Who each item belongs to is clear everywhere: a coloured initial and a name on to-dos, plans, ideas and the add-a-to-do chips ("Sam / Alex / Maya / Theo / Everyone"). This works for the kids too, and the chips are big enough to tap on a phone.
- It knows who it is. The monitor logo, the cursor block and the glyph pane are all the same idea, and Settings › "Colours in FamilyDB" spells out the rule: the smiling monitor is the app and never Vera, and green light belongs to Vera.
- The radar is a real idea. It turns "drive times from home" into one picture with north up, and on desktop the numbered list beside it is easy to scan.
- Monospace for Vera's confirmations sets her actions apart from what the family writes in Chat.

**What doesn't**
- The glyph-pane Vera has no face, letter or shape at the sizes it is used (about 32 px in Chat, 40 px on Home). At those sizes it reads as a dark smudge or a "Matrix" terminal, not as someone a 9-year-old is talking to. In Chat it is the darkest thing on the page, beside soft lilac and blue bubbles.
- The monospace text in Vera's confirmation cards is harder to read and wraps badly ("10 am–" / "2 pm · for Sam and Alex"). Monospace also shows up in places where it does no work, such as the "Vera is ready" pill.
- The radar breaks where it matters. Ideas 2–6 (19 to 27 minutes away) overlap in one blob labelled "2–6". Idea 1 (18 min) sits between the first two rings while 2–6 sit on the second. The "1 h" and "30 min" labels are crowded together on the vertical axis. On the phone the radar is pushed below twelve idea cards, so hardly anyone will scroll that far.
- Dark mode removes the filled green Ask Vera panel. On Home it becomes a dark card like all the others, so the main action no longer stands out. Primary buttons ("Add a plan", "+ Add") turn cream, so the green that means "act here" in light mode has no match in dark mode.
- The black logo tile and black status pill are the two heaviest marks on a cream sidebar. They pull the eye before Home or the user's name does.
- Faults shared with Q (see "What is still wrong" below): Wishes is missing from the phone tab bar; the desktop month calendar is mostly empty; small grey metadata text.

**Mark: 7/10**: a well-made, clear family planner with a real and consistent identity, but the identity is "terminal", it costs legibility and warmth exactly where the kids meet Vera, and its one new piece (the radar) doesn't read where the ideas cluster.

## Q

**What it is**
The same planner with a quieter identity. The logo is a white house on a green tile with a small yellow dot (a lit window or the sun). The status pill is pale green with green text. Vera is a white circle with a serif "V". The Ask Vera panel is a medium green that stays green in dark mode. Section labels are in sentence case ("Overdue · 3", "No date · 1"). Ideas is a plain grid of cards with no map. Dark mode is a warm brown-black that keeps the light theme's green fills and green primary buttons. Settings › Colours says only "Green is Vera" and "Red only means late, or broken."

**What works**
- Everything listed under P's basic system applies here too: the linked sentence on Home, the date blocks, the red overdue edge, the person chips, the surprise chips, and the "How did it go?" faces with Loved it / OK / Not great under each.
- Vera is easy to recognise: a letter in a circle, as easy to read at 24 px in Chat as on Home. A child can tell "V" apart from "S", "A", "M" and "T", because Vera's is the only white circle with a serif letter.
- Dark mode is better done. The Ask Vera panel stays a green block above the cards. "Add a plan", "+ Add" and "Add an idea" stay green. The warm black suits the cream of the light theme.
- All text is in the readable sans or the serif. There is no monospace, so Vera's "Added to Plans · Sun 4 Oct, 1 pm · for Maya and Theo" wraps like normal text.
- The Settings colour key is shorter and states the two rules that matter, green is Vera and red means late or broken.
- The sidebar is calmer. The pale "Vera is ready" pill doesn't compete with the nav, so the page title is the first thing you see.

**What doesn't**
- Its identity is generic. A house icon with a dot, a "V" monogram and green-on-cream could belong to any home-services or family app. Nothing here would be missed if the logo were swapped out.
- The yellow dot on the house is the only playful touch, and it is about 6 px across, too small to carry anything.
- Ideas has no overview of where things are. The drive times are only a grey line at the foot of each card ("about 1 h 50 min drive, north-east"), so you can't see near against far at a glance.
- The sentence-case section labels ("No date · 1") are weaker than P's capitals and are easy to confuse with body text on the phone To do page.
- Faults shared with P (see "What is still wrong" below): Wishes is missing from the phone tab bar; the desktop month calendar is mostly empty; small grey metadata text.

**Mark: 8/10**: the same clear, family-readable system, with a Vera the kids can recognise, readable type everywhere and a dark mode that keeps the hierarchy, held back by an identity that is pleasant but forgettable.

## Between them

### 1. Which would you build for this family, and the three reasons that decided it.
**Q.**
1. **Vera is a person the kids can see.** Q's "V" circle reads at every size and sits beside the family's own initials as one of them. P's glyph pane is a dark texture that looks like a machine, at the exact place (Chat, Ask Vera) where a 9- or 11-year-old is meant to talk to her.
2. **Dark mode keeps the hierarchy.** In Q the Ask Vera panel and the green primary buttons stay as they are in light mode. In P's dark Home the Ask Vera panel turns into an ordinary dark card, and green primary buttons turn cream.
3. **Plain type everywhere.** Q has no monospace, so Vera's confirmations wrap and read like the rest of Chat. P's monospace confirmation cards break awkwardly ("10 am–" / "2 pm") and are harder for a young reader.

### 2. Which has the stronger identity, and does that identity cost anything in use?
**P**, clearly. The smiling monitor with the cursor block, the black "Vera is ready" pill, the glyph-pane Vera, the monospace voice and the green-on-black radar make one idea that a family would recognise and remember, and Settings even spells out the rules. It does cost something:
- Vera is hard to recognise at small sizes and looks cold to kids.
- The monospace text is harder to read and wraps badly.
- Dark mode loses the green fills that mark Ask Vera and the primary buttons.
- The two heaviest marks on every page, the black logo tile and the black status pill, are branding, not content.

The radar is the one place where the identity adds use, and it still overlaps where five ideas sit close together.

### 3. Where the other one is better, if anywhere (page and element).
- **Ideas, the "How far each idea is from home" radar and list** (desktop). It gives a real at-a-glance answer to "what's close?", and the numbered list beside it is well set. Q has nothing like it.
- **To do, section labels.** P's spaced capitals "OVERDUE · 3" and "NO DATE · 1" divide the list more clearly than Q's sentence-case "Overdue · 3" / "No date · 1", above all on the phone.
- **Brand mark.** The smiling monitor plus cursor is a logo you could put on an app icon and remember. Q's house with a dot is not.

### 4. What is still wrong with Q, most important first.
1. **Wishes is missing from the phone tab bar.** The bar is Home, Chat, Ideas, Plans, To do. The kids' wish lists, the one part that is theirs, can only be reached through the "Wish lists" card far down Home. On a phone, which is how the kids will use it, Wishes should be a tab (or replace Ideas or Chat in the bar).
2. **The identity needs a real mark.** Keep the house but give it character: a bigger lit window, or a shape that can carry Vera, plus one family-friendly accent used on purpose. Add one visual idea on Ideas such as a simple "near → far" strip (borrowing P's radar idea, done without the overlap).
3. **Desktop Plans calendar is mostly empty.** Five rows of tall cells hold five events. "Coming up" and "How did it go?" sit below the fold on a laptop. Shorter cells or a two-week default would bring them up.
4. **Small grey metadata.** Lines such as "Not looked up yet", "about 22 min drive, south", the "2 to rate" and "1 to check" hints in the sidebar, and "Parent · admin" are small grey on cream and will be hard for a 9-year-old to read. Raise their size and contrast.
5. **On the phone To do, adding comes last.** The "Add a to-do" form sits below the whole list (on desktop it is at the top), so a child who opens To do to add something has to scroll past every late item first. Put a compact add field or a "+" at the top.
