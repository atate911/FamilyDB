# Review: FamilyDB, designs P and Q

The two designs share one skeleton almost exactly: the same left sidebar (Home … What Vera knows, then "Behind the scenes": Status, Settings, Family), the same cream background with deep-green and mustard accents, the same serif headings over a sans body, and the same cards, chips, calendar and five-tab phone bar. They also share the same copy. Below, P and Q are judged on what each one shows, and the "Between them" section deals mostly with where they differ.

## P

**What it is**
A warm, quiet, paper-like family dashboard. The logo is a green rounded square with a white house and a small yellow sun dot, next to "FamilyDB" in a serif. Vera appears as a white "V" in a green disc, and her status is a pale green pill ("Vera is ready"). The Ask Vera panel on Home is mid-green (#1f5c50-ish) with a mustard Send button. Dark mode is a warm charcoal that keeps the green buttons and the per-person colours.

**What works**
- **Home answers "what's happening" in one screen.** The headline "Good morning, Sam." is followed by a sentence with two links ("Roller rink tomorrow, and three to-dos are late"). Below it come Ask Vera, Next up (a big SUN 4 OCT date block, "Tomorrow" and "Leave by 12:30 pm" chips, then See the plan / Move it), and the To do list with a red left rule on late items. The right column holds Finish setting up, How did Silver Falls hike go?, Wish lists and Vera today. The hierarchy is clear, and the setup card is fenced off with "Only admins see this."
- **People are colour-coded and the code is applied everywhere.** Sam is blue, Alex purple, Maya crimson and Theo orange, plus a grey house for "Everyone". The colours appear as avatars on to-dos, as chips in "Who's it for?", as stacked avatars in Chat and on calendar events. Settings has a "Colours in FamilyDB" legend that explains them ("Green is Vera. Red only means late, or broken.").
- **To do is direct and kid-manageable.** Big circular tick targets, an "Overdue · 3" group, "6 days late" in red and a "Surprise · hidden from Maya" pill. On phone, the Add form sits below the list and uses large person chips that wrap onto two rows.
- **Plans** has a proper month grid with today (the 3rd) circled in green, multi-day Cannon Beach spanning Sat–Sun, a "Coming up" list and a "How did it go?" card with three large face buttons (Loved it / OK / Not great). On phone the grid shrinks to coloured dots under the dates, which is the right compression.
- **Chat** reads like a family thread. Vera's confirmations carry a tinted "Added to Plans" card with a chevron, suggestions come with "+ Plan it" buttons, and the footer says "Writing as Sam · Not Sam? Switch", which suits a shared laptop. The text "Sam and Alex can read Maya's and Theo's chats" is stated openly in the side list.
- **Ideas** cards that haven't been looked up yet get a dashed border, while ones with a drive time get a solid one: a quiet but honest distinction.
- **Dark mode** is careful. Cards lift off the background, the late red and the person colours survive, and the green CTAs stay green.

**What doesn't**
- **The identity is generic.** A house in a green square on cream, with a serif heading, could be any "family organiser" template. Vera is the letter V in a circle, with no character beyond her name.
- **Numerals use a slashed zero in body text** ("1Ø am", "$2.ØØ", "about 2Ø min drive", "1 h 5Ø min"). It looks like a code font leaking into a family app, and a 9-year-old may read it as a different character.
- **Secondary text is small and low-contrast.** Dates, "No reminder", drive times and the status chips are roughly 12–13 px grey on cream, and on desktop the To do metadata line is tiny next to the 15 px titles.
- **The phone tab bar** has Home / Chat / Ideas / Plans / To do only. Wishes, the page most likely to be the kids' own, isn't one tap away.
- **Maya's crimson sits close to the "late" red.** The Oaks Park and Nutcracker events are pink-tinted with a crimson left bar, much like the red left rule on overdue to-dos, which undercuts "Red only means late".
- Phone Ideas: the Quick idea placeholder is cut off ("e.g. a hike someone m").

**Mark: 7.5/10.** It is a clear, consistent, well-organised and readable family dashboard that works on desktop, phone and dark, but it has almost no personality of its own, and its small grey text and slashed zeros work against young readers.

## Q

**What it is**
The same layout and content as P, given a "friendly terminal" identity. The logo is a black rounded square holding a green smiling monitor, and the "FamilyDB" wordmark ends in a green block cursor. The status pill is black with green monospace text ("● Vera is ready"). Vera's avatar is a small dark pane full of green glyphs. Monospace also appears in chat timestamps, in the "Added to Plans · Sun 4 Oct, 1 pm · for Maya and Theo" plan cards, and in section labels ("OVERDUE · 3", "NO DATE · 1", "MON TUE …" in the calendar header). The Ask Vera panel is a darker, near-black green. Ideas gains a radar-style "How far each idea is from home" chart. In dark mode, Vera's chat bubbles get a glowing green border and the primary buttons ("Add", "Add a plan", "Add an idea") turn cream.

**What works**
- **Everything that works in P works here too**, because Home, To do, Plans, Chat, Status and Settings are laid out the same way: the same Next up block, overdue grouping, rating faces, calendar and person colours.
- **A distinct, ownable identity.** The smiling monitor, the blinking-cursor wordmark and the green-on-black "Vera is ready" pill are recognisable even in the half-size overview. Settings explains the system in the colour legend: "Vera is a little pane of glass full of glyphs. Green light belongs to her, and to FamilyDB." and "The smiling monitor is the app itself, never Vera." So the brand separates the app from the assistant, which P never does.
- **The radar on Ideas** is genuinely useful and fun. Home is the centre, there are rings at 30 min / 1 h / 2 h / 3 h, compass points, and numbered dots keyed to a list sorted nearest-first ("1 Pumpkin patch at Bi-Mart farm · 18 min, north-west" … "10 Cannon Beach weekend · 2 h 45 min, west"). The plotted positions agree with the stated times and directions. A kid can see which ideas are "close" at a glance.
- **Vera has a visible presence in Chat**, especially in dark mode, where her bubbles glow green and stand apart from Alex's purple and Sam's blue.
- **Small-caps mono group labels** ("OVERDUE · 3") separate the To do groups slightly more firmly than P's sentence-case labels.

**What doesn't**
- **Vera's avatar doesn't work at the sizes it's used.** At 28–40 px in Chat, Home and Status it reads as a dark, noisy square, not a face or a letter. In dark mode it nearly disappears into the background. A child can't tell what it is.
- **Monospace for data costs legibility.** "Added to Plans · Sun 4 Oct, 1 pm · for Maya and Theo" in mono is wider and wraps to four lines in the phone chat card. Mono timestamps and calendar headers add texture but no clarity.
- **The radar crowds the desktop Ideas page.** It sits above the cards, so in the first screen you see the radar and only the first row of ideas, where P shows three rows. Five dots collapse into one unreadable "2–6" cluster. On phone it is moved to the bottom, after all twelve cards, where it is easy to miss.
- **Dark-mode buttons switch from green to cream** ("Add", "Add a plan"), so the primary action changes colour between themes. The glowing green Vera borders also compete with the green "Ready" and "Working" status chips.
- It inherits all of P's shared faults: slashed zeros, small grey metadata, no Wishes tab on phone, Maya's crimson against late-red, and the truncated Quick idea placeholder.

**Mark: 7/10.** It is the same sound structure with a memorable, well-explained character on top, but that character's main element (Vera's glyph pane) is illegible at working size, and its monospace and radar add friction to some of the pages the family uses most.

## Between them

**1. Which would you build for this family, and the three reasons that decided it.**
**P.**
- **Vera is legible as a character.** A "V" in a green disc is readable by a 9-year-old at 28 px, in light and in dark. Q's glyph pane turns into a dark smudge at exactly the sizes where Vera appears most (Chat, Ask Vera, Status).
- **Plain sans for the family's data.** Plan cards, times and labels in P are set in the proportional body face. Q's monospace makes the same strings wider and wrap more on phone (the Oaks Park "Added to Plans" card), which matters for the kids.
- **The pages lead with their content.** P's desktop Ideas page opens on the idea cards. Q puts a decorative-looking (though accurate) chart first and pushes the list down. Elsewhere the two are equal, so these small usability costs decide it.

**2. Which has the stronger identity, and does it cost anything in use?**
Q, clearly. The smiling-monitor logo, the cursor after "FamilyDB", the black-and-green "Vera is ready" pill, Vera as a pane of glyphs and the radar together make something a family would recognise as *their* app. It also cleanly splits "the app" (the monitor) from "the assistant" (the glyph pane). It does cost something, in three places: Vera's avatar is unreadable at small sizes; monospace data text is harder to scan and wraps more; and the radar takes the first screen of desktop Ideas. In dark mode it also changes the primary button colour (green to cream) and puts green glow on Vera's bubbles that competes with the green status chips. None of these costs breaks anything, but each one lands on a page the family uses daily.

**3. Where the other one is better, if anywhere.**
- **Ideas, the "How far each idea is from home" radar.** It is accurate, fun for the kids, and answers "what's close?" faster than reading drive times off twelve cards. It is worth bringing into P, placed below the cards on desktop or as a toggle next to the list.
- **Settings, the "Colours in FamilyDB" legend.** Q's explains the brand marks (monitor = app, glass pane = Vera) as well as the colours. P only says "Green is Vera."
- **Logo and status pill.** Q's monitor and cursor and the black "Vera is ready" pill are more memorable than P's house tile and pale pill, and they cost nothing in use.
- **To do group labels** ("OVERDUE · 3" in small caps) are a touch firmer than P's.

**4. What is still wrong with P, most important first.**
1. **No identity to speak of.** The house icon plus cream/green/serif is interchangeable with many apps, and Vera is just a letter. It needs a real mark and a Vera character that stays legible at 28 px. Q's direction, with a simpler avatar, would do.
2. **The kids' path on phone.** Wishes, their own page, is missing from the five-tab bar. Add it, or replace a tab with it for the kids' accounts.
3. **Slashed-zero numerals throughout body text** ("1Ø am", "$2.ØØ"). Switch to a standard zero in the body face.
4. **Small, low-contrast secondary text** (dates, drive times, "No reminder", status chips) on desktop and phone. Raise its size and contrast for young and tired readers.
5. **Maya's crimson is too close to the "late" red**, on calendar events and avatars, against the overdue left rule. Shift Maya's hue so that red really "only means late". Also fix the truncated Quick idea placeholder on phone Ideas.
