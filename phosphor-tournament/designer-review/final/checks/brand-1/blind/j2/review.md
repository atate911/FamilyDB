# Review: FamilyDB designs P and Q

I looked at all six overviews (desktop, phone and dark for each design), then the full pages. I compared Home, To do, Plans and Ideas side by side on desktop and phone, and Home and Chat on phone in dark mode. I also read P's Chat page on desktop. The two designs share the same structure, copy, layout, type pairing (a serif for headings, a sans for body text) and warm cream palette. Most of what follows is about where they differ: how they show Vera and the brand, the Ideas page, and dark mode.

## P

**What it is**
A warm cream app with a strong "friendly machine" theme running through it. The logo is a green smiling monitor on a black tile, and a green cursor block follows the "FamilyDB" wordmark. The status pill is black with green monospace text ("● Vera is ready"). Vera's avatar is a small dark tile of green glyphs. Her timestamps and the "Added to Plans · Thu 1 Oct, 10 am–2 pm · for Sam and Alex" receipts are set in monospace, and in dark mode her chat bubbles get a green outline. The Ask Vera panel is a very dark forest green. Ideas opens with a black radar-style chart, "How far each idea is from home", with rings at 30 min, 1 h, 2 h and 3 h and numbered green dots, next to a numbered list. Settings ends with a "Colours in FamilyDB" key that writes the system down: each person has a colour, "Vera is a little pane of glass full of glyphs. Green light belongs to her, and to FamilyDB", and "The smiling monitor is the app itself, never Vera."

**What works**
- The identity is consistent across pages. Green light on black always means Vera or the app: the pill, the cursor, the glyph avatar and the radar. Settings explains that rule to the family.
- The Ideas radar gives an overview of the ten looked-up ideas that a grid of cards can't. The south cluster (2–6, all about 20 minutes away) and the far trips (Mount St. Helens to the north-north-east, Cannon Beach to the west) are readable at a glance.
- The shared parts are good, and they are the same in Q. Home leads with a plain-sentence summary ("Roller rink tomorrow, and three to-dos are late") whose key phrases are links. The Next up card has a tear-off date block and "Leave by 12:30 pm". Each person has a coloured initial that is used the same way everywhere (Sam blue, Alex purple, Maya pink, Theo orange). Overdue to-dos get a red left rule and "6 days late" in red. "Surprise · hidden from Maya" chips show what is kept from a child. The phone has a 5-tab bottom bar with a badge on To do.
- The To do page has an uppercase tracked "OVERDUE · 3" label. It separates the groups clearly and matches "NO DATE · 1".

**What doesn't**
- Vera's glyph avatar does not work at the size it is used. At about 32 px in chat it is a near-black square of noise: it reads as a broken thumbnail, not a character a 9-year-old would talk to. In dark mode it almost disappears into the background.
- Monospace on content the family needs to read costs legibility. On the phone, the Oaks Park receipt in Chat breaks into "Added to Plans · / Sun 4 Oct, 1 pm · / for Maya and Theo", with a lot of empty space beside it, and the radar labels are tiny mono type. It reads like a developer tool more than a family app.
- Dark mode weakens the main action. The Ask Vera panel turns near-black, the same as the page, so Home loses its one coloured block. Primary buttons ("Add an idea", "Add a plan", "+ Add") flip to cream pills, so the dark-mode green belongs only to Vera.
- On the phone, the radar moves to the bottom of Ideas, below all twelve cards. Its five clustered dots (2–6) overlap into one blob.
- Issues it shares with Q:
  - The "+ Add" button in the Add a to-do card stops short of the right edge of the field above it.
  - The phone Quick idea placeholder is cut off ("e.g. a hike someone m").
  - The "Surprise · hidden from Theo/Maya" chips wrap onto two lines in narrow cards.
  - The pink in the calendar is not explained.

**Mark**
7/10. A distinctive, well-built system on a solid shared base, but its signature elements (the glyph avatar, monospace receipts, dark-mode treatment) cost readability exactly where the kids and the main action need it.

## Q

**What it is**
The same app with a quieter skin. The logo is a white house with a yellow dot (a sun or light) on a green tile, and the wordmark stands alone. The status pill is pale mint with dark green sans text. Vera is a white circle with a serif "V" (green in chat). The Ask Vera panel is a mid teal-green, and it stays green in dark mode. All text is sans or serif; there is no monospace anywhere. Ideas is a plain three-column card grid, with no overview chart. The "Colours in FamilyDB" key in Settings is shorter: the person colours, "Green is Vera", and "6 days late: Red only means late, or broken."

**What works**
- Everything is readable at a glance. Vera's receipts in chat are set in the body sans ("Added to Plans · Sun 4 Oct, 1 pm · for Maya and Theo"). The "V" avatar reads as a character at every size, light and dark.
- Dark mode keeps the hierarchy. The Ask Vera panel stays a clearly coloured teal block on Home, and primary buttons stay green ("+ Add", "Add a plan", the chat Send button). The to-do red rules and "days late" text stay strong.
- The colour key states a rule a child can learn: red only means late or broken.
- The house-and-sun logo suits a family product better than a monitor does. The "3 steps left" badge sits on the same line as "Finish setting up", which keeps that card tighter than P's.
- It has all the shared strengths listed under P: the summary sentence, the Next up card, person colours, surprise chips and the bottom tab bar.

**What doesn't**
- The look is generic: a cream page, a teal panel, a yellow Send button. Without the name you could not tell it from many other calm productivity apps. Vera's "V" circle has the same form as the family's initial avatars, so she looks like a fifth family member rather than the assistant.
- Ideas has nothing that summarises the ideas. Twelve similar cards repeat "about 22 min drive, south"-style lines, and you have to read every card to compare them.
- On the phone, the mint "Ready" pill sits visibly higher than the logo and the "S" avatar in the header, on every page.
- The sentence-case "Overdue · 3" and "No date · 1" group labels are weaker than P's tracked caps. "No date · 1" looks like body text.
- It shares the same faults as P: the short "+ Add" button, the clipped Quick idea placeholder on the phone, the two-line surprise chips, and the Oaks Park receipt title in phone chat wrapping to "roller / rink".

**Mark**
7.5/10. Less memorable than P, but every page stays clear and readable on a phone, in dark mode and for a 9-year-old, which matters more for this family.

## Between them

### 1. Which would you build for this family, and the three reasons that decided it

**Q.**
1. **Readability for the kids.** Q never puts information the family needs in monospace or in a glyph texture. Vera's plan receipts, timestamps and the status pill are in plain type, and her avatar is a clear "V". In P the same receipts break into three ragged mono lines on the phone, and Vera is a dark noise square.
2. **Dark mode keeps the hierarchy.** Q's Ask Vera panel stays teal and its primary buttons stay green, so Home still has one clear place to start. In P's dark mode the panel turns the same near-black as the page and the main buttons turn cream.
3. **A family character, not a machine.** Q's house logo and plain green Vera feel like a family product. P's monitor, cursor block and terminal pill say "database", which is more a joke for the parent who set it up than something the kids would feel at home in.

### 2. Which has the stronger identity, and does that identity cost anything in use?

**P, clearly.** The smiling monitor, the green cursor after the wordmark, the black terminal-style "Vera is ready" pill, the glyph avatar and the radar on Ideas all come from one idea (green light on black glass). Settings even writes the rule down. A family would recognise it and remember it.

It does cost something in use:
- the glyph avatar is unreadable at chat size and almost disappears in dark mode;
- the monospace receipts and timestamps are harder for the 9-year-old to read and wrap badly on the phone;
- in dark mode, keeping green for Vera alone leaves the Ask Vera panel and the primary buttons without colour, so the main action is weaker.

The radar is the one identity element that adds value rather than costing it.

### 3. Where the other one is better, if anywhere

- **Ideas, desktop:** P's "How far each idea is from home" radar plus its numbered nearest-first list. It is the only summary of the ideas in either design. Q has nothing equivalent.
- **Settings, "Colours in FamilyDB":** P explains that the app and Vera are different things ("the smiling monitor is the app itself, never Vera"). Q's Vera avatar looks like a family member and nothing tells the family otherwise.
- **To do:** P's tracked uppercase "OVERDUE · 3" / "NO DATE · 1" group labels separate the groups more clearly than Q's sentence case.
- **Phone header:** P's "Ready" pill is vertically centred with the logo and avatar. Q's is not.

### 4. What is still wrong with Q, most important first

1. **Vera doesn't look like an assistant.** Her white or green "V" circle is the same shape as Sam, Alex, Maya and Theo's initial avatars, so in the family chat she looks like a fifth person. She needs her own shape (not a letter in a circle) that stays readable at 24–32 px. P's glyph tile is the right idea, at the wrong size and contrast.
2. **Ideas needs an overview.** Twelve near-identical cards don't help anyone compare distance, time or who each idea is for. Add a simpler, readable version of P's distance view, placed at the top on the phone as well.
3. **Phone layout slips:**
   - the "Ready" pill sits too high in the header on every page;
   - the Quick idea placeholder is cut off ("e.g. a hike someone m");
   - "Surprise · hidden from Maya/Theo" chips wrap onto two lines;
   - the Oaks Park receipt card in chat wraps "roller / rink" and leaves dead space.
4. **The calendar's colour meaning is not explained.** On Plans, Oaks Park and the Nutcracker are pink and the other plans are beige. Pink seems to follow Maya, but Oaks Park is for both Maya and Theo, and nothing on the page says what the colours mean.
5. **Small alignment and label issues.** The "+ Add" button in the Add a to-do card stops short of the right edge of the field above it, on desktop and phone. The "No date · 1" group label is too weak to read as a heading.
