# Stage 5: the brand

**What I kept**

- **The mark** replaces the house everywhere: sidebar, phone bar, sign-in, Settings' key, the favicon and the home-screen icons (`brand/`: SVG, 16, 32, 180, 512). It is a smiling monitor in one phosphor stroke on a charcoal square. The 16 px version is hand-pixelled so the smile survives.
- **The wordmark:** "FamilyDB" in soft Fraunces, followed by a lit cursor that blinks slowly.
- **Vera's screen** replaces the V everywhere she appears. It is a small pane of blurred glyphs that falls only while she answers and goes dark when she can't. It is hidden from screen readers and stops moving under reduced motion.
- **Her voice in a mono:** JetBrains Mono at 14 px, only for her times, receipt lines, the status pill and the panes. Her messages stay Atkinson, like the family's.
- **One instrument, on Ideas:** a glass pane that plots ideas by drive time and direction from home. Every dot is named in the list beside it.
- **Brand moments:** sign-in, a first empty day, the grown-ups page (the mark alone, glowing) and a new `404.html`.
- **Dark mode is phosphor at night:** charcoal, cream ink, phosphor for Vera, amber for what needs a look. The family's buttons and links turn cream, so green at night only ever means Vera, the brand, today or "all good".
- **A deeper, glassier green** for the Ask card by day.
- **Type is back to A1:** h1 42, h2 23, h3 19, lede 18, bold 18 px to-do titles, date tiles 34/28/21, money 30, capitals back where A1 had them, meta line height 1.5.
  - Only three changes from A1 remain, each fixing a named problem: open-zero figures for numbers that stand alone, 13 px as the smallest step, and tag line height for wrapping on the phone.
  - The one Fraunces choice is softness 100 on `h1` and the wordmark.
  - `type.html` shows the scale, plus six real lines as A1 drew them above the same lines now.

**How it changes the feel**

By day it is still the Kitchen Table the family chose, and with the type restored it reads like A1 again. What's new is a few small panes of dark glass with green light: the mark in the corner, Vera's screen wherever she talks, the pill that breathes while she's ready, and one dial on Ideas. That pairing is what nobody else has. Paper belongs to the family, and the glass belongs to the machine helping them. Vera feels present without a face: a screen that is busy when she is. At night the app becomes the glass, and only her things glow.

**Checks:** 24 pages, 96 shots (light and dark, desktop and phone), all looked at, fixed and rendered again. Fonts load on every shot, nothing is wider than the screen, and no page has `style=""` or `<script>`. Every contrast pair is recomputed in STANDARD §7; the lowest text pair at night is 6.5:1.
