What the marks say

The three judges mostly agree. Their rankings correlate at 0.8 to 0.9. The tomato and green Kitchen Tables lead (8.5 and 8.3), then Afterglow with scanlines (7.7) and Lit Manual, Pared (7.5). At the bottom are the monitor sketch (2.7), the fade sketch (4.0), today’s page (4.3) and the committee’s four finalists (4.8 to 5.5). Two of the three judges put Kitchen Table (green) first or second, and the third put it third. Its marks were 8, 8 and 9.

Look and finish are tangled. Among the mockups, warm light designs average 8.1 and dark phosphor ones 7.4, a gap of 0.6. That is about the size of the judges’ own disagreement on a single design, and there are only three designs on each side. The much bigger gap is between dark mockups (7.4) and dark designs built on the real app (5.4). But the real-app builds that got the most care, Pared (7.5) and Signal (7.2), score as high as the dark mockups. What pulls the rest down is mostly pages that were never styled: raw Status tables, native date and select controls, phone layouts that overflow. It is not the green.

Where the judges liked the retro screen. The dark designs that scored best made the screen carry information. Pared’s dial reads “next in 1 day” and its stem runs into the Coming up list. Afterglow’s radar plots ideas by drive time with a legend, and it adds “Leave by 12:30”. Where the radar was only decoration, repeated on several pages, they marked it down. Fourteen of the twenty designs share one structure (top bar, composer beside a CRT screen, the same Settings and Status), and the judges said much of that variety is surface.

The identity model. One judge pointed out that the top-bar dark designs assume nobody is signed in: they ask “who is asking?” in a dropdown. The sidebar designs, light or dark, know it is Sam, greet Sam, and close loops (“Vera will ask how it went”). That is a product decision more than a look, and it applies whichever look wins.

What the modes say

A short brief beat a long process. The three designers who got only the description average 8.1, the highest of any mode. Telling them the Phosphor idea cost little (7.4). The committee tournament’s four finalists average 5.1, below every from-scratch design and both team entries. The earlier blind panel had marked the same four 6 to 7.5, but against a smaller, easier field. Today’s page is 18th of 20.

Real-app builds can compete. One designer working from review notes (Pared, 7.5) and one team entry (Signal, 7.2) show that a design on the live templates can score with the mockups. The six sketches scored lowest as a group (4.9), which fits their being quick.

What the marks do not say

One kind of mind. Every designer and every judge is the same kind of AI. Cream paper, a soft serif and a tomato accent is a look AI design output tends to produce, so the judges may be rewarding what they would have drawn. That says little about what this family would pick. The family’s own taste is the verdict that counts, and it chose Kitchen Table (green).

Pictures, not pages. The judges saw screenshots of seven pages. They could not see motion, hover, focus or real text rendering, and said so. The glow and scanline designs could move by up to a point either way.

Small groups, one prompt each. Three designers per from-scratch group, one set of instructions, and every warm design is a mockup. A warm design built on the real pages has not been made, so look and medium are not separated.
