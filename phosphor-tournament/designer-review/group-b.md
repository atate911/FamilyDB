# Designer's review, group B

Seen against today's page (`00-current`). Today's page is a decent dark dashboard: rounded cards, one green
CRT panel with a radar on Home and Ideas, and everything else sans-serif and grey. It has no structure
drawn in line, and the glow is an ornament in one corner. All four designs below try to make the phosphor
mean more than that. They go about it in different ways.

---

## Desk Terminal, Even Dock (`r20-mut-1`)

**The idea in one line**
Every page is a desk: a small green monitor in a left-hand dock sums up the page, and ruled tables on the right hold the detail.

**What works**
- The dock is a real system. Status, Plans, To do, Ideas, Chat and Settings all have the same left column, and the monitor at the top of it always answers the page's question: what it cost, what's next, what's late, where things are.
- The tables are good. Ideas is a proper ruled list with Drive, Way, For and # columns, and the numbers in # are the same numbers as the blips on the radar. That is information design, not decoration.
- To do reads in one glance. The amber dash before "was due 6 days ago" and the thin left bracket tying the three late rows together say "late" without a red box.
- The glow is mostly kept to the monitors. The body of each page is calm.

**What doesn't**
- Every monitor is a different instrument: a dot chart on Home and Plans, a radar on Ideas, a needle gauge on Status, ASCII bar meters on To do, a glyph portrait on Chat. Each one is nice. Together they read as a cabinet of gadgets, not one hand.
- The Next up monitor has a perspective floor grid under its chart. That is synthwave and Tron, not a 1979 terminal. It is off-motif.
- There is document apparatus everywhere. Sections are numbered in rings (1 Finish setting up, 2 Coming up, 3 To do, 4 Lately added, 5 The kids' wishes), and an "ON THIS PAGE" index repeats them inside the dock on Home and Status. A family home page is not a manual.
- "Ready · OpenAI · gpt-6-luna · $2.00 a day" sits at the foot of every dock, on every page. Status news on the To do page is noise.
- Settings shows the nine parts twice: once as a sidebar in the dock, once as the main list beside it.
- On the phone the Plans list breaks: "Oaks / Park / roller / rink" wraps one word to a line in a column that is too narrow. That is a bug, and it would be the first thing a parent sees.
- Kind labels come in yellow, orange, blue, teal and pink. On Ideas the list goes rainbow down its right-hand side.

**What's beautiful**
- The Status gauge. It is a needle on an arc with fine ticks, "LIMIT 2.00" boxed at the red end, "$0.00 TODAY" in big phosphor type, and "AVERAGE DAY $0.04" set small beside it. This is the best single instrument in the four designs.
- The Ideas radar with numbered blips (5, 9, 4, 7, 2, 1, 3) on thin rings labelled "15 min", "45 min" and "2 h by road", with a small north arrow. The line art is fine and every mark is data.
- The mono "What's on your mind?" on its dark slab, with the solid block cursor and "LISTENING · watching 4 plans" under it. It is a true terminal prompt.

**What's ugly**
- The Tron floor under the Plans chart.
- The tiny hours chart (09, 12, 15, 18, 21 against the days 4, 9, 17, 24). You can't read it at that size, and the four rows of the list right below say the same thing in plain words.
- The Kind filter on Ideas: eleven pill buttons, each with its own icon and a little green LED in the selected one, stacked four rows deep in a narrow dock. It is a wall of chrome.
- The ringed numbers on the section headings look like a form from a tax office.

**Committee check**
The dock is the idea. The numbered sections, the "On this page" index, the floor grid, the repeated status footer and the five different instrument types were each added later. You can see the seams. Numbered sections came from someone who wanted wayfinding. The footer came from someone who wanted status visible everywhere. The floor came from someone who wanted more glow. None of them helps the dock.

**What I'd do**
1. Remove the "On this page" index and the ringed section numbers. Headings are enough.
2. Remove the Ready footer from every dock except Status.
3. Remove the floor grid. Replace the hours dot chart with the plain Next up text, or with a single date rail.
4. Pick two instrument types (the gauge and the radar) and let the other docks be plain phosphor text on the same screen.
5. On Settings, keep the sidebar or the list, not both.
6. Fix the phone Plans list.
7. Bring the kind colours down to one muted companion colour, and use icons to tell the kinds apart.

**Verdict**
6/10. A good idea, the docked monitor, buried under the kit that was added to it.

## Track Diagram, Rail Keys (`r19-mut-2`)

**The idea in one line**
A transit diagram: thin rails with stops run through the pages, from the heading ring down into the content, along the to-do due dates, and down the chat.

**What works**
- When the rail carries time, it is excellent. On To do, a vertical rail has an amber stop per due date ("was due 6 days ago", "5 days ago", "2 days ago") and a green "now" stop below them. The Chat page has a vertical rail with a stop at each message time. In both places the line art is the structure, which is exactly what the owner asked for.
- Home's Coming up is a horizontal rail: a green "now" dot, then stops for Mount St. Helens, Nutcracker and Cannon Beach.
- The big serif headings with a count ("Status 6 working", "Ideas 12 in all", "Plans 5 this month") are confident and editorial. They give each page a front-page feel.
- The Next up monitor on Home is a good size, and its radar is quiet.

**What doesn't**
- There are three typefaces in one heading: "What's on your mind?" is sans, with "mind" in a dotted pixel face, glowing, plus a block cursor. Serif headings sit elsewhere, and mono inside the monitors. Each face is fine. Mixed inside a single line, they look like a sampler.
- The heading ring has an elbow line that drops down and turns right under the subtitle. On Status it then draws a box around nothing. It is a bracket with no contents.
- The count after each heading changes colour by page: lilac 12 on Ideas, pale blue 5 on Plans, yellow 4 on To do. That is a colour code with no key, and the yellow is off-motif.
- Plans month has a ring clock beside the calendar ("3 plans", weekday ticks, "2 past, Today, 1 ahead"). It is lovely line art, but it says less than the calendar right next to it does.
- To do has a full-width, glowing green "Add something to do" bar across the page, and Plans has "Put something on the calendar". They are the loudest things on those pages, and they are for secondary actions.
- Settings has no rail at all. It is still today's card grid. The idea stops at the one page where a list of nine parts and their state would suit it best.
- The filter keys on Ideas (Any, Activity, Day trip... each with a tiny LED) take up as much space as today's dropdowns did, and are busier.

**What's beautiful**
- The To do rail. Small amber stops with the dates set in two lines of tiny type to their left, then a bright green "now" stop at the end. It is the cleanest drawing of "late" in any of the four designs.
- The Chat rail. Times hang off a thin line on the left, Sam and Alex's messages sit to the right of it with their initials in rings, Vera's sit to the left of it. A conversation drawn as a timetable.
- "Ideas 12" in the serif against "IN ALL" in small spaced caps.

**What's ugly**
- The pixel-font "mind" glowing inside a sans sentence. It reads as a mistake.
- The full-width green bar on To do.
- The empty elbow bracket under the Status heading.
- The rainbow of counts in the headings.

**Committee check**
The rail is one idea, and a good one. The serif headings with counts are a second idea. The pixel-font word is a third. The ring clock is a fourth. The rail keys with LEDs are a fifth. The giant add bars are a sixth. Settings never got the first. That is the definition of a camel. The rail alone would have made this design.

**What I'd do**
1. Drop the pixel face. Set "What's on your mind?" in one face and let the cursor carry the glow.
2. Shrink the add bars to an ordinary button by the heading.
3. Remove the ring clock from Plans.
4. Use one colour for every heading count: phosphor green, or plain ivory.
5. Remove the heading elbow wherever it frames nothing. Let the rail start where the content starts.
6. Put Settings on a rail: one line, a stop per part, amber for "needs a look".
7. Go back to plain dropdowns for the Ideas filters, or keep only the Kind keys.

**Verdict**
6/10. The best single piece of line art in the group (the To do and Chat rails) is surrounded by five other ideas that weren't asked for.

## Folio, Fastext (`x14b-folio-cross`)

**The idea in one line**
A teletext magazine: every page has a page number (P100, P300, P900), serif display numbers carry the counts, and a small CRT screen in the margin is that page's teletext summary.

**What works**
- It feels the most like one hand. Every page has the same parts: the page-number strip, a serif title with an old-style count ("Status 6 of 13 on · $0.00 today of $2.00", "Things to do 4 open · 3 late"), the monitor in the left margin, and a ruled table on the right.
- The numbers are beautiful, and they matter. The big light serif day numbers on Plans month, "27 MIN", "2h45 MIN" and "1h40 MIN" on Ideas, and the big date numerals on the Home Plans rail all make the data the art.
- To do is a model. A Due column with "27 SEP", "28 SEP" and "1 OCT" in serif, a thin rail with a dot per row, a green NOW line across the table, and Renew library cards under it with "no date". The quick-add row sits at the top of the table itself ("What needs doing... · More: who, when, a reminder"), not in a separate panel.
- Settings is a single list, with "Sign-in and security · Needs a look" moved to the top with an amber rule. It is honest and short.
- The Status spending tape (a scale marked $0.01, $0.10, $0.50, $1.00, $2.00 on a log-like ruler) is a clever way to show a tiny number against a limit.

**What doesn't**
- The teletext page-number strip ("P300 · FamilyDB · Ideas ... Sat 3 Oct") is the conceit made literal, and it costs a line on every page. The "Contents" block at the bottom of Home, with 310 Restaurants, 600 Wishes, 700 Memory and so on, says what the nav bar already says.
- Home repeats itself. Under the Vera box there are four tiles (Plans 4, To do 4, Late 3, Ideas 12) with little coloured bars on top (the "Fastext" keys). Each of those numbers then appears again as the count on its own section right next to it.
- Orange carries too many meanings: "3 LEFT" on setup, "3 late", "Restaurant", the Late tile and the hover edge. It drifts towards the brown the brief warned against.
- There are two radars: one on Home beside the Plans rail ("each mark is a plan, by its day") and another on Ideas. The Home one decorates a list that already makes its point.
- The Chat monitor shows a scramble of glyphs ("4 lines in this conversation"). It is charming but means nothing.

**What's beautiful**
- "2h45 MIN" on Ideas, with the h raised and small, in the serif. The best number in the tournament.
- Plans month: thin light serif day numbers (28, 29, 30, 1, 2) with the 3 in phosphor green inside a single green-ruled cell.
- The green NOW rule across the To do table, with the dot rail feeding into it.
- "Tell Vera what's on." set in the serif on a dark slab, with only "on." glowing and the block cursor after it.

**What's ugly**
- The four Fastext tiles on Home. Short coloured bars over boxed numbers look like a tablet game's score panel, and they compete with the Vera box they sit in.
- "P900 · FamilyDB · Status" in green mono on a pill: one more line of chrome above every title.
- The orange "Restaurant" against the cyan "Activity" in the Home Ideas list.

**Committee check**
The magazine idea is consistent, and that is rare here. The accretion is the teletext costume laid over it: the page numbers, the Contents block and the Fastext colour keys. They are three nods to the same period reference, and they were added to prove the theme rather than to help anyone find anything. The second radar on Home is the other one.

**What I'd do**
1. Remove the four Fastext tiles from Home. The sections already have counts.
2. Remove the Contents block, or keep it only on the phone where the nav is hidden.
3. Remove the page-number strip, or keep only the date and drop "P300". The serif title is already the masthead.
4. Remove the radar from Home's Plans. The date rail is enough.
5. Keep amber for late and needs-a-look only. Show kinds in grey with their icons.
6. Give the Chat monitor something true to show (who's talking, the last time), or drop it.

**Verdict**
7/10. The most disciplined of the four, with the best typography of numbers, wearing a teletext costume it doesn't need.

## Sill Line, Wired (`x14b-sill-cross`)

**The idea in one line**
One wire: a thin line with nodes runs through every page, and things hang off it like cards clipped to a line, with "now" always a lit node on it.

**What works**
- The idea is applied everywhere and it is the same idea everywhere. On Home, the Coming up cards hang from a baseline by drop lines, with a lit "now" at its left. On To do, a vertical wire with a "now" node splits late from the rest. On Chat, a wire with a node per time ends in "your turn" at the message box. On Settings, a wire with a status dot per part, amber where something needs a look. On Lately added, a wire with an icon ring per idea.
- Ideas replaces the radar with "THE LINE FROM HOME": a single line from home marked 15 min, 30 min, 1 h and 3 h, with the places as stops along it. It is simpler than a radar and says the same thing. That subtraction is in the spirit of the brief.
- The Status instrument panel is one bezel holding LED lamps in rows (Who answers, Keys, Connected), the counts "3 OF 4", "1 OF 4", "2 OF 5", and a needle gauge. Solid lamps, dashed rings and a half-lit amber lamp say on, off and needs a look without words.
- The condensed display face for "What needs a plan?", with only "plan" glowing, is strong and plain.
- The "Or start with" list is drawn on the wire, with arrows.

**What doesn't**
- The Ideas line is linear. Everything near home crowds into the first fifth ("Pumpkin patch · 18 min" in a knot of stops around 15 to 30 min), and the far right is empty to the 3 h tick. It wants a log scale, or a scale in steps.
- The Plans month draws a "now" wire from the 3 across to the 4. A wire laid over a grid fights the grid.
- To do and Status need a legend in the footer ("now, to come, done, needs a look"). If the symbols need a key on every page, there are too many of them.
- Kind words are coloured: activity yellow, restaurant orange, outing teal, show pink, trip blue. In the Ideas table and Lately added they turn the right-hand edge into confetti, and the yellow rings around the icons warm the whole list.
- On Home the three hanging cards and the Next up monitor are four different heights hanging from one line, and the drop lines are of different lengths. The idea calls for an even rhythm.
- On the phone the wire takes a margin the narrow screen can't spare, and the Coming up cards are cut off ("Mount St. Hele...").

**What's beautiful**
- The Home Coming up rail. A lit node labelled "now", a thin line, and the Next up monitor clipped to it with its own little lead coming down from its corner. The CRT is wired into the page rather than parked on it.
- The Status lamps: "Chat, Digest, Lookups" glowing, "Fallback" an empty ring, "Claude" and "Gemini" dashed rings, "Page" half lit.
- The Settings wire, with green dots running down the middle and an amber dot at Messages and at Sign-in and security. You could read the state of the whole install from across the room.
- "your turn" in mono beside the last node, at the message box on Chat.

**What's ugly**
- The knot of stops at the left end of the Ideas line.
- The rainbow of kind words.
- The legend in the To do footer.
- "Finish setting up" squashed into a strip on Home, with three numbered rings and "Next: choose your own password" at the far right. It is a third rail on a page that already has two.

**Committee check**
Less than the others. The wire is the one idea and almost everything serves it. The accretions are small: the legend, the coloured kind words, a setup strip that became another rail, and a "now" wire on the calendar that only exists because every page was told it must have one.

**What I'd do**
1. Put the Ideas line on a log or stepped scale so the places spread out.
2. Remove the coloured kind words. Use the icon in a grey ring.
3. Remove the "now" wire from Plans month. Let the today cell glow instead.
4. Remove the footer legend, and cut the node types down to what reads without one (lit, empty, amber).
5. Make the Home cards one height, with equal drop lines.
6. On the phone, move the wire to a thin rule at the edge and let the cards use the full width.
7. Turn the setup strip into one line: "Next: choose your own password →".

**Verdict**
7/10. The clearest single idea of the four, carried through nearly every page, held back by a linear scale, a colour habit and a little over-wiring.
