# Brief

You are an expert creative graphic and interface designer.

The work: sixteen designs for the web page of a family planning app. A family talks to an assistant
called Vera, and the app keeps their ideas (places to go, things to do), plans, to-dos and settings.
The family is two parents and two kids, on phones and laptops. Each design is a complete visual
treatment of the same app, showing the same content. Designs are known only by a code (X01 to X16).
They come from different designers. Treat all sixteen alike.

Each design has its own folder, `X01/` to `X16/`, with:
- `overview-desktop.jpg`: its seven pages on a desktop (Home, Chat, Ideas, Plans, To do, Status,
  Settings), the top part of each at half size. Read this first.
- `overview-phone.jpg`: the same seven pages on a phone, top part, half size.
- `desktop/<page>.jpg`: each page full length at 1280 px wide (home, chat, ideas, plans, todo,
  status, settings).
- `phone/<page>.jpg`: each page full length on a phone.

Look at the overviews for all sixteen. Then open full-size pages wherever you want to check
something: at least Home for every design, and as many other pages as you need to judge fairly. Do
not judge from the overview alone where the full page shows something different. Be specific about
what you see; do not invent anything you cannot see.

Write your work to `review.md` in this folder, in two parts.

## Part 1: one review per design, in code order

For each design use exactly these headings:
### <code>
**What it is** (the design in one line, in your own words)
**What works**
**What doesn't**
**What's beautiful** (name the page and the element)
**What's ugly** (name the page and the element)
**Mark** (out of 10, as a piece of graphic and interface design, and one sentence why)

## Part 2: across all sixteen

1. A ranking from best to worst, with your mark beside each. Use the full range of the scale.
2. Your top three, and why each is there.
3. Your bottom three, and why.
4. How many different design ideas are there really? Group the designs into families, and say
   what separates one family from another.
5. If this family could have only one, which, and what would you change in it first?
6. What would you take from the others and add to your pick? Name the design, the page and the
   element.
7. Anything that most of the designs share, good or bad.
8. How sure are you? Say which of your marks you would change if you saw the pages in a real
   browser instead of pictures.

When you are done, reply with a short summary and your ranking.
