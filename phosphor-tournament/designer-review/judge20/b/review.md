# FamilyDB: review of twenty designs

For each design I looked at the desktop overview, the phone overview and the full-length desktop Home. Where something needed checking I also opened other full pages (for example X05's phone Status). All marks are for the work as graphic and interface design.

---

## Part 1: the designs

### X01
**What it is** A black and phosphor-green "terminal" dashboard. Several pages carry a green CRT panel with a radar sweep, and Home has a monospace ticker strip.

**What works** The hierarchy on Home is clear. A big sentence ("Tell Vera what to **plan.**") sits over a wide composer, with the CRT "NEXT UP" panel beside it. The ticker strip (TODAY · PLANS 4 · TO DO 4 · LATE 3 · IDEAS 12) gives a one-line summary. Amber is kept for lateness ("was due 6 days ago") and for the setup bar. The Plans month grid is clean.

**What doesn't** The radar appears on Home, Ideas, To do and Status, and on most of them it decorates rather than says anything. The kids' wishes card is a large empty box holding two lines ("Maya 0 open"). On the phone To do page, the title "Book the campsite at Silver Falls" runs into the Edit button. Status is three equal grey columns of text.

**What's beautiful** Home, the ticker strip. Its mono labels and right-aligned big numerals read like an instrument.

**What's ugly** Phone To do, where the task title collides with "Edit".

**Mark** 4.5. It is the competent baseline the others start from, but its effects are repeated more than designed, and the phone has a visible collision.

### X02
**What it is** The same terminal look, made calmer: coming plans and to-dos become timelines with a green "NOW" node.

**What works** On Home, "Coming up" and "To do" are both vertical timelines. Overdue to-dos sit above the NOW line on amber dots, so lateness is shown by position as well as colour. The suggestion chips say where each prompt will land ("→ Plans", "→ To do", "→ Ideas"). A caption under the radar explains it ("The nearer the day, the nearer the middle"). In Chat, Vera's replies are green-bordered monospace, so she reads as a different kind of speaker. The right-hand nav uses text labels (Memory, Family, Status, Settings) instead of bare icons.

**What doesn't** Settings has roughly a third of a screen of empty black under its 3×3 grid. "Lately added" is a plain table with no visual grouping. The kids' wishes column is half empty. The Status cost table is unstyled.

**What's beautiful** Home, the To do timeline. The three amber dots stack above the green NOW node, with "Renew library cards" alone below it.

**What's ugly** Settings, the large dead area under the card grid.

**Mark** 6.5. It takes one good idea (time as a line with "now" on it) and applies it consistently.

### X03
**What it is** The most finished of the top-nav terminal designs. Every page title carries a count, and the colours have a stated meaning.

**What works** The footer legend ("● lit now or live · ● amber needs you") turns the palette into a rule, and the pages follow it. Titles carry counts: "Ideas 12 listed", "Status 6 of 13 lit", "Settings 9 parts · 1 needs a look". Settings moves the one item that needs attention to the top. Plans pairs a CRT agenda ("OCTOBER 2026 · 5 busy days") with the month grid, and the phone shows only the agenda, which is the right call. To do has a small CRT bar chart (TALLY). Chat gets Family / Maya / Theo tabs.

**What doesn't** Much of the supporting text is small grey monospace, about 11 px at desktop. On the phone, Home puts the mic button alone on a row above a full-width Send. Ideas puts a tall radar beside a long list, so the radar repeats the list.

**What's beautiful** Status, the analog gauge: a $0–$2 arc whose last segment turns amber, a needle at zero, and "$0.00 SPENT TODAY" under it.

**What's ugly** Phone Home, the orphaned mic button on a row of its own.

**Mark** 7.5. The terminal idea finally has a grammar of colour and counts, and the instruments carry real information.

### X04
**What it is** The X01 terminal with a condensed display face and a two-column Home.

**What works** The condensed headline has punch. The setup card lists all three steps in full. The "12 Ideas, lately added" cards get a coloured left edge by kind (amber restaurant, teal trip, green outing). To do adds a left rail with a big "4 OPEN" and Open / Done / Cancelled / All filters, which is the best filter treatment in the plain terminal group.

**What doesn't** Home shows the same four plans twice. The CRT "NEXT UP" panel now lists all four, and the "Coming up" timeline directly below repeats them. The right column then runs out, leaving about 450 px of empty space beside "To do" and "The kids' wishes". The rest of the site is essentially X01.

**What's beautiful** To do, the left rail: a huge condensed "4" over "OPEN", with the status filters stacked under it.

**What's ugly** Home, the duplicated plan list and the dead right column under it.

**Mark** 5. One good page and one clumsy Home, with nothing new elsewhere.

### X05
**What it is** The terminal look reduced to text: dotted leaders, underlined links and very few boxes.

**What works** Home is short (about 1,090 px) and calm. The "NEXT UP" CRT uses dotted leaders ("Mount St. Helens day trip ……… Fri 9 Oct"), and an "Also" column uses the same leaders for "Something to eat · 2 to try" and the wish lists. The newest idea is green with a NEW tag. Chat is a flat log, easy to read.

**What doesn't** The "NOW" line on Home hangs above three columns where it means nothing. Status is plain bold and regular text with no structure, and looks unfinished. On the phone Status page, the cost-by-model table is wider than the screen, so the whole page renders wider than the header and tab bar, and the table is cut off at the right edge.

**What's beautiful** Home, the dotted leaders in the "Also" column. They are quiet and typographic.

**What's ugly** Phone Status, the overflowing table that breaks the page width.

**Mark** 4. The restraint is a real idea, but Status looks unstyled and the phone layout breaks.

### X06
**What it is** A different product: a sidebar app in the green-terminal palette, personal ("Hi Sam█") and built around what the family does next.

**What works** Home opens with a sentence that does the work of a dashboard: "The roller rink is tomorrow at 13:00. **3 to-dos are late.**" The Next plan card carries real planning detail ("27 min drive, south", "Leave by 12:30", Open plan / Ask Vera about it). "How did it go?" asks for Loved it / OK / Not again. The empty wish lists say what to do ("Maya can tell Vera 'I wish for…'"). Chat lists separate conversations ("Theo and Vera · parents can read"). A Vera reply carries an "Added to Plans · See it →" receipt. Status opens with a heartbeat trace and "Vera is working." Settings gives every row a status pill and has a "3 need a look" side panel with an amber "Finish setting up" button.

**What doesn't** The sidebar counts, pills, mono caps labels and boxed cards everywhere make it dense and evenly loud. The phone's quick-add date field is a raw "mm/dd/yyyy". Phone calendar chips are cut to "Pho Ore…".

**What's beautiful** Status, the ECG heartbeat monitor beside "Vera is working."

**What's ugly** Home, the setup card: a giant amber "3" plus three bordered tiles inside a bordered panel, which is too much frame for three links.

**Mark** 7.5. It is the first design to treat this as a family app rather than a server console. The visuals are a little crowded.

### X07
**What it is** The terminal pushed to Tron: numbered caps navigation, a circuit trace down the page and a perspective grid floor.

**What works** It has real atmosphere. The condensed "WHAT'S ON YOUR **MIND?**" uses a scanline texture in the green word. The vertical trace joins the sections, and the footer ends in a glowing "UP" node over a grid floor. The live "NOW 06:58" chip on the timeline is a nice touch. On Status, the analog VU needle meter is lovely. The To do timeline puts the due age ("6 days ago") beside each node.

**What doesn't** The numbering contradicts itself. The nav says 00 Home … 05 Wishes, but the Home sections run 05, 03, 04, 02, and Status and Settings become "C" and "D". The setup, wishes and coming-up cards sit in a four-column grid with the last column empty. The phone breaks in several places: "Finish setting up" wraps to three lines and is jammed against "ALL THE SETUP STEPS", the tab label reads "WISHE", the settings gear is clipped at the header edge, and the radar text shows solid green blocks.

**What's beautiful** Status, the VU meter: "$0.00 TODAY", LIMIT 2.00 and a sweeping needle.

**What's ugly** Phone Home, the three-line "Finish / setting / up" heading.

**Mark** 5.5. It is a memorable look, but the system under it (numbering, grid, phone) doesn't hold.

### X08
**What it is** A tidier X06: a sidebar app in green terminal with a smiling-monitor mascot. It is the most usable of the dark designs.

**What works** It keeps X06's product thinking (feedback, receipts, conversations, ECG status) and organises it better. The amber "Finish setup · 3 left" card has a segmented progress bar and an action link per step ("Choose password →", "Set up Telegram →"). To-dos carry round assignee initials. Ideas has an "Around home" map of dots and a "Free weekend? Ask Vera for a plan" card. Plans adds "How was it?" beside the calendar, and a dashed chip for "Cannon Beach, day 2". Status adds a 30-day bar chart. Settings adds an alert banner and a Family panel listing everyone with their role.

**What doesn't** Monospace is used for everything, body text included, which tires the eye on long pages. On the phone, the Plans calendar shrinks events to green bars with no text, so you cannot tell what is on any day without leaving the page.

**What's beautiful** Home, the mascot tile beside "Hello, Sam█". It is friendly and unmistakable.

**What's ugly** Phone Plans, the calendar of unlabelled bars.

**Mark** 8. It has the best structure in the dark group. Too much monospace is its only real cost.

### X09
**What it is** An editorial version of the terminal look. A round dial and a green wire tie the next plan to the timeline.

**What works** Home's hero has a round dial, "NEXT IN 1 DAY", with the plan days (4, 9, 17, 24) on its bezel. A glowing wire drops from the dial into the NOW node of "Coming up". It is the best single image in the terminal group, and it means something. The setup list ends with "Then you're ready." Ideas plots every idea on "The line from home" by drive time. The phone Plans page is an agenda with NOW placed between past and future plans. The Status title carries the money: "Status $0.00 of $2.00".

**What doesn't** On "The line from home", nearby labels collide: "7 10 6 2 8 1" on desktop, and "7106281" on the phone. On phone Home, the wire runs straight through the "Coming up" heading. Body typography is plain next to the hero. Chat's left rail is faint.

**What's beautiful** Home, the dial and the wire into the timeline.

**What's ugly** Ideas, the overlapping number labels on the drive-time line.

**Mark** 7. It has the best idea in its group, held back by a few collisions.

### X10
**What it is** A warm, light, serif-headed family app: cream paper, tomato red and a tomato-face mascot.

**What works** It reads as a family app at a glance. "Good morning, Sam." sits in a warm serif over a one-line summary. The composer has Vera's tomato avatar and a red "Send to Vera". The suggestion pills include "What's overdue?" and "Plan next weekend". The Next plan card has a tear-off calendar tile and says "Vera will ask how it went on Monday at 10:00." The dark espresso setup card with a salmon progress bar is the one strong contrast block on the page. The empty wish lists are dashed cards that say what to do. Chat lets you pick "Who's writing?" (Sam, Alex, Maya, Theo). Calendar chips are tinted by kind. The Settings side panel explains the "Kids' view".

**What doesn't** Some secondary text is pale grey on cream and low in contrast. On the phone, the suggestion pills and the "By distance" tab are cut at the right edge. The Status bar chart switches to a cold blue that belongs to no other part of the palette.

**What's beautiful** Home, the Next plan card: a red-topped "OCT 4 Sunday" tile beside a serif "Oaks Park roller rink".

**What's ugly** Status, the blue bar chart in an otherwise tomato-and-cream system.

**Mark** 8.5. It is warm and clear, and the most thoroughly considered product of the twenty.

### X11
**What it is** A cream-and-teal sibling of X10, with heavier serif headings and an illustrated Next plan card.

**What works** The Home summary links and colours its parts: "Tomorrow is the <u>roller rink</u>. **Three to-dos are overdue**". The teal Next plan card has a roller skate drawn with amber wheels. Drive times carry small compass chips (▼ S, ◀ W, NNE). To-dos say "Was due Sun 27 Sep · 6 days late" in red with a warning icon, and you can add a to-do inline. Vera's chat bubble carries linked chips for the places it mentions. The Status chart prints call counts over its bars. Settings lists "Who can sign in", with Maya marked "Kid · no settings".

**What doesn't** The phone Settings page is badly broken. The "Sign-in and security" row collapses to one word per line ("Sign- / in / and / security / Still / the…"). Phone calendar chips are cut to "Pho Or…" and "Si.. Fa.. hike".

**What's beautiful** Home, the teal Next plan card with the drawn roller skate.

**What's ugly** Phone Settings, the one-word-per-line "Sign-in and security" row.

**Mark** 7.5. The desktop is perhaps the loveliest in the set, but the phone Settings break is serious.

### X12
**What it is** The X11 content model in the dark sidebar terminal look, with ASCII humour.

**What works** The Home composer opens with Vera speaking ("Vera: Tomorrow is the roller rink at 13:00. Anything you'd like me to remember, plan or look up?"), which puts the conversation first. The amber setup band has a ring showing progress. The Next plan card has a line-drawn roller skate. The empty wish lists read "empty_ / Nothing wished for yet". On Plans, events are solid green blocks, Cannon Beach spans its two days as one bar, and "How did it go?" uses ASCII faces (":D Loved it", ":| Fine", ":( Not again"). Settings has "Do these first" and a "This server" box.

**What doesn't** The bright solid green event blocks shout on the calendar. On the phone, the calendar is again bars without words, and a suggestion chip is cut to "Rem". Small mono caps labels are everywhere.

**What's beautiful** Plans, the ":D :| :(" feedback buttons. They are witty and on theme.

**What's ugly** Plans, the slabs of saturated green on the month grid.

**Mark** 8. It is the most charming dark design and as well organised as X08.

### X13
**What it is** The terminal look as an editorial poster: a giant "MIND?" and numbered sections with colour-capped rules.

**What works** It is bold. "WHAT'S ON YOUR **MIND?**" stands about 170 px tall in scanline green. Each Home section (Vera, Ideas, Plans, To do, Wishes) opens on a thin rule with a coloured end cap (green, violet, blue, yellow, pink) and a huge condensed heading. Idea kinds are hatched swatches. To do uses tree connectors and dotted leaders.

**What doesn't** Home says "01 Vera — Nothing said yet. Start a message" while Chat shows four messages, so the page contradicts the data. Numbering mixes systems: 00–05, A–D, and phone Settings rows numbered "D1–D5". Home is about 2,560 px tall, with much of it black space. The phone tab label is clipped to "WISHE".

**What's beautiful** Home, the colour-capped section rules over huge condensed headings.

**What's ugly** Home, "Nothing said yet" in a family that has been chatting.

**Mark** 5. Striking as a poster, weak as an app.

### X14
**What it is** The terminal look on slate blue, mixing a serif display face, a pixel word and monospace, with lots of glow.

**What works** The Home composer is wired to Vera's avatar by a little cable and says "Listening · watching 4 plans". "Coming up" is a horizontal timeline. Plans has a round "3 PLANS this week" dial with past / today / ahead. Ideas turns its filters into toggle chips with LED dots. Status reuses the VU meter.

**What doesn't** Three typographic voices fight: serif "Things to do", the pixel and hatched "mind?", and mono labels. The pixel "mind?" is low in contrast and hard to read. The LED chip wall on Ideas is long. On the phone, "who is asking?" wraps to two lines inside its select.

**What's beautiful** Plans, the round week dial with its big "3".

**What's ugly** Home, the hatched pixel "mind?" in the headline.

**Mark** 5.5. Rich parts that don't agree with each other.

### X15
**What it is** The softest and most generic terminal design: rounded cards, gentle glow and X01's layout without the ticker.

**What works** It is calm and readable. The amber setup card with three circle bullets is clear. The "2 restaurants to try" card is warm.

**What doesn't** The composer's Send button sits alone on its own row at the bottom left, under the From select, with the mic far off to the right. The empty kids' wishes card is promoted above "Coming up". "Coming up" and "To do" stack in the left column, leaving about 300 px of dead space on the right. To do shows ISO timestamps ("Due 2026-09-27 19:56"). Little here is not done better in X01 or X02.

**What's beautiful** Home, the "SOMETHING TO EAT · 2 restaurants to try" card with its amber fork-and-knife tile.

**What's ugly** Home, the Send button stranded under the select.

**Mark** 3.5. Nothing is broken, but nothing is decided either.

### X16
**What it is** The disciplined terminal design: X02's timelines with a real chart in each CRT.

**What works** The Home CRT doesn't just decorate. It plots the four plans on a "now · 1 wk · 2 wk · 3 wk · 4 wk" axis ("4 plans in the next weeks · see them"). The suggestions are rows labelled "lands in Plans / To do / Ideas". The setup bar has a compact 1–2–3 stepper. Ideas has a large "THE LINE FROM HOME" chart that stacks nearby labels vertically, so they no longer collide. The idea list has big right-aligned drive times ("27 min", "2 h 10"). On Plans, days with plans get circled numbers. Status pairs a gauge with "Today: about **$0.00** of the $2.00 daily limit", three plain stats and WORKING / OFF rows. Settings puts "NEEDS A LOOK" first.

**What doesn't** It is a little austere: Home below the fold is two plain lists. Mono metadata is small. Only the Status top is very distinctive.

**What's beautiful** Home, the 4-week axis inside the NEXT UP screen.

**What's ugly** Ideas, the label stack on the line chart. It is legible, but "7 / 10 / 6 / 8" piled into a tower is awkward.

**Mark** 7. The most coherent of the top-nav terminal designs.

### X17
**What it is** The terminal look as an instrument panel. Each page has a left column of CRT instruments, and Home has an "On this page" index.

**What works** Home's "ON THIS PAGE" box lists every section with its count (3 steps · 3 plans · 4 open – 3 late · 12 ideas · 0 open) plus "Ready · OpenAI · gpt-6-luna $2.00 a day", which is a genuinely useful summary. The Ideas list is a dense ledger of name, kind, drive time, bearing arrow and number. Status has a numbered index with LED dots ("3 of 4 on").

**What doesn't** The big Home CRT plots plans by day and hour, but its lower half is an empty perspective floor. The pages are heavy, with frames inside frames. The phone is full of truncation and overflow: to-dos read "Call the…", "Book th…", "Buy Ma…"; ideas read "Cannon Beach week", "Mount St. Helens da"; and the "Add a plan" and "Add an idea" buttons and the filter fields run off the right edge.

**What's beautiful** Ideas, the ledger rows with drive time and a little bearing arrow.

**What's ugly** Phone To do, where every task title is cut to a few letters.

**Mark** 6. Excellent density on desktop, but it fails on the phone.

### X18
**What it is** A cream, pine and mustard family app. It is the most complete and best explained of the twenty.

**What works** Home: a serif "Good morning, Sam." with "three to-dos have slipped past their dates". A pine "Ask Vera" card with a white field and a mustard Send ("say it the way you'd say it at the table"). Agenda rows have kind pills (Day trip, Show, Trip). To-dos show "6 days late" in red with a pencil to edit. The setup steps each have a link and the note "Only parents see this." The "Vera today" card has On / Not set up pills.

Across the other pages:
- Chat has a "Telegram not linked yet" pill.
- Ideas offers "Not looked up yet · Look it up".
- Past plans on the calendar offer "Rate it".
- To-dos offer "No reminder · Add one".
- Status headlines "Vera is working, and well under budget." with a "Check again" button.
- Settings has a red banner, "One thing needs a look", with "Choose a password". Its tags read "Needs a look", "Could be better", "Tell her about you" and "2 not set up", and a legend card titled "Reading this list" explains them.

The phone header shows "Vera is on" and a six-tab strip ending in "More".

**What doesn't** In phone Settings, each chevron drops onto its own line under the row ("General" then "›" below). The phone calendar is coloured bars without text. A suggestion chip is cut to "Remi". Like X10, some small grey text is faint on cream.

**What's beautiful** Home, the pine "Ask Vera" card with its mustard Send and the soft circle in the corner.

**What's ugly** Phone Settings, the chevrons orphaned on separate lines.

**Mark** 9. Every state tells the family what to do next, and it looks warm doing it.

### X19
**What it is** The terminal look with teletext page numbers (P100 Home, P200 Vera … P990 Settings), and the whole composer inside a tall CRT handset.

**What works** The teletext numbering is a witty idea. The handset holding the composer, suggestions and Next-up radar is a strong object, and a wire leads from it into the NOW node of "Coming up". Home's right column is orderly. The footer legend explains green and amber.

**What doesn't** Plans is broken. The month grid has no cell borders and uneven columns (Wed and Thu are squeezed, "28 29" is jammed together), events are plain underlined text, and the agenda below is an unstyled list. On the phone, event names wrap one word per line ("Mount / St. / Helens / day / trip"). Status is unstyled bold and plain text. Under the handset on Home, about 700 px of the left column is empty. Phone To do puts the tally and filters before the page title. In Chat, Vera's avatar is a blank grey square.

**What's beautiful** Home, the green CRT handset holding the composer.

**What's ugly** Plans, the unstyled calendar.

**Mark** 2. A clever surface over pages that are visibly unfinished.

### X20
**What it is** The terminal look with a sharp grotesk headline, and a radar where distance from the centre means time.

**What works** "Tell Vera. Let's **plan.**" is the best headline type in the dark designs: tight, confident and well spaced. Under the radar, "sooner at the middle · rings a week, two, four" fixes the radar's meaning problem: it now plots time. The ticker strip uses leader lines between label and number. The suggestions are cards marked "lands in Plans / To do / Ideas". To do has a "WHO HAS WHAT" CRT bar chart by person (Sam 1, Alex 2, Household 1).

**What doesn't** On Home, the three late to-dos have nearly invisible checkboxes (dark on dark), while "Renew library cards" has a visible one, so the most urgent rows look non-interactive. The kids' wishes card spans the full width for two empty lines. The cost section on Status falls back to an unstyled table. On the phone, the to-do search field shrinks to show "de", and the status select reads "any but droppe".

**What's beautiful** Home, the headline "Tell Vera. Let's plan." in tight grotesk with green "plan."

**What's ugly** Home, the late to-dos with checkboxes you can barely see.

**Mark** 6.5. Strong type and a radar that finally means something. The details let it down.

### 2. Top three

- **X18.** It is the only design where every empty or unfinished state tells you the next action, and does it in plain words:
  - "Look it up" on ideas
  - "Rate it" on past plans
  - "Add one" for reminders
  - "Check again" on Status
  - "Choose a password" on Settings

  Its Settings legend ("Reading this list") is the best piece of interface writing in the set. The pine, mustard and cream palette is warm without being childish.
- **X10.** It has the same product thinking in a cleaner, lighter skin. The tomato mascot, serif greeting and tear-off date tile make it feel like a family's object rather than a server panel. The espresso setup card is the best-judged single contrast block of any design.
- **X08.** It is the best of the dark designs. It keeps the terminal personality (mascot, pixel headings, ECG status) but organises Home into a clear two-column plan: ask, next, ideas and wishes on the left, then setup, to-dos and spend on the right. Each setup step has its own action link.

### 3. Bottom three

- **X19.** Plans and Status look unstyled, the calendar grid has collapsed, and the phone calendar wraps event names one word per line. The teletext numbering can't cover for that.
- **X15.** It is the least decided. The Send button is stranded, empty wishes are promoted above the plans, there is dead space beside the to-dos, and there is no idea of its own.
- **X05.** Its restraint is admirable on Home, but Status is unstructured text and the phone Status page overflows the screen width.

### 4. How many ideas are there really?

Two, with a skin split that makes three families.

- **Family A: the top-nav terminal dashboard** (X01, X02, X03, X04, X05, X07, X09, X13, X14, X15, X16, X17, X19, X20). Fourteen designs. The same page set, the same top nav with icons on the right, a CRT "NEXT UP" box with a radar, amber for late, and a "kids' wishes" list. They differ in how far they push the effect:
  - **Plain:** X01, X04, X15.
  - **Time as a line with NOW on it:** X02, X16, X20, and X03 with its colour legend.
  - **Theatrical:** X07 (Tron floor), X13 (poster), X14 (serif and glow), X17 (instrument panel), X19 (teletext).
  - **Editorial with the dial:** X09.
  - **Reduced to text:** X05.
- **Family B: the sidebar family app, dark** (X06, X08, X12). It has a left sidebar with counts, a personal greeting ("Hi / Hello, Sam"), and Next plan with drive time and who is going. It adds "How did it go?" feedback, separate kid conversations, receipts in chat ("Added to Plans"), an ECG Status page, and Settings rows with status pills.
- **Family C: the same sidebar family app, light and warm** (X10, X11, X18). Family B's model with cream paper, serif headings and one warm accent each: tomato for X10, teal for X11, pine and mustard for X18.

What separates A from B and C is the model of the user. Family A is built for whoever installed the server: it shows counts, a radar, keys and a "who is asking?" select. Families B and C are built for a signed-in parent. They know it is Sam, so they ask "Writing as Sam", greet by name and close loops ("Vera will ask how it went"). B and C differ only in skin.

### 5. If the family could have only one

**X18.** The first thing I would change is the phone calendar and the phone Settings rows:
- Give each calendar day at least a truncated label, or switch the phone to the agenda list that X09 and X10 use.
- Keep each chevron on the row's own line, not dropped underneath.

Then I would raise the contrast of the small grey-on-cream secondary text.

### 6. What to take from the others into X18

- **X09, Home, the dial-and-wire.** A "Next in 1 day" dial whose wire drops into the Coming-up list, redrawn in pine and mustard. It is the clearest image of "what's next" in the set.
- **X11, Home, the illustrated Next plan card** (the drawn roller skate) and **the compass-bearing chips** (▼ S, ◀ W) next to drive times.
- **X12, Plans, the ":D / :| / :(" feedback buttons.** X18's "Rate it" could open exactly this.
- **X17, Home, the "On this page" index with counts.** It is a good one-glance summary for a parent on a laptop.
- **X16, Ideas, the big right-aligned drive-time numerals** in the list view, and its **4-week axis** for upcoming plans.
- **X03, footer, the colour legend** ("lit now or live · amber needs you"). X18 already does this for Settings, and should do it once for the whole app.
- **X10, Chat, "Who's writing?" chips with each person's colour,** applied to bubbles too, so Maya's and Theo's messages are recognisable at a glance.

### 7. What most designs share

**Good:**
- Amber or red means "late or needs you" almost everywhere, and lateness is said in human terms ("was due 6 days ago").
- Nearly all designs keep the honest note "Google Calendar is not connected, so these are the plans the bot made."
- Most show the next plan prominently and say "tomorrow" rather than only a date.

**Bad:**
- **Empty kids' wishes.** The empty wishes ("Maya 0 open") get prime space in about half the designs. Only X06, X10, X11, X12 and X18 turn the emptiness into an instruction.
- **The radar.** The radar is reused as decoration on three or four pages in most of Family A, and only X20 (and in a different form X09 and X16) makes it encode something.
- **Phone calendars.** Month grids on the phone almost always lose the event text: bars only, or "Pho Or…".
- **Small grey text.** Small grey monospace metadata at about 11 px is common across the dark designs.
- **The "who is asking?" select.** Every Family A design keeps it as the identity model. On a family's shared phone that is defensible, but Families B and C show it is avoidable.
- **The phone.** It is the weak point across the set: truncation, overflow or awkward wraps appear in X01, X05, X07, X11, X13, X17, X18, X19 and X20.

### 8. How sure am I?

I am fairly sure of the order of the groups: C and B above A, and the broken designs at the bottom. Within groups, a half point either way is honest uncertainty. I judged from screenshots, so I could not see hover or focus states, motion, or real text rendering.

What would move in a real browser:
- **Glow and scanline designs could go either way.** X07, X13, X14 and X17 depend on glow, scanlines and animated sweeps. Live, these could look better (motion helps the radar and needle) or more tiring. I would revisit them by ±1.
- **X19 and X05 could be loading failures.** If their unstyled Plans and Status are a stylesheet that failed to load in the capture rather than the design, they would rise. X19 could reach about 5.
- **X20 could rise half a point.** The nearly invisible checkboxes on Home might be a capture artefact. If they show clearly on screen, it rises by half a point.
- **X10 and X18 could fall.** Their faint grey-on-cream text might prove too light on a real screen, and if so they drop by about half a point. The ranking between them would not change.
- **X11 depends on its phone break.** If the one-word-per-line Settings row happens only at the exact screenshot width, X11 rises to about 8.
