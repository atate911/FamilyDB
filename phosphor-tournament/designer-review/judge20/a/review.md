# Review: twenty designs for the FamilyDB / Vera web app

I read both overviews for every design and the full desktop Home for all twenty. I also opened full pages wherever the overview hinted at a problem or a standout. These included X05 Plans (desktop), X02 Settings (phone), X13 Ideas (phone), X20 Status (phone), X18 Ideas (desktop) and X19 Home (phone). Marks are out of 10 and judge graphic and interface design together.

---

## Part 1: one review per design

### X01
**What it is**
A near-black dashboard in phosphor green, with one retro CRT "radar" screen added on Home, Ideas and Status.

**What works**
- The Vera composer leads the page, with a glowing green border and three prompt chips. It is clearly the main thing to do.
- Coming up uses neat date tiles (OCT / 9 / Fri) with "in 6 days" pills.
- Overdue to-dos show "was due 6 days ago" in amber.
- Settings is a calm 3×3 grid of cards, and "Sign-in and security" gets an amber outline and a "Needs a look" tag.
- The Plans month grid has an orange circle on today (3).

**What doesn't**
- Apart from the CRT boxes, this is a generic dark admin template.
- On Home the right column ends after "Lately added", leaving a large dead area beside the To do list.
- "The kids' wishes" is a full-width card near the top of Home that only says "Maya 0 open / Theo 0 open".
- Ideas and To do open with heavy boxed filter forms (Search / Kind / Status / For + a green Filter button) before any content.
- On Status, every item is a dot plus small grey text in three dense columns.

**What's beautiful**
Home, the "NEXT UP" CRT. The bezel, scanlines, mono "Oaks Park roller rink" in green and the sweeping radar make a lovely object.

**What's ugly**
Home, the empty bottom-right quarter beside the To do card, plus the near-empty Kids' wishes card sitting above the useful content.

**Mark**
5/10. One charming widget sits on top of an ordinary layout with dead space and a lot of form furniture.

### X02
**What it is**
A warm cream "paper" app with a deep teal accent, a bold serif display face and a left sidebar. It reads like a well-made family organiser.

**What works**
- "Good morning, Sam." sits over one summary sentence that underlines "roller rink" and puts "Three to-dos are overdue" in red. The page tells you what matters before you scroll.
- The teal Next plan card has a "SUN 4 OCT" tile, "See the plan / Move it" buttons and a little roller-skate drawing with yellow wheels.
- Coming up rows carry drive times and direction pills (NNE, S, W).
- In Chat, Vera's replies end with linked chips and a confirmation card ("Silver Falls hike · Added to Plans").
- Status opens with "Vera is working." in a green band, a big serif "$0.00" and a 30-day bar chart.
- Settings has a "First-time setup" rail and "Who can sign in".

**What doesn't**
- The phone Settings page is broken. "Sign-in and security", "Messages" and "Connections" squeeze their text into a column one word wide, so the rows become tall ladders of single words (confirmed on the full phone page).
- On phone, calendar chips shrink to "Sil… Fa…".
- On phone, the To do add form stacks its fields unevenly.

**What's beautiful**
Home, the teal "NEXT PLAN · TOMORROW AT 13:00" card with the cream date tile and the roller-skate illustration.

**What's ugly**
Phone Settings, the "Sign-in and security" row with one word per line beside a stranded "Needs a look" tag.

**Mark**
7.5/10. The desktop is one of the best in the set, held back by a plainly broken phone Settings page.

### X03
**What it is**
X01 tightened up, with a big "Tell Vera what to plan." headline, a stat strip and a three-column Home.

**What works**
- A CRT-style stat strip (TODAY Saturday 3 October | PLANS 4 | TO DO 4 | LATE 3 | IDEAS 12) gives the whole state in one line.
- Setup is cut to one line: "Next: Choose your own password" plus step circles 2 and 3.
- Coming up, To do and Lately added sit side by side and the page ends cleanly.
- On To do, a CRT counter panel ("With a deadline 3 / No date 1 / Reminders set 0") is a nice touch.

**What doesn't**
- Inner pages are the same as X01, filter boxes included.
- The To do table shows raw machine timestamps ("2026-09-27 19:56").
- Kids' wishes is still a full-width card that only says "0 open".
- On phone To do, the title "Book the campsite at Silver Falls" runs under the Edit button.

**What's beautiful**
Home, the mono green stat strip under the hero. It is compact, readable and in character.

**What's ugly**
Phone To do, the task title colliding with the Edit button.

**Mark**
6/10. It improves X01's Home, but the rest is unchanged and it has a phone collision.

### X04
**What it is**
The CRT family made flatter and more editorial. Coming up and To do become timelines with a "NOW" marker.

**What works**
- The to-do timeline puts overdue items above a green "NOW" line and undated ones below. It is a genuinely clear idea.
- The chips say where they land ("What should we do today? → Plans").
- The radar has a caption: "The nearer the day, the nearer the middle."
- Status adds mono "Working / Off" words and a cost table.

**What doesn't**
- Native, unstyled browser selects on "who is asking?" and To do's "Show" clash with the styled UI.
- Chat sets Vera's replies in monospace, which reads like a log rather than a person.
- Lately added becomes a plain table.
- The kids' wishes column is half empty.

**What's beautiful**
Home, the Coming up timeline: green "NOW Saturday 3 October" node, big day numerals and right-aligned "tomorrow / in 6 days".

**What's ugly**
Home, the native grey "who is asking?" select box inside the dark composer.

**Mark**
6.5/10. The timeline is a good idea, but the finish (native controls, mono chat) is unpolished.

### X05
**What it is**
The CRT family as a Teletext terminal: "P100 FamilyDB · Home" page codes, and the whole composer inside one CRT monitor.

**What works**
- Committing the composer and Next up to a single monitor bezel gives Home a strong left anchor.
- A footer legend explains the colours ("Green: the screen, and what to do · Amber: late, or needs a look").
- The To do CRT draws hash-mark bar charts ("###########…").
- Settings is a tidy single list with an amber bar on Sign-in.

**What doesn't**
- The Plans page is broken (confirmed full size). The month grid is an unstyled table with columns of uneven width: MON is narrow, TUE wide, WED narrow. There is no today marker.
- Under the grid, the plans list is unpadded bold/underlined text flush against its box.
- The List/Month toggle icons touch their labels ("☰List").
- On phone, calendar event names break one word per line.
- Status loses its status dots and becomes plain stacked text.
- On Home, the CRT column ends half way down, leaving the left side empty.

**What's beautiful**
Home, the CRT monitor holding "What's on your mind?" in mono with "> What should we do today?" prompts and "SEND" in caps. It is the most immersive composer in the set.

**What's ugly**
Plans (desktop), the uneven, unstyled calendar table above the bare unpadded list.

**Mark**
3/10. A strong concept, but the calendar (a core page) is broken on desktop and phone.

**What doesn't**

**What's beautiful**
Home, the green wire from the bottom of the CRT into the "NOW" node of Coming up.

**What's ugly**
Home, the duplicated plan list (CRT, then timeline) next to the empty right column.

**Mark**
6/10. It has typographic punch, but the Home duplicates content and leaves a hole.

### X07
**What it is**
The CRT family where every retro screen shows real data: a 4-week timeline, a drive-time line, a cost gauge.

**What works**

**What doesn't**

**What's beautiful**

**What's ugly**
Ideas, the stacked number tower at 15 minutes on the drive-time line.

### X08
**What it is**
A full phosphor terminal: green-tinted background with scanlines, a pixel-mono display face, a left sidebar and an amber setup panel.

**What works**
- The atmosphere is total and consistent.
- On Home, Vera speaks first inside the composer ("Tomorrow is the roller rink at 13:00…").
- The setup panel has a progress ring "3" and three step tiles.
- The Next plan card has a line-drawn roller skate.
- Wish lists show "empty_ Nothing wished for yet" in dashed boxes.
- Plans has a "HOW DID IT GO?" rail with :D :| :( buttons.

**What doesn't**
- Everything is green on green: surfaces, text, borders and glow. Secondary text is low contrast.
- Small mono text (the setup step descriptions, ideas meta) is hard to read.
- Calendar events are solid bright green blocks that shout.
- On phone, the calendar shows bars with no names.

**What's beautiful**
Plans, the "HOW DID IT GO?" cards with the :D / :| / :( emoticon buttons.

**What's ugly**
Home, the amber "3 STEPS LEFT" panel. Its tiny mono descriptions ("so each person signs in as themselves") sit on a busy amber ground.

**Mark**
6.5/10. It is memorable and has good ideas, but it is tiring to read and short on contrast.

### X09

**What's beautiful**
Ideas, the "Around home" polar plot with dots by direction and drive time, rings and a legend.

**What's ugly**
Phone Plans, the calendar with nameless green bars and the clipped "TODA" label.

**Mark**
7/10. It is a well-organised terminal with one excellent chart. The mono-heavy text costs readability.

Nothing goes below 3, because every design is complete and usable on desktop. Marks of 1 or 2 would mean the app doesn't work, and none of these is that bad.

### 2. Top three
- **X18.** It is the one design where the retro screen does real work. The dial shows when the next plan is and how the month is spaced, and it is physically wired into the list it summarises. Everything else is quiet: one accent, consistent NOW markers, empty states cut to one line. It also holds together on phone.
- **X19.** It best fits a family of four. It is warm, readable and friendly (the tomato mascot), the composer comes first, and the empty states are kind. It has the best phone build of the twenty, with Plans becoming an agenda instead of a squashed grid.
- **X14.** It does almost everything X19 does, with the best Settings page in the set: a rose banner with one action, plus a "Reading this list" legend that explains the tags. It is held back only by small phone faults (orphaned chevrons, clipped chips).

### 3. Bottom three
- **X05.** The Plans calendar is an unstyled table with uneven columns, no today marker and an unpadded list underneath, and it breaks on phone too. A broken core page outweighs a lovely composer.
- **X20.** Its Home is beautiful, but Status is unstyled on desktop and overflows on phone, and the other pages are thin. It looks unfinished.
- **X13.** It has the most interesting desktop of the three, but the phone layout overflows its right edge on Ideas, Plans, To do and Status. For a family on phones, that matters.

### 4. How many different ideas are there really?
There are really two underlying ideas, worn in three skins.

- **Family A, "warm paper organiser" (X02, X14, X19).** Cream background, serif headings, one strong accent (teal or tomato), left sidebar, a "Good morning, Sam." greeting with a summary sentence, a Next plan card with a calendar tile, a setup checklist in the right rail, and two-pane chat. They differ mainly in accent colour and finish.
- **Family B, "CRT radar dashboard" (X01, X03, X04, X05, X06, X07, X10, X11, X12, X13, X15, X17, X18, X20): fourteen designs.** Near-black ground, phosphor green, top nav, a composer hero beside a CRT "NEXT UP" screen (usually a radar), the same Settings and Status structures, and the same footer. Within it:
  - *Base* (X01, X03, X10, X12, X20) varies the Home grid and stat strip.
  - *Timeline/NOW* (X04, X05, X06, X07, X18) turns lists into timelines with a NOW marker and starts making the CRT show data.
  - *Reskins* (X11 serif slate, X13 control panel, X15 sci-fi HUD, X17 magazine poster) change the type and decoration more than the structure.
- **Family C, "green terminal with sidebar" (X08, X09, X16).** This is Family A's structure (sidebar, greeting, setup panel, "How did it go?") dressed in Family B's phosphor skin, with pixel-mono headings and a green-tinted ground.

What separates the families is light versus dark, and sidebar-plus-greeting versus top-nav-plus-CRT hero. Inside Family B, the differences are mostly typography and how literally the CRT is used.

### 5. If the family could have only one
**X18.** The first thing I'd change is the "From: who is asking?" dropdown in the composer. Four people share this app on shared laptops, and a dropdown that defaults to nobody is the weakest control on the most important part of the page. I would replace it with the avatar pills X14 and X19 use ("Writing as · Sam · Alex · Maya · Theo"), remembered per device. Second, I'd move the phone dial's wire so it doesn't strike through the "Coming up" heading.

### 7. What most of them share
- **Good:** overdue items are flagged consistently (amber or red, "was due 6 days ago"). Most designs explain plainly that "Google Calendar is not connected, so these are the plans the bot made." The composer is the first thing on Home in nearly all twenty.
- **Bad:**
  - Most designs give the empty kids' wishes ("0 open") a full section or card, often high on the page. Only X18 and X20 cut them down to one line.
  - Machine details leak into a family app: "gpt-6-luna", raw timestamps like "2026-09-27 19:56", "end_turn".
  - On phone, calendar chips get truncated mid-word or reduced to bars in most designs, and only a few (X18, X19, X01) switch to an agenda.
  - Chip rows are clipped at the right edge on phone in several designs.
  - Status pages are long walls of small text everywhere.
  - In Family B, fourteen designs share one footer, one Settings grid and one Status layout, so a lot of the "variety" is surface.

### 8. How sure am I?
I'm fairly sure of the ranking at the top and bottom, and less sure of the 5.5–7 band, where differences come down to taste. Seeing them in a real browser would change these:
- **X05, X13, X20.** The breaks I saw on full pages look structural (table sizing, overflow), not screenshot artifacts. I would keep these marks unless a real phone shows the X13 and X20 overflow is a capture-width quirk, in which case X13 could rise to about 6.
- **X02.** The one-word-per-line phone Settings could come from a single flex bug at exactly this width. If it renders properly on real phones, X02 would rise to 8.5.
- **X08, X15, X11.** Scanlines, glows, grid floors and the dithered font may look better or worse live. Moving radar sweeps and a ticking "NOW 06:58" might charm, or might distract and drain batteries. Low-contrast green text usually looks worse on a real screen, so X08 could drop by half a point.
- **X01, X03, X10, X12.** If the radars actually sweep and the cursors blink, these gain a little life. I'd add up to half a point.
- **X04, X12.** Native selects look different on each OS. On macOS they might sit in better.
- **All of them.** I couldn't see hover and focus states, keyboard focus rings, transitions or tap-target feel, which matter a lot for a family on phones. Those could move any mark by about half a point.
